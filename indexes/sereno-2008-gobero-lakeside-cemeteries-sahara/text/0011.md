On the other hand, several pollen and other sporomorphs suggest wet environments. For example, Cypera￾ceae, which includes both aquatic and dune herb species, may result from sedge belts in the vicinity of a lake, and hydrophytes and algal elements thrive in permanent fresh water. The hygrophilous fig tree (Ficus) probably had a scattered distribution near the wettest places of the area, which it may have shared with Capparis, Ziziphus, Salvadora and Cadaba. Sudanian and tropical elements, such as Celtis, Ficus, Salvadora persica, Hyphaene thebaica, could have been part of riverine forests that also included Combretum, Cadaba, Ziziphus, Pterocarpus and Capparis. None of these taxa, however, occur in very high frequencies among non-arboreal pollen (Figure 10C).
