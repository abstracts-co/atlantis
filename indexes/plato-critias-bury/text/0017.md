# Critias 114d-115b: wealth, orichalcum and metals, timber, elephants, plants and fruits

For because of their headship they had a large supply of imports from abroad, [114e] and the island itself furnished most of the requirements of daily life,—metals, to begin with, both the hard kind and the fusible kind, which are extracted by mining, and also that kind which is now known only by name but was more than a name then, there being mines of it in many places of the island,—I mean orichalcum, [n1] which was the most precious of the metals then known, except gold. It brought forth also in abundance all the timbers that a forest provides for the labors of carpenters; and of animals it produced a sufficiency, both of tame and wild.

Moreover, it contained a very large stock of elephants; for there was an ample food-supply not only for all the other animals which haunt the marshes and lakes and rivers, [115a] or the mountains or the plains, but likewise also for this animal, which of its nature is the largest and most voracious. And in addition to all this, it produced and brought to perfection all those sweet-scented stuffs which the earth produces now, whether made of roots or herbs or trees, or of liquid gums derived from flowers or fruits. The cultivated fruit[n2] also, and the dry, [n3] which serves us for nutriment, and all the other kinds that we use for our meals—the various species of which are comprehended under the name vegetables— [115b] and all the produce of trees which affords liquid and solid food and unguents, [n4] and the fruit of the orchard-trees, so hard to store, which is grown for the sake of amusement and pleasure, [n5] and all the after-dinner fruits [n6] that we serve up as welcome remedies for the sufferer from repletion,—all these that hallowed island, as it lay then beneath the sun, produced in marvellous beauty and endless abundance.
