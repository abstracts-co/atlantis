# Spartel Bank (Majuán Bank): evidence on the ground

Checked 2026-11-11. Data file: `spartel-bank.json`. Centre: the bank's summit, 35.9036 N, 5.9615 W.

## In one paragraph

Spartel Bank is a real drowned island, about 13 km north-north-west of Cape Spartel and just outside the Strait of Gibraltar. At the ice-age low it stood above the sea. Jacques Collina-Girard (2001, 2002, 2009) says it was an island 14 km by 5 km that went under for good about 11,300 years ago, and that this is the root of Plato's story. Marc-André Gutscher (2005) mapped it with new sonar data and tested whether a great earthquake and tsunami could have sunk it. He found that one is plausible in the Gulf of Cádiz. He then wrote that Spartel was "the right place, the right circumstances, but no it's not the right time", and that it held no Bronze Age society. A classicist reviewing Collina-Girard's book concluded he "has not found Plato's Atlantis". No excavation, dive or sampled site exists on the bank in any source I read.

## What was claimed

- **Collina-Girard 2002** (open follow-up to his 2001 paper, doi:10.4000/pm.248). A horst "formait alors une île (14 km de long sur 5 km de large) actuellement engloutie à –56 m". It was 8 to 10 km from the coasts. Sea level at the ice-age low was -130 to -135 m; it reached -55 m by 11,300 years ago (meltwater pulse 1B), which "submerge définitivement l'île du Cap Spartel (-56 m)". He draws six more islets (numbers 2 to 7) and an inner sea 77 km long. He says Plato's size ("larger than Libya and Asia") is the one point that does not fit, and puts it down to exaggeration over 9,000 years of retelling.
- **The 2009 book** (as reviewed by Nesselrath, BMCR): an island "about 15 km northwest of Cape Spartel", six other islands, meltwater pulses at about 12,200 BC and 9500 BC, and an earthquake and tsunami about 10,000 BC that sank the island by 10 m.
- **Drawn on the globe:** `claimed-island-1`, the island outline from his Figure 1, digitised against the map's graticule.

## What was checked, and where

- **Gutscher 2005**, Geology 33(8), 685-688, doi:10.1130/G21597.1. I read only the abstract. It reports "the detailed morphology of Spartel paleoisland, as determined from recently acquired high-resolution bathymetric data", discusses habitation about 11.6 ka, and says great earthquakes (M 8 to 9) hit the Gulf of Cádiz every 1.5 to 2 thousand years "according to the sedimentary record". It cites "an unusually thick turbidite dated as ca. 12 ka" as a possible match. A 2005 Spektrum news piece adds: land area at the ice-age maximum "etwas mehr als 25 Quadratkilometer", top of the bank at most 50 m down, the island would need to have stood at least 40 m higher to be habitable, and the 12 ka layer (called H8) is about five times larger than the 1755 layer.
- **The survey.** Gutscher and others 2009 (Tectonophysics, open HAL copy) list cruise **TV-GIB** among Gulf of Cádiz surveys "all using the R/V Suroit EM300 system". A search snippet of the Geology figure caption says July 2003 and a 5 m grid. I found **no open outline or track** for TV-GIB (EMODnet, Sextant/Ifremer catalogue, French fleet site), so no survey box is drawn.
- **Nearby open survey:** a SHOM 2009 box (NOMADS 09, R/V Laplace) offshore Cape Spartel, drawn as context. It does not cover the bank.

## What was found

- **The summit.** In the GMRT 50 m grid and the EMODnet 100 m mean grid the shallowest point of the bank is about **72 m** down, at 35.9036 N, 5.9615 W. The two grids agree within 150 m. This is my own readout of those grids, not a published figure.
- **The bank is smaller than drawn.** In the GMRT grid the bank connected to the summit and shallower than 130 m is about 13.8 sq km, 5.9 km by 3.6 km (at -100 m: 6.0 sq km; at -120 m: 11.5 sq km). Collina-Girard's outline is 37 sq km. Spektrum reports "more than 25 sq km" from Gutscher's map. The globe should draw the shore from the depth grid at the chosen sea level.
- **Gutscher's later view** (his e-mail reposted on an About.com blog; web archive copy): "The bottom line is, Spartel was not inhabited by an advanced bronze age culture." If anyone lived there it was "simple fishermen", or Plato's chronology is wrong.

## Where sources disagree

- **Summit depth.** Collina-Girard 2002 says 56 m. The BMCR review says about 55 m. Spektrum 2005 says up to 50 m. GMRT and EMODnet grids say about 72 m. The grids are compilations, and a narrow pinnacle could be shallower than a 50 to 100 m cell, but the 16 m gap is large. The old pin (Wikidata, 35.91671 N, 5.96683 W) sits 1.5 km north-north-west of the summit, at about 86 m in GMRT. The 220 m the owner saw on the globe was not reproduced here.
- **Island size.** Collina-Girard: 14 km by 5 km. Spektrum on Gutscher: over 25 sq km. Today's grids: 13.8 sq km at -130 m. A figure of under 500 m across at the time of drowning, from a forum repost of a 2005 Nature news piece, is not used: the original was not fetched.
- **Date and sea level inside Collina-Girard's own account.** The 2002 article has meltwater pulse 1B at 11,300 years ago and -55 m. Nesselrath notes the book says the sea reached -55 m about 9500 BC but also that an earthquake about 10,000 BC sank the island 10 m, which "would mean that already then water levels had risen to about -45 m". Gutscher uses 11.6 ka, from Plato. At -55 m the bank's summit (-56 m in his figures, -72 m in the grids) is already under water.
- **Which meltwater pulse.** Our earlier draft data says pulse 1A around 9400 BCE. The 2002 article puts pulse 1A at about 14,000 years ago and pulse 1B at 11,300. Use the article.
- **Distance from Cape Spartel.** BMCR says about 15 km north-west. From the two positions the summit is 13.0 km away, about 345 degrees (north-north-west) from the cape.

## Held against Plato

Plato gives no number for the island's size or for the distance from the Pillars. At its largest the bank is 14 km by 5 km (claimed) or about 6 km by 3.6 km (grids); Plato's central island alone is 5 stades, about 925 m, across. Plato's 9,000 years before Solon is about 11,600 years before present, which fits Gutscher's date and sits close to Collina-Girard's 11,300. The drowning happens at slow sea-level rise, not in one day and night, unless an earthquake sank the bank by about 10 m, which is Gutscher's and Collina-Girard's argument, not a measurement.

## What I left out

- Collina-Girard's islets 2 to 7: I could not match 5, 6 and 7 to any rise in the grids (130 to 200 m of water there). The figure is schematic.
- The Gulf of Cádiz sources of 1755, the Gràcia 2010 turbidite cores and IODP Expedition 339 sites. They lie 150 to 400 km west and are outside the 40 km scene. Handed off.
- The EMODnet 29 m CSIC-ICM grid (2011 cruises GEOMARGEN-I and TOPOMED Leg 2). I downloaded it but could not read it with the tools available. Whether it covers the bank was not checked.
