# Doñana marshes (Marisma de Hinojos), Spain: evidence on the ground

Checked 2026-11-11. Data: `donana.json` (11 features, 9 measurements, 7 story steps). Centre: Kühne's printed position for his claimed rectangle, 36°57'25" N, 6°22'58" W. Radius 25 km.

## What was claimed

- **Werner Wickboldt** spotted two rectangles and parts of concentric rings in satellite photos of the Marisma de Hinojos. **Rainer Kühne** published them in *Antiquity* Project Gallery 78(300), 2004, "A location for 'Atlantis'?". He reads the rectangles as the temples of Poseidon and of Cleito and Poseidon, and the rings as Plato's rings. He links Atlantis to Tartessos and to the Sea Peoples (about 1200 BCE). The note is a short project report, not a full article.
- What Kühne prints: a rectangle 230 m long and 140 m wide at 36°57'25" N, 6°22'58" W (± 6" and 8"); a "quadratic" structure 280 m by 240 m whose centre is 500 m south-west of the rectangle. He gives no bearings and no ring sizes. BBC (2004) adds that the "island" and rings are "slightly larger" than Plato's, and that a stadion 20% longer than usual would make the rectangle match the temple.
- Older: Schulten, Bonsor and Jessen searched for Tartessos at Cerro del Trigo in the 1920s. Schulten, though apparently not Bonsor, took Tartessos to be Atlantis's core.
- Freund / National Geographic, 2011: radar, resistivity, magnetometers and spectrometers; "a communal oven and evidence of canal systems"; a flooded 4,000-year-old community. Merlin Burrows, 2018: "large circles", a temple, a sea wall, "concrete" said to be 10,000 to 12,000 years old.

## What was checked, and where

- **Hinojos Project** (CSIC with Universities of Huelva, Granada, Barcelona; Rodríguez-Ramírez, Villarías-Robles, Celestino-Pérez, López-Sáez and others), from 2005. It began as a test of the Wickboldt-Kühne idea. Its own account: it compared the satellite images with other satellites and aerial photos ("Some of them, as it turned out, could be seen; while others could not"), surveyed the ground, and drilled cores to 12 m.
- **Cores.** S1 (12 m, July 2016): printed coordinates, 0.45 km from Kühne's rectangle. S7, S9 (12 m) and S11 (18 m), drilled 2006 to 2009: no coordinates printed; I placed them from the paper's Figure 1.
- **Cerro del Trigo** (1920s): the dig found Roman remains and a Roman cemetery, and "only sand and the water table" below.

## What was found

- Under the marsh at S1: grey estuary mud from about 5,000 years ago (Copper Age) up to the present, 1.5 m above sea level now. Four sandy layers with sea shells at about 4560 to 4450, 4200 to 4000, 3500 to 3435 and 3185 to 3140 cal BP. The team calls them extreme wave events (storm surges or tsunamis) and does not choose.
- In S7, S9 and S11 the same sea-wave event (A, about 4000 cal BP, about 2000 BCE) left a sand and shell layer 40 to 70 cm thick, traced about 10 km inland. Earlier Neolithic and Copper Age finds lie among the dunes to the north-west.
- Pollen at S1 shows crops and herding in the Copper Age, and no sign of them through the Early Bronze Age and the early Late Bronze Age (to about 3140 cal BP). The team's reading: people lived on this ground before the sea came in about 2000 BCE.
- The team's reply on the shapes (Rodríguez-Ramírez and Villarías-Robles, conference paper): "the geometric features on the ground, man-made though most certainly are, cannot be old enough as to date back to Tartessian or earlier times"; material of Tartessian age is "several meters down". That says the surface shapes are not Plato's age. It does not say what made them. The team did not publish a test of the specific rectangles that I could find.
- 96 oval ponds, up to 250 m long, were dug in 2004 to 2005 on the Caracoles estate (PLoS ONE). A blog says Burrows's "circles" are these ponds.

## Measurements to hold against Plato

| Thing | Here | Plato |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle length | 230 m (Kühne) | temple 1 stadion, 185 m |
| Rectangle width | 140 m | 3 plethra, about 92 m |
| Distance to the sea | 8.0 km to today's coast (my OSM calculation) | canal 50 stadia, about 9.25 km |
| Ground height | marsh averages 1.35 m above sea level | "rose sheer out of the sea to a great height" |
| Age of the marsh mud | base about 5,000 years old at 12 m | 9,000 years before Solon |
| Wave event | about 4000 cal BP | one day and night, about 9600 BCE |

The marsh was a brackish lagoon and estuary when the cores began (about 5300 cal BP), not dry land at the coast. I did not draw the sea distance on a Bronze Age shore.

## Position quality

By `position.how`: stated-coordinates 3 (Kühne's rectangle, core S1, Caracoles estate); digitised-from-figure 3 (S7, S9, S11); from-dataset 4 (Cerro del Trigo, three towns); from-description 1 (the square). By `side`: claimed 2, checked 4, found 2, context 3.

- Digitised cores: I fitted the coast in the paper's Figure 1 to the OpenStreetMap coastline by translation only (170 points, rms 177 m), with the scale from the figure's 10 km bar. The result is sensitive to scale: S11 moves 2.4 km north to south across 34 to 46 px per km, so I give 700 to 1000 m. I did not use the figure's printed tick labels.
- Kühne's text on the Cambridge page prints "36E°57'25''" and "6E°22'58''". The E is a typesetting artefact; the same note prints Spartel the same way. Read as 36° and 6°, the point lies in the Hinojos marsh beside core S1, which supports the reading.

## Where sources disagree

- S7 to S11: the paper says 3.5 km; my fit gives 3.2 km.
- Number of wave events: Rodríguez-Ramírez and others (2015) name three (A about 4000, B about 3550, C about 3150 cal BP). López-Sáez and others (2018) find four in S1, adding one at 4560 to 4450 cal BP.
- Tsunami or storm: the CSIC press release (2018) says the marine episode "coincided with a tsunami"; the papers say extreme wave events and keep both options.
- Hinojos Project dates: "2005 to 2013" (title of a 2016 chapter cited by López-Sáez 2018) against "2005 on" and a 2018 paper in the CSIC release.
- Freund: Popular Science reports his team's claim; Colavito's blog (quoting the Telegraph) gives early dates of 5,000 to 2,400 years and quotes Villarías-Robles saying Freund "was a newcomer to our project"; Atlantipedia says Freund spent under a week on site. Neither blog is a primary source.
- Kühne puts the flood at 800 to 500 BCE (BBC) and the Sea Peoples war at about 1200 BCE; Plato's date is about 9600 BCE.

## Left out

- Shallow cores CV1 to CV3, M1 to M3, ML, PN, VA, VT, MT1 to MT4 (Figure 1 of the 2015 paper); chenier ridges and the spits; the Doñana park boundary; Hinojos village (37 km north).
- Wickboldt's rings: no centre or radius. Freund's scan lines. Burrows's structures. Lacus Ligustinus shoreline. See `not_located`.
