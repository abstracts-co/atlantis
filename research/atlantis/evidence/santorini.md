# Santorini (Thera) and its Bronze Age eruption: evidence on the ground

Checked 2026-11-11. Data file: `santorini.json` (38 features, 19 measurements, 7 story steps).

## What was claimed

- Marinatos (1939, Antiquity 13) tied the end of the Minoan palaces on Crete to a volcanic eruption. His abstract does not mention Atlantis; later writers made that link. Frost (1913) wrote on the Critias and Minoan Crete.
- Galanopoulos (1960, and with Bacon 1969) and Luce (1969) said the Thera eruption was the real event behind Plato's sunken island. Mavor (WHOI, 1966) searched the caldera for a Minoan city.
- A critic, Finley (NYRB 1969), states the idea in one line: "The explosion, the Atlanticists argue, was 'remembered' in a mixed-up way, until Plato brought it back to consciousness in his myth." A summary of Galanopoulos's factor-of-ten reading of Plato's numbers is on Atlantipedia (secondary). Druitt and others (1999) call the link "plausible".
- On the scene this is one `claimed` point (the caldera centre, position from description, 5 km error). I found no claimed shape that anyone drew with a position, so none is drawn.

## What was checked and found

- **Akrotiri.** Dug from 1967 (Marinatos, then Doumas). The dig is drawn as the protective roof outline from OpenStreetMap. It shows a real town buried by the eruption. It does not test any link to Plato.
- **Dating.** Radiocarbon on an olive branch from the Phira quarry (Friedrich and others 2006) gave 1627 to 1600 BCE (95.4%). A second olive, from Therasia (Pearson and others 2023), leans to the mid 16th century BCE. Both are drawn as points with large position errors (see below).
- **Tsunami.** Palaikastro in eastern Crete (Bruins and others 2008): chaotic layers of ash, building stone and shells, run-up of at least 9 m, ash first and then the wave. It is about 155 km from the caldera, so the scene needs a wide view or an inset.
- **Sea floor.** Nomikou and others (2016): the caldera was not open to the sea in the main phase of the eruption. The sea broke in through the north-west strait afterwards and refilled the caldera in under two days. Drawn: the study box, two seismic lines, the headwall and both channel edges.
- **Drilling.** IODP Expedition 398 (Dec 2022 to Feb 2023): four holes in the two caldera basins (U1594 to U1597), two on the Kolumbo flank (U1590, U1593) and two in the Christiana Basin (U1591, U1598). All have stated coordinates.
- **The island before the eruption.** Yes, there was a ring of water with an island in it, but only in some models, and it was a lagoon, not Plato's ringed city. Before 1600 BCE the caldera was a shallow lagoon left by the 22,000-year-old Cape Riva eruption, with a central lava island ("Pre-Kameni") that the eruption destroyed. Karatson and others (2018): volume 2.2 to 2.5 km3, started growing at 20.2 ± 1.0 ka. I traced three published model outlines from the inset of their Fig. 1 (Druitt and Francaviglia 1992; Druitt 2014; Athanassas and others 2016). The islands are 3.3 to 3.5 km across in the two newer models and 4.6 km in the 1992 one, against 925 m for Plato's central island. The Kameni islands did not exist then.

Checks I ran:

- The Nomikou map axes were checked by laying the OpenStreetMap coast over the figure. The two agree to a few pixels.
- The Karatson map was checked the same way and agrees to about 150 m.
- The inset outlines were fitted to today's coast by overlap of the grey island shape (0.78 to 0.80). They are small schematic drawings in a published figure. They are models, not surveys, and are marked `confidence: low`.

Positions that are weak:

- Phira olive: the paper says only "at Phira Quarry". The point is the nearest OpenStreetMap quarry (450 m from the dot on their schematic map; error 900 m).
- Therasia olive: text says the southern tip, the figure dot sits mid-island (1.6 km apart). I used the tip (error 1.5 km).
- Palaikastro: the polygon is the mapped town. The cliff sections were a few hundred metres away.

## Measurements held against Plato

- **Date.** Radiocarbon 1627 to 1600 BCE (Friedrich 2006), "late 17th century" (Manning 2006), 1593 to 1540 BCE for 72% (Pearson 2023). Plato: about 9600 BCE. Gap about 8,000 years (our subtraction).
- **Caldera.** 10 × 7 km (Nomikou). Plato's whole city is 27 stades, about 5 km at 185 m a stade.
- **Central island.** 3.3 to 3.5 km if round, against 5 stades (about 925 m).
- **Land area.** About 85 km2 today (our sum of the OpenStreetMap polygons). Plato: "larger than Libya and Asia put together".
- **Distance.** About 2,760 km to the Strait of Gibraltar centre. Plato puts the island outside that strait.
- **Tsunami.** At least 9 m run-up at Palaikastro. A layer 10 to 12 m above sea level on Santorini is cited by Nomikou but has no place.
- **Flooding.** The caldera refilled in under 2 days (Plato: one day and night).
- **Volume.** See the disagreements below.

## Where sources disagree

- **Eruption date.**
  - Friedrich and others 2006: 1627–1600 BCE (95.4%).
  - Manning and others 2006: late 17th century BCE.
  - Pearson and others 2018: radiocarbon says late 17th to early 16th century BCE, archaeology says mid 16th to early 15th century. A new calibration curve moves radiocarbon toward the 16th.
  - Pearson and others 2023: 1593–1540 BCE (72.3%) or 1621–1598 BCE (23.2%) for the same branch.
  - Friedrich says archaeologists had put it in the mid to late 16th century.
  - Finley 1969: 1525–1500 BCE, citing the 1968 dig report.
  - WHOI 2021 (about Mavor's work): 1,400 BCE.
  - Pearson 2023 says the olive rings may not be yearly, so the branch dating is argued.
  - Wikipedia lists more studies. I did not rely on them.
- **Volume.** Karatson 2018 gives 117 to 129 km3 bulk and 78 to 86 km3 DRE, and says this "may be regarded as a maximum value". Nomikou 2016 gives 30 to 80 km3 DRE. Wikipedia quotes 28 to 41 km3 DRE from later cruises (not checked).
- **Pre-eruption shape.** Karatson 2018 shows six published models. Heiken and McCoy (1984) put a caldera in the south; Druitt and others (1999) showed it had long been filled in. Friedrich 1988 and Druitt and Francaviglia 1992 have a lagoon with an island; Druitt 2014 and Athanassas 2016 put it in the north basin. The island volume was thought to be 3 to 5 km3 and is now 2.2 to 2.5.
- **Kolumbo.** Wikipedia gives 36.5167 N 25.4917 E. The crater on Nomikou Fig. 1a is at about 36.527 N 25.4845 E, 1.1 km off. IODP says U1590 and U1593 are 5 and 8 km north-west of the crater, which fits the figure.
- **Tsunami size.** Bruins and Nomikou give at least 9 m run-up at Palaikastro, and about +35 m at the source from a model. Wikipedia says 35 to 150 m, which I did not check.

## Left out

- IODP sites U1592, U1599, U1600 (Anafi Basin, 25 to 35 km east, coordinates known) and U1589 (no coordinates in the summary).
- Roughly 20 of the 30 outcrop dots in Karatson Fig. 1, merged or skipped (8 shown).
- The Pichler 1972, Friedrich 1988 and Heiken and McCoy 1984 outlines (poor fit or open line).
- The old lagoon's land rim and the Therasia–Aspronisi gap in the models.
- Quarries other than the Phira one, and the Minoan ash exposures near Athinios (no source read that places them).

Most classicists read Atlantis as Plato's invention. Nothing here shows the Thera eruption is the source of his story.
