# Richat Structure (Eye of the Sahara), Mauritania: evidence on the ground

Checked 2026-11-11. Data file: `richat.json`. Hypothesis id `richat`.

## What was claimed

- **2008 to 2011, before the video.** Atlantipedia (a sceptical reference site, second source) records: Charles Giuliani's self-published book (2008) names the Richat as Atlantis; George Sarantitis put the idea forward in 2008 (Greek book 2010); George S. Alexander and Natalis Rosen visited in late 2008 and made the film *Visiting Atlantis* (site 2010, listed 2011). Sarantitis's own FAQ (archived 2022): "Morphologically, Richat matches Plato's description of Atlantis in orientation, shape and size and in details." None of these publish coordinates for a drawn outline.
- **2018, Bright Insight (Jimmy Corsetti).** Seen only through critics. Ancient Origins lists: rings match Plato's; "Atlantis was supposedly 127 stadia, or 23 kilometers (14 miles) across, which matches a portion of the Richat" (measured in Google Earth); "mountains to the north, through which rivers flowed down into a wide valley"; water draining out of the structure to the sea. Colavito's post dated 14 Sept 2018 shows the video was already circulating then (hypotheses.json says Nov 2018, the date of Novella's reply).
- The number 127: our sum from `plato.json`, 27 stades for island plus rings (5 + 2 x (1+2+2+3+3)) plus 2 x 50 stades to the outer wall (Critias 117e), is 127. So 127 stades is Plato's whole city inside its outer wall, about 23.5 km at 185 m a stade (20.0 to 26.5 km for stades of 157.5 to 209 m). The rings alone are 27 stades, about 5 km. Colavito says the fit "can only be matched by selectively choosing to ignore the outermost 'ditch'".
- Needs for the claim: the Atlantic must have flooded the Sahara ("He has to imagine that this part of the Sahara was flooded by the Atlantic 12,000 years ago", Novella). The centre stands about 419 m above today's sea and 509 km from the nearest Atlantic cell.

## What was checked, and where

- **Impact idea.** Early workers read the crater shape as a meteorite scar (Cailleux and others, 1964). Dietz, Fudali and Cassidy (1969, GSA Bulletin) argued "not astroblemes"; Wikipedia's reference list has Fudali 1969 (Science 166:228), "Coesite from the Richat Dome, Mauritania: A Misidentification". We did not read Dietz 1969 (not open).
- **Matton, Jébrak and Lee 2005, Geology 33(8):665-668.** Open copy at UQAM. Field mapping and petrology, plus argon dating of the breccia's internal sediment: 98.2 +/- 2.6 Ma. Carbonatite fission tracks (Poupeau and others 1996): 99 +/- 5 Ma.
- **Matton's 2008 thesis (UQAM, open).** Same results; chapter 2 is the 2014 paper in draft form. Ring dykes 20 m and 50 m wide, ~3 km and 7-8 km from the centre; ellipticity 0.87 to 0.88 with the long axis NE-SW; a volcanic crater of ~2.5 km diameter with a salt flat in the south-west.
- **Matton and Jébrak 2014, J. African Earth Sci. 97:109-124.** Paywalled, not read.
- **Abdeina, Jourdan, Chazot, Bertrand and Le Gall 2024, Lithos 482-483:107698 (open, CC BY).** The most useful source here: its Figure 1 is a geological map with a lat/long grid and a scale bar. Six gabbro samples (SR021, 022, 023, 025, 035, 037); two were dated by argon (SR037 outer gabbro, SR025 inner gabbro). Neither gave an accurate age. Modelling suggests 230 to 200 Ma. They argue the two gabbro bodies are sills of the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, not Cretaceous ring dykes; the 99 Ma carbonatites came about 100 million years later.
- **Archaeology.** Wikipedia (citing Sao, Giresse and others 2008, L'Anthropologie 112:1-14, paywalled): "exceptional accumulations of Acheulean artifacts" along wadis of the outermost annular depression, mainly north-west of the outer ring between wadis Akerdil and Bamouéré; pre-Acheulean tools; sparse Neolithic points; Middle Stone Age Aterian tools. Artefacts are "generally absent in its innermost depressions" and the surface wealth is due to deflation. No settlement or built structure is reported (Colavito, Novella, Alexander and Rosen's own failure to show "a single piece of physical evidence" per Atlantipedia).

## What was found, mapped

- **Rock ridges, not water.** Strata dip outward at 10 to 20 degrees; erosion made "three nested rings" of cuestas (Matton 2005). Traced from the 30 m Copernicus elevation model, the crest lines are about **6, 13 and 20 km across** (mean), each slightly elliptical (minor/major about 0.86 for ring 3); an outer rim ridge lies **21.5 to 24.2 km** from the centre on its east and south side. The dome is "at least 40 km" across.
- Centre height 419 m (30 m model; Wikidata 423 m). Ridge crests 387 to 486 m.
- **Core.** Breccia about 3 km across and at least 40 m thick; hot-water karst collapse; sediment 98.2 Ma. Carbonatite dykes (more than 60, mostly in the southern half), a buried kimberlite body in the north (~99 Ma, unpublished company age), two rhyolite maar craters.
- **Distance to the sea.** Nearest Atlantic cell 509 km (559 km due west) today. About 125 m lower sea (last ice age low, USGS via Wikipedia): 605 km (636 km due west). The shelf is wide there, so the low sea moves the coast only 50 to 90 km.
- **Ages.** Breccia and carbonatites about 99 Ma; gabbros about 230 to 200 Ma; Plato's date is 9,000 years before Solon.

## Position quality

33 features: by side, 1 claimed, 6 context, 20 found, 6 checked; by `position.how`, **11 from-dataset, 22 digitised-from-figure**, 0 stated-coordinates, 0 from-description.

- **from-dataset (11).** The four ring ridges and the outer rim arc (error about 250-300 m) are my own trace of the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM (a ridge filter plus a connected-crest search per degree of azimuth). That is a measurement of a natural shape, not something a source drew; the claim "ring 1/2/3 = Matton's three rings" is my reading. The claimed centre (the structure centre) and six OpenStreetMap places (names from the US GNS gazetteer) make up the rest. The wadi nodes are at the wadi mouths, not at dig sites.
- **digitised-from-figure (22).** Everything from Figure 1 of Abdeina 2024: the two gabbro rings (3 lines), breccia, kimberlite, south-west salt flat, 10 of the longest carbonatite dykes, 6 sample points. Control: the figure's printed graticule (a line every 0.04 degrees both ways; found at x = 242, 548, 855, 1160, 1467, 1772 px and y = 164, 491, 819, 1147, 1475 px) and its 2.5 km scale bar, which agree (73.8 px/km). Honest errors: 300 to 700 m. **Weak point:** the printed corner labels are not on grid lines, so I numbered the grid lines by hand (lines at multiples of 0.04 degrees). Check: the flattest ground in the 30 m DEM, south-west of the centre, lies within 0.2 km of the map's salt flat; the traced rings fit the map's unit boundaries on overlay. Against that, the map's well "Hassi Thiba" plots 2.1 km east of the OSM/GNS position.
- The breccia polygon is low confidence: the map draws the breccia in patches and the envelope covers about 14 km2 (4 km across) against 3 km in the text.
- The map's gabbro ring centres sit 1 km east (inner) and 0.9 km west (outer) of each other and of our DEM centre, so the outer ring is 5.7 to 8.5 km from the site centre. Matton 2005 says the dykes are "almost centered".

## Disagreements between sources

- Gabbro ring width: Matton 2005 and the 2008 thesis say ~50 m (outer) and ~20 m (inner); Abdeina 2024 and Wikipedia say ~70 m and ~30 m.
- Gabbro ring or sill, and age: Matton and Jébrak 2014 read ring dykes and an older order (gabbro first, undated); Abdeina 2024 reads CAMP sills of 230 to 200 Ma, "no robust age". The 2005 and thesis text say no radiometric age exists for the gabbros.
- Carbonatite age: Poupeau 1996 gave 99 +/- 5 Ma and discarded an earlier 85 +/- 5 Ma fission track age on suspected irradiation problems (per Abdeina 2024). The IUGS heritage page lists the carbonatites as "85 ± 5 Ma and 99 ± 5 Ma" (citing Matton and Jébrak 2014), so the discarded age is still in circulation.
- Breccia size: 3 km (Matton 2005; Abdeina 2024) against Wikipedia's "at least 30 kilometres in diameter" in one sentence (treated as an error).
- Size of the structure: "at least 40 km" (Matton), "45 km" (NASA per Novella), 35 km (Atlantipedia, Richter). Our outer rim arc is 21.5 to 24.2 km from the centre, so 43 to 48 km if symmetric.
- Discovery of the Acheulean sites: Wikipedia says "first discovered by Théodore Monod in 1974"; Monod's 1952 expedition recorded "four crateriform or circular irregularities" in the region (Atlantipedia, Wikipedia). Not resolved.
- Peak: OSM tags the peak at 485 m; the model gives 419 m at the centre and 515 m at the highest crest.

## Left out

- Ten of about 40 red carbonatite dykes drawn (the longest). Faults, the analcimolite patches, the second salt flat and the volcanic basin in the north-east were not digitised. The thesis's figure 2A (ring dykes, crater, kimberlite) has no graticule and a low resolution, so the 2024 map was used instead.
- The Adrar plateau escarpment: visible in the DEM but not separable from the ring by filter.
- The Semsiyat dome (about 50 km WSW).
