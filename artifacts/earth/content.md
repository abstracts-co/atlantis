# Atlantis

Walk the city Plato describes, meet people in the hall at its centre, and hunt the real Earth, sea floor included, for the place that fits his words.

Atlantis comes from two dialogues by Plato, the Timaeus and the Critias, written about 360 BCE. Most scholars read it as a story he invented, and no proposed place has archaeological support. Everything here says where it comes from.

## What you can do

- **Enter the city.** Plato's city, built to the measurements in the Critias, stands on the open Atlantic beyond the strait, where his words point and where there is nothing. Walk it: click the ground to go there, drag to look. Water and walls are solid, and the rings are crossed by their bridges.
- **Go into the hall.** The shrine at the centre, which Plato says nobody entered, stands open. Twelve boards on its wall show his claims, how the Earth scores against them, the places people have named, every search since 1679, what is still unknown, and work that is open for anyone. A globe on its table opens the whole Earth.
- **Meet people there.** Signed in on Abstracts, you see who else is in the city, write to the room, and talk by voice with whoever stands near you. Walking needs no account.
- **Watch the story**: Plato's tale in his own words over the globe, with what it names shown as it is named, in under two minutes.
- **Pick up work.** Each open task comes with a prompt to hand to your AI.
- **Hunt.** Every 10 km of land that has gone under since Plato's date is scored on five things he says: it sank, it lay beyond the Pillars, it had a great plain, mountains to the north, and it was an island. The land that matches glows. Point at any spot for its score, claim it, and post the result. Nothing on Earth scores five of five.
- **See the 40 proposed places.** Each pin shows how the proposal stands with scholars and whether anyone has checked on the ground. A place's card shows what is said to fit Plato and what breaks, with the reason and source behind each mark.
- **Explore 8 places as scenes** (Azores Plateau and the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Bimini Road, Sea bed south-east of Cyprus, Doñana marshes, Spain, Helike, Gulf of Corinth, Richat Structure, Santorini and its Bronze Age eruption, Spartel Bank): the research is drawn where it happened. Survey boxes and ship tracks on the sea bed, cores and digs on the ground, the shape that was claimed and what the checks found. Switch between claimed and found, or walk through it step by step. 178 features in all, each with its source and how its position is known.
- **Open the board.** Every place against every detail Plato gives, and every search since 1679 with how it ended. A blank cell is work nobody has done.
- **Test the fit.** Lay Plato's measurements on any place and read the ground: is it above water, is there level ground for the rings, how far is the sea, is there room for the plain.
- **Lower the sea to Plato's date**, about 9600 BCE, or to the low point of the last ice age, and see which land was dry.
- **Stand Plato's city on any place, at true size.** It is built to the measurements in the Critias: a central island five stadia across, three rings of sea and two of land, a canal fifty stadia long, walls faced with brass, tin and orichalcum. A panel lists what comes from the text, passage by passage, and what is our choice.
- **Strip the ground bare** and move the sun, or lay open lidar and sea-floor surveys over it.
- **Check what is already recorded** near any spot, from Wikidata and OpenStreetMap.
- **Share a view** as a link, or post a picture of it.

## What it can't show

Sea-floor depth is 500 m to 2 km a pixel away from a few surveyed strips, and 60 to 500 m at the five places with their own depth grid: it shows banks and seamounts, never a wall. Only research with a known position is drawn; what could not be placed is listed, not guessed. One sea level is used for the whole planet, though real coasts also rose and sank. The rebuilt city is a drawing of a text. Nothing like it has been found.

## The places and the other legends

### Sunken lands

#### Atlantis (legend)

Plato describes a great island power beyond the Pillars of Heracles that attacked ancient Athens and sank into the sea in a single day and night. The story appears only in his dialogues Timaeus and Critias, and no earlier text mentions it. Most classicists read it as a philosophical fiction that Plato invented, and no proposed location has archaeological support. *Set by Plato about 9,000 years before Solon (about 9600 BCE); written about 360 BCE.*

What to look for: Plato describes rings of land and water around a central island, canals, harbour works and a large plain cut by ditches, which would leave straight, regular walls and channels in seafloor depth data or lidar. Every site tested so far has turned out to be natural rock or a much later ruin.

- **Helike** (possible inspiration, plausible; 38.22°N 22.13°E, give or take 3 km). The Greek city of Helike was destroyed by an earthquake and wave in 373 BCE, within Plato's lifetime, and some scholars think the disaster gave him the image of a city swallowed in one night. Its buried remains were located in 2001. Against: Helike was a small city near Athens, not a distant empire; the link is to the manner of destruction only. Sources: [BBC Horizon, "Helike: The Real Atlantis" (2002)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/science/horizon/2001/helike.shtml); [S. Soter and D. Katsonopoulou, "Submergence and uplift of settlements in the area of Helike, Greece", Geoarchaeology 26 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1002/gea.20366).
- **Santorini (Thera)** (possible inspiration, plausible; 36.41°N 25.43°E, give or take 10 km). Spyridon Marinatos argued in 1939 that the Bronze Age eruption of Thera wrecked Minoan Crete, and later writers tied that disaster to Plato. The eruption, about 1600 BCE, buried the town of Akrotiri and is the real event most often named behind the story. Against: The date, place and size do not fit Plato: he puts Atlantis beyond the Pillars of Heracles, 9,000 years before Solon, and Minoan Crete did not end with the eruption. Sources: [BBC News, "The wave that destroyed Atlantis" (H. Lilley, 2007)](https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/science/nature/6568053.stm); [S. Marinatos, "The Volcanic Destruction of Minoan Crete", Antiquity 13 (1939)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00028088).
- **Minoan Crete (Knossos)** (possible inspiration, plausible; 35.30°N 25.16°E, give or take 5 km). K. T. Frost proposed in 1913 that Plato's Atlantis is a memory of Minoan Crete, a rich sea power with bull rituals and a great palace at Knossos that faded from Greek knowledge. Against: Crete is inside the Mediterranean and never sank; the match rests on loose parallels with a text most scholars read as invented. Sources: [K. T. Frost, "The Critias and Minoan Crete", Journal of Hellenic Studies 33 (1913)](https://doi.org/10.2307/624108); [National Geographic, "Did these real-life events inspire the myth of Atlantis?" (W. Drye, updated 2025)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/atlantis).
- **Doñana marshes (Marisma de Hinojos)** (proposed, fringe; 37.02°N 6.44°W, give or take 20 km). Writers since the 1600s have placed Atlantis in the marshes at the mouth of the Guadalquivir and linked it to Tartessos. In 2004 Rainer Kühne pointed to satellite photos of the Marisma de Hinojos that seem to show two rectangles and parts of rings in the mud. Against: No excavation has found a city there, and the shapes in the satellite photos have not been shown to be buildings. Sources: [BBC News, "Satellite images 'show Atlantis'" (P. Rincon, 2004)](https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/science/nature/3766863.stm); [R. W. Kühne, "A location for 'Atlantis'?", Antiquity Project Gallery (2004)](https://doi.org/10.15184/s2978855200905171).
- **Richat Structure (Eye of the Sahara)** (proposed, fringe; 21.12°N 11.40°W, give or take 20 km). Online videos since 2018 have claimed that this 40 km ring of ridges in Mauritania matches the rings of Plato's city. Against: Geologists have shown it is an eroded dome above an igneous intrusion, it sits far inland and high above sea level, and surveys found no buildings or settlement debris in it. Sources: [G. Matton, M. Jebrak and J. K. W. Lee, "Resolving the Richat enigma: Doming and hydrothermal karstification above an alkaline complex", Geology 33 (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1130/G21542AR.1); [Geographical (Royal Geographical Society magazine), "Phenomena: The Eye of the Sahara"](https://geographical.co.uk/science-environment/phenomena-the-eye-of-the-sahara).
- **Black Sea (Snake Island and the north-west shelf)** (proposed, fringe; 43.41°N 34.26°E, give or take 300 km). Before about 5500 BCE a great plain lay in the north-west of what is now the Black Sea, then a smaller freshwater lake. The Schoppes say the Bosporus barrier broke open about 5510 BCE, drowning it, and that the Bosporus is the Pillars of Heracles; Snake Island is their capital. Against: Their date is about 5500 BCE, not 9,600 BCE. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/schoppe-christian-siegfried/); [Wikipedia, Black Sea deluge hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_deluge_hypothesis).
- **Cádiz (Gadir) and its bay** (claimed find, fringe; 36.53°N 6.30°W, give or take 30 km). Plato names the king of the island's tip Gadeiros, twin of Atlas, a name Atlantipedia links to Gades (Cádiz). Asher led an expedition off the coast in 1973 and announced a find of Plato's island. Against: The place-name link is old, but the 1973 expedition found nothing verifiable; this is Atlantipedia's account. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/cadiz/).
- **Hoggar (Ahaggar) Mountains, Sahara** (proposed, fringe; 23.29°N 5.53°E, give or take 150 km). Khun de Prorok, an explorer, became convinced Atlantis had a North African origin on the Hoggar plateau. Other writers have proposed the Ahaggar's Mount Tahat as the original Atlas of Herodotus. Against: The Hoggar is deep inland in the central Sahara, not in front of the Pillars. Atlantipedia's entry shows a map placing Atlantis in the Atlantic to the west. Sources: [Byron Khun de Prorok; James Bramwell, 1937](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/de-prorok-count-byron-khun/); [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/ahaggar-mountains/).
- **Lake Copais, Boeotia** (possible inspiration, fringe; 38.45°N 23.05°E, give or take 10 km). Scranton, a classical art historian at Chicago, suggested that Atlantis was the Copaic drainage complex and its civilisation: a Bronze Age society that cut canals and tunnels to drain a lake in central Greece. Against: Atlantipedia notes Scranton was 'somewhat unsettled' that Plato puts Atlantis to the west of Athens and Egypt, not in Boeotia. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/scranton-robert-lorentz/); [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Copais).
- **Little Sole Bank and the Celtic Shelf** (proposed, fringe; 48.50°N 8.75°W, give or take 25 km). Koudriavtsev, a Russian scientist, argued that Atlantis lay on the Celtic Shelf near the Scilly Isles, naming Little Sole Bank, whose highest point is about 75 m below the sea surface. Against: A press-reported claim never surveyed; Atlantipedia notes the institute he named is doubtful. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/koudriavtsev-viatcheslav/); [Marine Regions, Little Sole Bank](https://www.marineregions.org/).
- **Los Atlantes seamount, east of Lanzarote** (possible inspiration, fringe; 29.04°N 13.63°W, give or take 60 km). A seamount with three inactive volcanoes, once a series of islands in the Eocene, was found with beaches, cliffs and dunes at its flat summit. The project head suggested it could have inspired the legend. Against: The islands existed 56 to 34 million years ago (Eocene), long before people. Sources: [www.livescience.com](https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/volcanos/this-could-be-the-origin-of-the-atlantis-legend-mountain-that-sank-beneath-the-waves-discovered-off-canary-islands).
- **Malta** (proposed, fringe; 35.90°N 14.40°E, give or take 25 km). The Maltese authors compare Malta's archaeology, geology and topography with Plato's description and argue the islands are the remnants of Atlantis, citing the Neolithic megalithic temples. Against: Reply by a Maltese archaeologist; the page was blocked to me and is listed, not summarised. Sources: [Thorsten C. Franke (Atlantis-Scout, historical review), 2013](https://www.atlantis-scout.de/atlantis-malta-hoax.htm); [Malta: Echoes of Plato's Island (Prehistoric Society of Malta)](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/malta-echoes-of-platos-island-2/).
- **Seabed off Cabo San Antonio, western Cuba** (claimed find, fringe; 21.72°N 84.33°W, give or take 30 km). While surveying for the Cuban government, a Canadian company's sonar showed what looked like smooth blocks, crests and geometric shapes on the sea floor about 650 m down, resembling an urban complex. News sites ran 'Atlantis Discovered in Cuba'. Against: The Cuban geologist Manuel Iturralde said it would have taken about 50,000 years for such structures to sink to that depth, when no culture could build complex buildings. Sources: [www.ancient-origins.net](https://www.ancient-origins.net/ancient-places-americas/what-happened-sunken-city-cuba-001883?nopaging=1); [Atlantipedia, Paulina Zelitsky](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/tag/paulina-zelitsky).
- **Atlas Mountains and Morocco (Godron, Berlioux, Lagneau)** (proposed, fringe; 31.06°N 7.92°W, give or take 300 km). 19th-century French writers tied Plato's Atlantes to the Atlas range and the people Herodotus calls Atalantes in north-west Africa, and placed Atlantis in Morocco. Against: A 19th-century reading revived by modern writers; Atlantipedia says it had little support beyond the 1920s, and I found no field test. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/morocco-revised/); [Atlantipedia, chronology of Atlantis theories](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/chronology-of-atlantis-theories-2024/).
- **Chott el Djerid and the Gulf of Gabès, Tunisia** (proposed, fringe; 33.70°N 8.43°E, give or take 80 km). Herrmann held that the dried-up salt lake of Chott el Djerid was Lake Tritonis, known in Solon's day as the 'Atlantic Sea', and that an upheaval raised a barrier between the lake and the sea where the Pillars of Heracles stood. Borchardt put the city between the dried lake and Gabès. Against: Herrmann reduced Plato's dimensions by a factor of 30, claiming the Egyptian priest erred converting schoinoi into stades. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/tunisia/); [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/herrmann-albert/).
- **Dogger Bank and Silverpit crater** (possible inspiration, fringe; 55.25°N 3.50°E, give or take 120 km). Erlingsson takes the island of Atlantis to be Ireland, the sunken land to be the flooding of Dogger Bank, and the city to be the Silverpit crater at its base (Wikipedia). Against: The flooding is dated about 6200 BCE, not 9,600 BCE. Sources: [Atlantis from a Geographer's Perspective: Mapping the Fairy Land](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna5623857); [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doggerland).
- **Greenland and the north Atlantic (La Mothe le Vayer to Dantas)** (proposed, fringe; 72.00°N 40.00°W, give or take 800 km). Greenland was first proposed in the 17th century. Modern authors compare its outline with Athanasius Kircher's map of Atlantis, or propose the Baffin Basin to its west as the plain. Against: Atlantipedia notes Kircher's map places Atlantis between the Strait of Gibraltar and America, not between Canada and Scandinavia. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/greenland/).
- **Ife, south-west Nigeria (Frobenius)** (possible inspiration, fringe; 7.47°N 4.57°E, give or take 30 km). Frobenius held that the Yoruba culture of West Africa was a remainder of a separate culture circle, and saw in the Atlantis story a cultural memory of that ancient culture. Against: A diffusionist reading from the early 20th century that treats the story as a memory of Yoruba culture; no modern scholarship in the sources I found defends it. Sources: [Atlantipedia, chronology of Atlantis theories](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/chronology-of-atlantis-theories-2024/); [African expeditions; he formed an 'African Atlantis' idea (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Frobenius).
- **Ireland** (proposed, fringe; 53.35°N 7.92°W, give or take 200 km). The Swedish geographer Ulf Erlingsson argued in 2004 that the size and shape of Plato's island match Ireland, that its temples echo the passage tombs at Newgrange and Knowth, and that the sinking recalls the flooding of Dogger Bank in the North Sea about 6100 BCE. Against: Ireland did not sink, and its passage tombs were built thousands of years after the date Plato gives. Sources: [NBC News / Reuters, "Was Atlantis actually Ireland?" (2004)](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna5623857); [Wikipedia, "Location hypotheses of Atlantis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **Irish Sea plain (Anglesey to the Isle of Man)** (proposed, fringe; 53.50°N 5.00°W, give or take 120 km). Dunbavin argues that an impact of a comet changed climate and sea level, drowning a Neolithic civilisation on a low-lying plain off Wales, which was Plato's plain of Atlantis. Against: His date for the catastrophe is about 3100 BCE (Wikipedia). Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/dunbavin-paul/); [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **Le Danois Bank (El Cachucho), Bay of Biscay** (proposed, fringe; 44.08°N 5.10°W, give or take 25 km). Ribero-Meneses, a Spanish philologist, places Atlantis in northern Spain, and Atlantipedia says he chose this submarine mountain off Asturias, now a protected marine area. Wikipedia adds that he held it broke off the continental margin about 12,000 years ago, with a tsunami. Against: Wikipedia says detailed geological studies show the bank has been submerged for millions of years; the studies are not named there. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/ribero-meneses-jorge-maria/); [Marine Regions gazetteer, Le Danois Bank](https://www.marineregions.org/).
- **Pampa Aullagas, Bolivian Altiplano** (proposed, fringe; 19.20°S 67.06°W, give or take 40 km). Allen, formerly an RAF satellite-mapping expert, compares the Altiplano with Plato's description. He cites a vast canal network, aerial photographs showing concentric rings, and red, white and black rock at Pampa Aullagas. Against: Atlantipedia notes it conflicts with Plato's dating, dimensions, and the improbability of an invasion of the eastern Mediterranean from the west coast of South America. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/allen-j-m/); [Atlantipedia, Bolivia](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/bolivia/).
- **Salé and Chellah, near Rabat** (proposed, fringe; 34.01°N 6.82°W, give or take 10 km). Bergman compares the topography of Morocco with Plato's text. He first favoured Lixus, then a location near Rabat, an ancient Phoenician colony on the river Bou Regreg, called Salé, Sala or Chellah. Against: Presented at an Atlantis conference; no archaeology targeted at the claim and the site is a Phoenician and Roman city, not a sunken one. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/morocco-revised/).
- **Sardinia** (proposed, fringe; 40.00°N 9.00°E, give or take 100 km). The Italian journalist Sergio Frau argues that the Pillars of Heracles first meant the strait between Sicily and Tunisia, which would put Atlantis at Sardinia, home of the Bronze Age tower builders, and that a catastrophe, perhaps a tidal wave, struck the island about 1175 BCE. Against: It depends on moving the Pillars of Heracles away from Gibraltar, which most scholars do not accept, and Sardinia did not sink. Sources: [The Guardian, "Was Sardinia home to the mythical civilisation of Atlantis?" (2015)](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2015/aug/15/bronze-age-sardinia-archaeology-atlantis).
- **Sea of Azov** (proposed, fringe; 46.00°N 37.00°E, give or take 150 km). The authors say Plato's account describes an event of 9,600 BCE in the Black Sea corridor: glacial meltwater raised the Caspian, an earthquake broke a barrier, and the plain of Atlantis sank to form the Sea of Azov. Against: It is far east of the Strait of Gibraltar. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/sea-of-azov/); [listed by Atlantipedia's chronology as Sea of Azov](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/chronology-of-atlantis-theories-2024/).
- **Souss-Massa plain, Morocco** (proposed, fringe; 30.08°N 8.48°W, give or take 60 km). Hübner weighs Plato's clues by a method he calls hierarchical constraint satisfaction and says they lead to the Souss-Massa plain. He shows rocks coloured red, white and black still used in local buildings and possible harbour remains near Cape Ghir, and treats the destruction as a tsunami, not a sinking. Against: Atlantipedia's editor says Agadir is about 3,300 km by sea from Athens, too far for the Atlantean war, and that Plato says the empire reached Tyrrhenia, not lay west of it. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/hubner-michael/); [National Geographic, Does New Theory Pinpoint Lost City of Atlantis? (2015, Mark Adams interview)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/news/2015/03/150318-atlantis-morocco-santorini-plato-adams-ngbooktalk/).
- **Sundaland and Indonesia** (proposed, fringe; 0.00°N 110.00°E, give or take 1500 km). Santos, a Brazilian physicist, argued that Atlantis was Sundaland, the shelf of South-East Asia that flooded at the end of the last ice age, and that Plato's Pillars of Heracles are the volcanoes Krakatau and Dempo. Natawidjaja, an Indonesian geologist, has argued a related case. Against: Santos redefines the Pillars of Heracles as the Krakatau and Dempo mountains in the Sunda Strait, against the Greek-usage of Gibraltar. Sources: [www.thejakartapost.com](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2010/03/23/indonesia-the-lost-atlantis); [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/indonesia-3/).
- **Tantalis near Mount Sipylus (western Anatolia)** (possible inspiration, fringe; 38.57°N 27.45°E, give or take 20 km). James argues that Solon gathered the story in Lydia, not Egypt, and that Atlantis is Tantalis, the city of Tantalus, said by the Greeks to have been destroyed by an earthquake. He ties the war to Madduwattas of Zippasla, and the site to a vanished lake north of Mount Sipylus. Against: James moves the origin of the story to Lydia and treats Plato's Egypt and Atlantic setting as a mistake (Atlantipedia). Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/the-sunken-kingdom/); [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **Troy (Hisarlık)** (proposed, fringe; 39.96°N 26.24°E, give or take 2 km). The geoarchaeologist Eberhard Zangger argued in 1992 and 1993 that Plato's story is an Egyptian account of the Trojan War, and that the rings of water are the harbour basins of Bronze Age Troy. Against: Troy is in the wrong place, of the wrong date and did not sink; classicists did not take up the idea. Sources: [E. Zangger, "Plato's Atlantis Account: A Distorted Recollection of the Trojan War", Oxford Journal of Archaeology 12 (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0092.1993.tb00283.x); [Wikipedia, "Location hypotheses of Atlantis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **Antarctica** (proposed, rejected; 90.00°S 0.00°E, give or take 2500 km). Charles Hapgood argued from 1958 that the whole crust of the Earth slipped and moved a once temperate Antarctica to the pole. Rose and Rand Flem-Ath (When the Sky Fell, 1995) and Graham Hancock used the idea to place Atlantis under the ice. Against: Plate tectonics replaced crustal slip, and ice cores drilled there record eight glacial cycles of unbroken ice. Sources: [EPICA community members, "Eight glacial cycles from an Antarctic ice core", Nature 429 (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02599); [National Geographic, "Did these real-life events inspire the myth of Atlantis?" (W. Drye, updated 2025)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/atlantis).
- **Azores Plateau** (proposed, rejected; 38.62°N 28.03°W, give or take 300 km). Ignatius Donnelly argued in Atlantis: The Antediluvian World (1882) that the Azores are the mountain tops of a sunken continent in the mid Atlantic. His book started the modern search for Atlantis. Against: The islands are volcanoes on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge; Charles Schuchert showed in 1917 that they are not the remains of a continent, and cores show the plateau has been under water for millions of years. Sources: [I. Donnelly, Atlantis: The Antediluvian World (1882), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/4032); [C. Schuchert, "Atlantis and the permanency of the North Atlantic Ocean bottom", PNAS 3 (1917)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.3.2.65).
- **Bimini Road** (claimed find, rejected; 25.77°N 79.28°W, give or take 1 km). Divers found a 0.8 km line of large flat limestone blocks in 5 m of water off North Bimini in 1968. Followers of the psychic Edgar Cayce, who had said Atlantis would rise near Bimini, took it for a road or wall. Against: Cores and radiocarbon dates published in Nature in 1980 show it is natural beachrock that cracked into blocks where it formed. Sources: [M. McKusick and E. A. Shinn, "Bahamian Atlantis reconsidered", Nature 287 (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1038/287011a0); [Wikipedia, "Bimini Road"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimini_Road).
- **Cyprus Basin** (claimed find, rejected; 35.00°N 33.00°E, give or take 150 km). Robert Sarmast announced in 2004 that sonar showed walls and canals 1.5 km deep on the seabed between Cyprus and Syria, and that Cyprus is the last part of Atlantis still above water. Against: Marine geologists who had surveyed the same seabed said the shapes are natural folds and slide scars, and the basin has been deep water for millions of years. Sources: [Live Science, "Claimed Discovery of Atlantis Called 'Completely Bogus'" (2004)](https://www.livescience.com/history/atlantis_theory_041115.html); [C. Hübscher et al., "Salt tectonics and mud volcanism in the Latakia and Cyprus Basins, eastern Mediterranean", Tectonophysics 470 (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tecto.2008.08.019).
- **Heligoland** (proposed, rejected; 54.18°N 7.88°E, give or take 20 km). The German author Jürgen Spanuth claimed that Atlantis was a Bronze Age kingdom on drowned land beside Heligoland in the North Sea, destroyed about 1200 BCE. Against: No survey has found a Bronze Age city on the seabed there and archaeologists did not accept the claim; a 2025 article in the state archaeology journal of Schleswig-Holstein looks back on the affair. Sources: [U. Ickerodt and F. Huber, "Als Atlantis vor Helgoland lag", Archäologische Nachrichten aus Schleswig-Holstein (2025)](https://doi.org/10.5771/0942-9107-2025-1-18); [Wikipedia, "Location hypotheses of Atlantis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **Spartel Bank** (proposed, rejected; 35.90°N 5.96°W, give or take 5 km). Jacques Collina-Girard proposed in 2001 that this drowned island just west of the Strait of Gibraltar, above water until about 12,000 years ago, was Plato's island. Marc-Andre Gutscher mapped it in 2005 and tested whether an earthquake and tsunami could have destroyed it. Against: The mapped island was small and its top is put 56 to 72 m down, so by Plato's date it was a shoal or a reef; Gutscher later said it held no Bronze Age society. Sources: [M.-A. Gutscher, "Destruction of Atlantis by a great earthquake and tsunami? A geological analysis of the Spartel Bank hypothesis", Geology 33 (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1130/G21597AR.1); [J. Collina-Girard, "L'Atlantide devant le detroit de Gibraltar? Mythe et geologie", Comptes Rendus de l'Academie des Sciences (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1251-8050(01)01629-9).
- **Yucatán and the Maya (Brasseur, Le Plongeon)** (possible inspiration, rejected; 19.55°N 89.30°W, give or take 300 km). Brasseur read the Troano Codex and other Maya sources as records of Atlantis. Le Plongeon, a photographer, followed Donnelly's idea that the Old and New World alphabets derive from a Phoenician root through Atlantis, and tried to decipher the Maya script. Against: Brasseur is significant today primarily for discovering and publishing colonial sources on the Maya, including the Troano Codex, not for his Atlantis reading. Sources: [Later epigraphers (via Atlantipedia)](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/le-plongeon-augustus/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/brasseur-de-bourbourg-charles-etienne-1814-1874).
- **Gamla Uppsala (Sweden)** (proposed, rejected; 59.89°N 17.64°E, give or take 5 km). Olof Rudbeck, a professor at Uppsala, argued in his Atlantica (1679 to 1702) that Sweden was Atlantis and the cradle of civilisation, with its capital at Old Uppsala. Against: It was a patriotic exercise built on forced word likenesses; no later scholar has accepted it. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Olof Rudbeck"](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Olof-Rudbeck); [Wikipedia, "Location hypotheses of Atlantis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).
- **The Arctic and Spitsbergen (Bailly)** (proposed, rejected; 78.75°N 16.00°E, give or take 600 km). Bailly, an astronomer, proposed that Spitsbergen was the site of Hyperborea and Atlantis and that Atlantis was the root civilisation that civilised the Chinese, Indians and Egyptians. He met the problem of Plato's elephants by saying the Arctic was warmer in ancient times. Against: Spitsbergen is not in front of the Pillars. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/bailly-jean-silvain/); [Wikipedia, Jean Sylvain Bailly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Sylvain_Bailly).
- **The Holy Land, Israel and Sinai** (proposed, rejected; 31.00°N 35.00°E, give or take 150 km). From the 16th to the 18th century, several scholars took Plato's story as a garbled account of early Jewish history, with the Promised Land as Atlantis. A modern writer, Jaime Manuschevich, identifies Israel with Sinai as the site. Against: The Levant is at the eastern end of the Mediterranean. Sources: [atlantipedia.ie](https://atlantipedia.ie/samples/holy-land/).
- **The New World (López de Gómara to Bacon)** (proposed, rejected; 19.00°N 96.00°W, give or take 2500 km). After 1492 several writers took the Americas to be Plato's island or the 'opposite continent' beyond it. Zárate said the ancient peoples crossed from Atlantis; Sarmiento stretched Atlantis from two leagues west of Gibraltar to include all of America. Bacon's fable reuses the name for America. Against: America did not sink; Bacon himself calls Plato's description 'poetical and fabulous' while keeping a core of truth. Sources: [www.gutenberg.org](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2434/pg2434.txt); [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Location_hypotheses_of_Atlantis).

#### Alpena-Amberley Ridge hunting sites (found)

About 9,000 years ago the water in Lake Huron stood far lower than now and a rocky ridge ran dry across the lake from Michigan to Ontario. Caribou crossed it and hunters built stone lanes and blinds to ambush them. John O'Shea's team found the structures on the lake bed, where nothing has disturbed them since. *About 9,000 years ago; reported 2009 and 2014.*

What to look for: Lines and rings of boulders on a drowned ridge: drive lanes, hunting blinds and caches. Side-scan and scanning sonar find them, then divers and remote vehicles look for stone flakes.

- **Alpena-Amberley Ridge, Lake Huron** (found here, confirmed; 44.59°N 82.76°W, give or take 80 km). The Drop 45 Drive Lane is a stone structure built to channel migrating caribou towards waiting hunters. With the artefacts found beside it, it shows how early hunters organised the drives by season. Sources: [J. O'Shea et al., "A 9,000-year-old caribou hunting structure beneath Lake Huron", PNAS 111 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1404404111).

#### Atlit Yam (found)

Atlit Yam is a Neolithic village on the seabed off the Carmel coast of Israel, 8 to 12 m under water. People lived there about 9,000 years ago and left houses, wells, graves and a ring of standing stones. Rising seas after the Ice Age covered it, and sand kept it whole until it was found in 1984. *Occupied about 8100 to 7500 BP (late Pre-Pottery Neolithic); found 1984.*

What to look for: Stone wall footings, stone-lined wells and upright stones on a clay seabed, exposed when storms strip the sand. Divers find them; they are too small and low for most sonar.

- **Atlit Yam** (found here, confirmed; 32.71°N 34.94°E, give or take 2 km). Ehud Galili and colleagues describe it as the largest and most deeply submerged prehistoric settlement found on the Mediterranean coast: about 60,000 square metres, 8 to 12 m below sea level, dated to about 8100 to 7500 BP. Sources: [E. Galili et al., "Atlit-Yam: A Prehistoric Site on the Sea Floor off the Israeli Coast", Journal of Field Archaeology 20 (1993)](https://doi.org/10.1179/jfa.1993.20.2.133); [Wikipedia, "Atlit Yam"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlit_Yam).

#### Baiae (found)

Baiae was the most fashionable seaside resort of the Roman rich, on the Bay of Naples. The ground there rises and falls with the volcanic activity of the Phlegraean Fields, and the lower part of the town has sunk under the bay. Its villas, mosaics and statues can be seen under shallow water. *Resort from the 1st century BCE; deserted because of malaria in 1500 CE.*

What to look for: Mosaic floors, walls, fish ponds, roads and harbour moles under a few metres of clear water, visible in air photos and mapped by divers and photogrammetry.

- **Baiae** (found here, confirmed; 40.82°N 14.07°E, give or take 2 km). More than 100 m of the old shore front now lies under the bay because of bradyseism, the slow sinking and rising of the ground over the magma chamber of the Phlegraean Fields. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Baiae"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Baiae); [Wikipedia, "Baiae"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiae).

#### Beringia (found)

Beringia is the land that joined Siberia and Alaska when Ice Age seas were low. People and animals crossed it into the Americas. The central part now lies under the shallow Bering and Chukchi seas. *Exposed during glacial periods, when sea level was 100 m or more lower.*

What to look for: Old river channels, lake basins and shorelines on the shallow seabed, seen in bathymetry and sonar. Pollen, insects and plant remains in seabed cores show what grew on the bridge.

- **Bering Strait** (found here, confirmed; 65.75°N 168.98°W, give or take 500 km). During glacial periods sea level fell by 100 m or more and the shallow floor of the Bering and Chukchi seas became dry land. Seabed cores hold the remains of the tundra plants and insects that lived on it. Sources: [US National Park Service, "Beringia: Lost World of the Ice Age"](https://www.nps.gov/articles/aps-v12-i2-c8.htm); [S. Elias et al., "Life and times of the Bering land bridge", Nature 382 (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1038/382060a0).
- **Bering Land Bridge National Preserve** (found here, confirmed; 65.83°N 164.17°W, give or take 50 km). This preserve on the Seward Peninsula in Alaska protects a part of Beringia that stayed above water. Sources: [US National Park Service, Bering Land Bridge National Preserve](https://www.nps.gov/bela/index.htm); [US National Park Service, Shared Beringian Heritage Program](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/index.htm).

#### Bouldnor Cliff (found)

Bouldnor Cliff is a Mesolithic site on the bed of the Solent, off the Isle of Wight, about 11 m under water. People lived there about 8,000 years ago, when the Solent was a wooded valley, and left worked timber, flint tools and hearths in peat. It was found in 1999 when divers saw a lobster throwing worked flints out of its burrow. *Occupied about 6000 BCE; found 1999.*

What to look for: A drowned land surface of peat and tree stumps at the foot of an underwater cliff, with flints and cut wood eroding out. Bathymetry finds the cliff; only divers find the finds.

- **Bouldnor Cliff** (found here, confirmed; 50.71°N 1.46°W, give or take 1 km). Sediment from the old land surface held DNA of wheat about 8,000 years old, 2,000 years before farming is known in Britain, which the authors read as trade with farmers on the continent. Another scientist published a comment questioning the result. Sources: [O. Smith, G. Momber et al., "Sedimentary DNA from a submerged site reveals wheat in the British Isles 8000 years ago", Science 347 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261278); [K. D. Bennett, Comment on "Sedimentary DNA from a submerged site reveals wheat in the British Isles 8000 years ago", Science 349 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab1886).

#### Canopus (found)

Canopus was a town near the western mouth of the Nile, famous in Greek and Roman times for its temples of Osiris and Serapis and for its pleasure seekers. Its eastern part sank under the Bay of Abu Qir, like nearby Thonis-Heracleion. Franck Goddio's team has mapped the sunken remains east of the modern port of Abu Qir. *Named by Solon in the 6th century BCE; sunk by the 8th century CE.*

What to look for: Temple walls, statues and building rubble under sand in shallow, cloudy water, found by magnetometer and sonar survey and checked by divers.

- **East Canopus (Bay of Abu Qir)** (found here, confirmed; 31.30°N 30.08°E, give or take 5 km). Two kilometres east of the port of Abu Qir the IEASM team identified a zone with many sunken remains, some of them matching what Prince Omar Toussoun recorded there in 1933. It identifies the zone as the eastern part of Canopus. Sources: [Franck Goddio / IEASM, "Canopus"](https://www.franckgoddio.org/projects/sunken-civilizations/canopus/); [Review of F. Goddio, Underwater Archaeology in the Canopic Region in Egypt: The Topography of Heracleion-Thonis and East Canopus 1996-2006, Journal of Egyptian Archaeology 95 (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1177/030751330909500127).

#### Cantre'r Gwaelod (legend)

Welsh legend tells of a rich lowland in Cardigan Bay, ruled by Gwyddno Garanhir, that the sea drowned when its dykes or its well were left unwatched. The oldest form is a poem in the 13th century Black Book of Carmarthen. Drowned forests on the shore and a medieval map suggest real memories of lost coast lie behind it. *Set in about the 6th century CE; earliest text about 1250.*

What to look for: Tree stumps and peat on beaches at low tide, and drowned islands or ridges in bathymetry of the bay. Shingle ridges that look like causeways are natural glacial moraines.

- **Borth and Ynyslas submerged forest** (possible inspiration, leading view; 52.48°N 4.05°W, give or take 5 km). Stumps of pine, oak, alder and birch, killed by rising water and peat thousands of years ago, stand in the beach here and show after storms. They are the most likely source of the story of a drowned land. Against: The forest drowned in prehistory, long before the time the legend describes. Sources: [BBC Travel, "How a storm revealed a Welsh kingdom" (2020)](https://www.bbc.com/travel/gallery/20200318-how-a-storm-revealed-a-welsh-kingdom); [Wikipedia, "Cantre'r Gwaelod"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantre'r_Gwaelod).
- **Lost islands of Cardigan Bay** (proposed, plausible; 52.50°N 4.42°W, give or take 30 km). Simon Haslett and David Willis showed in 2022 that the Gough Map, a map of Britain from the 13th or 14th century, marks two islands in Cardigan Bay that no longer exist. They argue the islands were the last pieces of a low coast of glacial deposits, worn away by the 16th century. Against: The case rests on one medieval map and a model of erosion; no seabed remains of the islands have been found, and other scholars have challenged the reading of the map. Sources: [S. Haslett and D. Willis, "The 'lost' islands of Cardigan Bay, Wales, UK", Atlantic Geoscience 58 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.4138/atlgeo.2022.005); [C. Delano-Smith et al., Discussion of "The 'lost' islands of Cardigan Bay", Atlantic Geoscience 58 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.4138/atlgeo.2022.011).
- **Sarn Badrig** (proposed, rejected; 52.81°N 4.16°W, give or take 10 km). This shingle reef runs out to sea from the coast of Gwynedd. Folklore took it for one of the dykes of the drowned land. Against: It is made of glacial deposits left by retreating ice sheets at the end of the last Ice Age. Sources: [BBC News, "Map may show evidence of Wales' Atlantis off Ceredigion" (2022)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-wales-62605682); [Wikipedia, "Sarn Badrig"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarn_Badrig).

#### Doggerland (found)

Doggerland was the plain that joined Britain to mainland Europe when Ice Age seas were low. Mesolithic hunters lived on it until rising water drowned it about 8,000 years ago. It is a real, mapped landscape under the southern North Sea, known from trawled finds and from seismic survey data. *Dry land from the last Ice Age until about 6200 BCE.*

What to look for: River valleys, lake basins and old coastlines buried under seabed mud, visible in seismic reflection data and fine bathymetry. Finds come from peat and from stone and bone tools caught in trawls or washed on to beaches.

- **Doggerland plain (southern North Sea)** (found here, confirmed; 52.00°N 3.00°E, give or take 250 km). Teams led by Vincent Gaffney have used seismic data gathered for oil and gas to map the rivers, lakes and coasts of the drowned plain. A 2020 study found the first deposits of the Storegga tsunami of about 8150 BP in the southern North Sea and argued that the wave did not end Doggerland on its own. Sources: [J. Walker, V. Gaffney et al., "A great wave: the Storegga tsunami and the end of Doggerland?", Antiquity 94 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2020.49); [National Geographic Magazine, "Searching for Doggerland" (2012)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/doggerland).
- **Dogger Bank** (found here, confirmed; 55.25°N 3.50°E, give or take 100 km). The bank was the high ground of Doggerland; a barbed antler point trawled up east of the Wash in 1931 first showed that people had lived on what is now seabed. Models of the Storegga tsunami suggest the wave overran what was left of it. Sources: [BBC News, "Prehistoric North Sea 'Atlantis' hit by 5m tsunami" (2014)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-27224243); [Wikipedia, "Doggerland"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doggerland).

#### Dunwich (found)

Dunwich in Suffolk was one of the largest ports of medieval England. Storms in the 13th and 14th centuries blocked its harbour and the cliff has been falling into the sea ever since, taking the town with it. Sonar surveys led by David Sear have mapped the ruins of its churches on the seabed. *Lost to the sea from the 13th century CE on; seabed surveys from 2008.*

What to look for: Piles of worked stone and flint rubble where churches fell from the cliff, up to 10 m down in water with almost no visibility. Multibeam and side-scan sonar, sub-bottom profiling and acoustic imaging cameras find them; old maps say which building is which.

- **Dunwich seabed** (found here, confirmed; 52.28°N 1.63°E, give or take 2 km). The Dunwich project matched historic maps to multibeam, side-scan and sub-bottom sonar and then to acoustic images, and located the remains of medieval buildings, churches and a priory among them, up to 10 m under the sea. Sources: [D. Sear et al., "Cartographic, Geophysical and Diver Surveys of the Medieval Town Site at Dunwich" (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.2010.00275.x); [BBC News, "Dunwich underwater images show 'Britain's Atlantis'" (2013)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-suffolk-22482097).

#### Dwarka (Dvaraka) (disputed)

The Mahabharata and the Puranas say that Krishna's city of Dvaraka on the coast of Gujarat was swallowed by the sea after his death. Marine archaeologists have found stone structures and many stone anchors in shallow water off modern Dwarka and Bet Dwarka. Their age is disputed: the finds show a harbour of the historical and medieval periods, and nothing proves a city of Krishna's legendary time. *Legend set in the age of the Mahabharata; underwater surveys since 1983.*

What to look for: Dressed stone blocks, walls and stone anchors on the seabed in 3 to 12 m of water, seen by divers and side-scan sonar. Dates need pottery or organic material sealed under the stones, which is what is still missing.

- **Seabed off Dwarka** (claimed find, plausible; 22.24°N 68.97°E, give or take 3 km). The Marine Archaeology Unit of India's National Institute of Oceanography dug under water here from 1983 to 1990, and S. R. Rao, who led the work, took the stone structures on the seabed for a city of about 1500 BCE. Later seasons found many stone anchors in 10 to 14 m of water. Against: The 1500 BCE date rests on a single radiocarbon sample, and the institute's own later study dates the grapnel anchors to Indo-Arab trade of the 8th to 16th centuries CE. Sources: [A. S. Gaur, Sundaresh, S. Tripati et al., "A group of 20 stone anchors from the waters of Dwarka, on the Gujarat Coast, India", International Journal of Nautical Archaeology 30 (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.2001.tb01359.x); [Wikipedia, "Dvārakā"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dv%C4%81rak%C4%81).
- **Bet Dwarka** (proposed, plausible; 22.45°N 69.12°E, give or take 3 km). Work on this island in the 1980s found pottery of the Late Harappan period. In 5 to 8 m of water near the jetty lie stone and lead anchors, amphora sherds and a lead ingot of about the 1st century BCE to the 2nd century CE, which show an old anchorage. Against: The finds are a small coastal settlement, not the great city of the epic. Sources: [A. S. Gaur, Sundaresh and S. Tripati, "Evidence for Indo-Roman Trade from Bet Dwarka Waters, West Coast of India", International Journal of Nautical Archaeology 35 (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.2005.00080.x); [Wikipedia, "Bet Dwarka"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bet_Dwarka).
- **Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay)** (claimed find, rejected; 20.73°N 72.30°E, give or take 60 km). In 2001 and 2002 India's National Institute of Ocean Technology reported side-scan sonar images of geometric shapes about 36 m deep in the gulf, with dredged objects and a piece of wood dated to about 9,500 years ago. India's minister for ocean development presented it as a city older than any known. Against: The objects were dredged, not excavated, so nothing ties the dated wood to the sonar shapes, and critics say the stone "artefacts" may be natural; no site has been confirmed. Sources: [S. Kathiroli et al., "New Archaeological sites in the Gulf of Cambay, India", Episodes 26 (2003)](https://doi.org/10.18814/epiiugs/2003/v26i1/003); [BBC News, "Lost city 'could rewrite history'" (2002)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/south_asia/1768109.stm).

#### Helike (found)

Helike was a Greek city on the Gulf of Corinth, destroyed in one winter night of 373 BCE by an earthquake and a wave from the sea. Ancient visitors said its ruins could be seen under water for centuries, then the site was lost. In 2001 the Helike Project found its buried remains on land, under the silt of a filled-in lagoon. *Destroyed 373 BCE; located 2001 CE.*

What to look for: The city is not on the seabed, as searchers assumed for years, but under metres of river and lagoon silt on the coastal plain. Bore holes, ground-penetrating radar and magnetometry find walls and pottery layers under farmland.

- **Helike (near Rizomylos and Nikolaiika)** (found here, confirmed; 38.22°N 22.13°E, give or take 3 km). Dora Katsonopoulou and Steven Soter began the search in 1988 and drilled bore holes across the delta. They found occupation layers from the Early Bronze Age to late antiquity, with signs that settlements here were drowned and raised again more than once. Sources: [S. Soter and D. Katsonopoulou, "Submergence and uplift of settlements in the area of Helike, Greece, from the Early Bronze Age to late antiquity", Geoarchaeology 26 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1002/gea.20366); [BBC Horizon, "Helike: The Real Atlantis" (2002)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/science/horizon/2001/helike.shtml).

#### Hy-Brasil (legend)

Hy-Brasil is a phantom island that sea charts showed west of Ireland from about 1325 until 1865. Expeditions left Bristol in 1480 and 1481 to look for it and found nothing. Scholars see it as a map error that merged with Irish stories of an island in the western sea. *On charts from about 1325 to 1865 CE.*

What to look for: A real island would be a shoal in bathymetry. The shallowest ground in the mapped position, Porcupine Bank, is still about 200 m under water.

- **Porcupine Bank** (proposed, fringe; 53.33°N 13.50°W, give or take 60 km). This bank about 200 km west of Ireland, found in 1862, has been suggested as the origin of the island on the charts. Against: The bank lies about 200 m deep and has not been land in historic times; scholars see the island as a map error mixed with Celtic otherworld lore. Sources: [B. Freitag, Hy Brasil: The Metamorphosis of an Island (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789401209106); [Wikipedia, "Hy Brasil"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy_Brasil).

#### Kumari Kandam (legend)

Medieval commentaries on Tamil literature tell of Pandya lands south of Cape Comorin that the sea swallowed, along with the first two Tamil literary academies. In the late 1800s and 1900s Tamil writers joined this to Lemuria and described a lost continent called Kumari Kandam, the home of the Tamils. Historians treat the continent as a modern myth built on older flood legends. *Flood legends in commentaries of about the 8th to 14th centuries CE; the continent idea dates from the 1890s on.*

What to look for: Rising seas did drown a strip of the south Indian shelf after the Ice Age, so drowned shorelines and river mouths exist offshore in bathymetry. There is no sunken continent in the deep ocean south of India.

- **Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin)** (where it began, fringe; 8.09°N 77.54°E, give or take 300 km). The legend places the lost land south of this cape, the southern tip of India. Tamil revivalists drew maps of a continent reaching from here towards Madagascar and Australia. Against: Plate tectonics rules out a sunken continent, and the texts speak of lost territory, not a continent; the scale was added in modern times. Sources: [S. Ramaswamy, "History at Land's End: Lemuria in Tamil Spatial Fables", Journal of Asian Studies 59 (2000)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2658944); [Wikipedia, "Kumari Kandam"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumari_Kandam).

#### Lake Titicaca underwater ruins (found)

Andean tradition makes Lake Titicaca the place where the world began, and stories of drowned temples there are old. Underwater excavation on Khoa Reef, near the Island of the Sun, has proved that the Tiwanaku state made rich offerings in the lake between the 8th and 10th centuries CE. A claim in 2000 of a large temple on the lake bed has never been published in detail. *Tiwanaku offerings of the 8th to 10th centuries CE.*

What to look for: Offerings on a reef top: stone boxes, incense burners, gold sheet, shells and animal bones in lake mud, found by divers and excavated with suction dredges. Walls or terraces would show in multibeam sonar of the lake floor.

- **Khoa Reef, Island of the Sun** (found here, confirmed; 16.02°S 69.18°W, give or take 5 km). The first systematic underwater excavation here found ceramic incense burners in the shape of cats, killed young llamas and ornaments of metal, shell and worked stone, left in repeated rituals by the Tiwanaku state between the 8th and 10th centuries CE. Sources: [C. Delaere, J. Capriles and C. Stanish, "Underwater ritual offerings in the Island of the Sun and the formation of the Tiwanaku state", PNAS 116 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1820749116).
- **Lake bed near Copacabana (2000 claim)** (claimed find, fringe; 16.17°S 69.09°W, give or take 20 km). In August 2000 a team of divers backed by the group Akakor Geographical Exploring said it had found a temple about 200 m long, a crop terrace, a road and an 800 m wall under the lake, not far from Copacabana. Against: It was announced to the press with full results promised later; no scholarly report of a temple was found for this dataset, so treat it as unconfirmed. Sources: [BBC News, "Ancient temple found under Lake Titicaca" (2000)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/americas/892616.stm).

#### Lemuria (legend)

The zoologist Philip Sclater proposed Lemuria in 1864 as a sunken land bridge in the Indian Ocean, to explain why lemur-like fossils and animals occur in both Madagascar and India. Continental drift explained the pattern better and geologists dropped the idea. Occult writers and Tamil nationalists later remade it as a lost homeland of humankind. *Proposed in 1864; abandoned by science by the 1960s.*

What to look for: Nothing: ocean floor is young basalt, not drowned continent. The real traces of the old link between India and Madagascar are matching rocks and fossils on land, and scraps of continental crust under volcanic islands.

- **Madagascar** (where it began, rejected; 20.00°S 47.00°E, give or take 500 km). Sclater started from the lemurs of Madagascar and their relatives in India, and supposed a continent had once joined the two. Against: Madagascar and India were once joined, but plate movement carried them apart tens of millions of years ago; no land bridge sank. Sources: [S. Ramaswamy, The Lost Land of Lemuria (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1525/9780520931855); [Wikipedia, "Lemuria"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemuria).
- **Mauritia (under Mauritius)** (proposed, rejected; 12.18°S 61.17°E, give or take 500 km). In 2013 geologists reported zircons between 600 and 1,970 million years old in the beach sand of Mauritius, a volcanic island only about 9 million years old. They take them as proof of a buried scrap of continent, which they named Mauritia. Against: Mauritia broke up by about 85 million years ago, long before there were people or lemurs; it is not the Lemuria of legend. Sources: [T. Torsvik et al., "A Precambrian microcontinent in the Indian Ocean", Nature Geoscience 6 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo1736); [BBC News, "Fragments of ancient continent buried under Indian Ocean" (2013)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-21551149).

#### Lyonesse (legend)

Lyonesse is the home of Tristan in Arthurian romance; the name first appears in Thomas Malory's Le Morte Darthur in the late 15th century. William Camden gave the name in 1586 to a land between Cornwall and the Isles of Scilly that tradition said the sea had drowned. The romance kingdom is fiction, but the Isles of Scilly really were a single larger island that rising seas broke up over thousands of years. *Named by Malory in the late 15th century; drowned land called Lyonnesse by Camden, 1586.*

What to look for: Field walls, hut circles and cists that run down the beach and under the sea between the Scilly islands, visible at low tide, in bathymetry and in air photos. Peat and tree stumps in the intertidal zone date the flooding.

- **Isles of Scilly** (possible inspiration, leading view; 49.92°N 6.30°W, give or take 15 km). Prehistoric field walls and houses lie under the shallow water between the islands. A 2020 study of Scilly in Science Advances rebuilt 12,000 years of sea level and coastline there and found that land was lost at an uneven rate as one large island broke into many. Against: The drowning took centuries and happened long before the Middle Ages; it was not one night. Sources: [R. Barnett, D. Charman et al., "Nonlinear landscape and cultural response to sea-level rise", Science Advances 6 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abb6376); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Lyonnesse"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lyonnesse).
- **Seven Stones Reef** (proposed, fringe; 50.05°N 6.07°W, give or take 5 km). Cornish tradition names this reef between Land's End and Scilly as the site of the chief town of Lyonesse, and fishermen told of hauling up pieces of doors and windows there. Against: The reef is bare rock; no survey has found any building. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Lyonesse"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyonesse); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Lyonnesse"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lyonnesse).

#### Mu (legend)

Augustus Le Plongeon invented Mu in the 1800s from a mistaken reading of a Maya book. James Churchward moved it to the Pacific in a series of books from 1926 and said an advanced civilisation lived there until 12,000 years ago. No geologist or archaeologist accepts it: the Pacific floor holds no sunken continent. *Named in the 19th century; popularised from 1926.*

What to look for: Nothing: seafloor mapping and drilling show the Pacific basin is ocean crust. Monuments that fans link to Mu, such as those on Easter Island or Pohnpei, were built by Polynesians and Micronesians within the last 1,500 years.

- **Central Pacific Ocean** (proposed, rejected; 0.00°N 160.00°W, give or take 4000 km). Churchward claimed a continent once filled the central Pacific from Hawaii to Easter Island and Fiji, and that the islands are its mountain tops. Against: The Pacific islands are volcanoes and atolls on ocean crust, and the tablets Churchward said he read have never been produced. Sources: [Review of J. Churchward, The Lost Continent of Mu, The Geographical Journal 79 (1932)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1785261); [Wikipedia, "Mu (mythical lost continent)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_(mythical_lost_continent)).

#### Old Winchelsea (not found yet)

Old Winchelsea was one of the main ports of medieval Sussex, built on a shingle spit in Rye Bay. The sea wore the spit away through the 13th century and a great storm in 1287 destroyed what was left. Edward I laid out the present town of Winchelsea on a hill inland; the old site has not been found. *Destroyed by the storm of 1287 CE.*

What to look for: Building stone, wharf timbers and pottery under shingle, marsh clay or the bed of Rye Bay. The coast here has moved so much that the remains may be under land as easily as under sea; boreholes and seismic survey are the likely tools.

- **Rye Bay, south-east of Winchelsea** (search area, leading view; 50.92°N 0.71°E, give or take 8 km). The old town is believed to have stood on a sand and shingle spit that shielded the marshes between Fairlight and Dungeness. The pin is the present town, which replaced it; the lost site lies some way to the south-east. Against: The exact position is not known, and the shingle has been reworked many times since. Sources: [University of Sussex, Coastview project, "Changes in the Rother Mouth"](https://www.sussex.ac.uk/geography/researchprojects/coastview/Estuaries/rye-slides.htm); [Wikipedia, "Winchelsea"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchelsea).

#### Olous (found)

Olous was a Greek city state on the north coast of Crete, at the neck of land that joins the Spinalonga peninsula to the island. Part of it now lies under the shallow water of Elounda Bay. Its walls can be seen by swimmers, and geophysicists have used it to test survey methods for very shallow sea. *City of the Classical and Hellenistic periods.*

What to look for: Wall lines under less than 2 m of water and under the sand below it. Electrical resistivity tomography with cables laid on the sea floor maps buried walls where sonar and magnetometers do poorly.

- **Olous (Elounda)** (found here, confirmed; 35.26°N 25.73°E, give or take 1 km). A team from the Institute for Mediterranean Studies ran 8.3 km of electrical resistivity lines over the shallow sea bed here and mapped buried and submerged structures of the ancient town. Sources: [K. Simyrdanis, N. Papadopoulos and G. Cantoro, "Shallow Off-Shore Archaeological Prospection with 3-D Electrical Resistivity Tomography: The Case of Olous" (2016)](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs8110897); [Wikipedia, "Olous"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olous).

#### Pavlopetri (found)

Pavlopetri is a Bronze Age town that lies in 3 to 4 m of water off the coast of Laconia in southern Greece. Nicholas Flemming found it in 1967 and a Cambridge team mapped it in 1968. Work since 2009 has shown that people lived there about 5,000 years ago, which makes it the oldest known submerged town. *Occupied from about 3000 BCE; found 1967.*

What to look for: Stone wall footings, streets, courtyards and cist graves on a sandy seabed a few metres deep, clear in air photos, sector-scan sonar and stereo photogrammetry. Shifting sand covers and uncovers them.

- **Pavlopetri** (found here, confirmed; 36.52°N 22.99°E, give or take 1 km). A Greek and British team led by Jon Henderson of the University of Nottingham surveyed the town from 2009 and found pottery from the end of the Stone Age, which put its start some 1,200 years earlier than thought. Sources: [The Guardian, "Lost Greek city that may have inspired Atlantis myth gives up secrets" (2009)](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2009/oct/16/lost-greek-city-atlantis-myth); [A. Harding, G. Cadogan and R. Howell, "Pavlopetri: an Underwater Bronze Age Town in Laconia" (1969)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0068245400014544).

#### Phanagoria (found)

Phanagoria was the largest Greek city on the Taman Peninsula, on the Asian side of the Kerch Strait, founded about 543 BCE by settlers from Teos. It was later the eastern capital of the Bosporan Kingdom. About a third of its 75 hectares is now under the sea. *Founded about 543 BCE; lived in until the Middle Ages.*

What to look for: Harbour works, streets and building stone in a few metres of water off the present beach, mapped by divers and sonar. The land part is a low mound with a large cemetery around it.

- **Phanagoria** (found here, confirmed; 45.28°N 36.97°E, give or take 2 km). A survey in 1998 recorded the submerged remains of the city in Taman Bay. One third of the ancient town lies under water. Sources: [C. Brandon and G. Tsetskhladze, "Notes on the Survey of the Submerged Remains of Phanagoria in the Taman Peninsula, 1998", Ancient West and East 1 (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004496446_024); [Wikipedia, "Phanagoria"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phanagoria).

#### Poompuhar (Kaveripattinam) (disputed)

Poompuhar was the great port of the early Chola kings at the mouth of the Kaveri, praised in Tamil poems of about 2,000 years ago and in the epics Silappathikaram and Manimekalai. Erosion and floods washed much of the old town away. Remains of the port lie on the shore and in shallow water; a claim that sonar shows a far older harbour 30 km out to sea is not accepted. *Port of about 300 BCE to 300 CE; underwater surveys since the 1990s.*

What to look for: Brick structures, ring wells and pottery on the shore and in a few metres of water, found by divers. Claims from coarse global depth grids need multibeam survey and diving to check, because gridding makes false straight lines.

- **Poompuhar coast** (found here, confirmed; 11.14°N 79.86°E, give or take 5 km). Underwater exploration by the Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology in 2026 found ancient ring wells and a piece of a West Asian torpedo jar off the coast, more evidence of the early historic port. Sources: [The Hindu, "Torpedo jar fragment, ring wells found in underwater archaeological exploration off the Poompuhar coast" (2026)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/torpedo-jar-fragment-ring-wells-found-in-underwater-archaeological-exploration-off-the-poompuhar-coast-in-mayiladuthuraidistrict/article71496643.ece); [Wikipedia, "Poombuhar"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poombuhar).
- **Claimed harbour 30 km offshore** (claimed find, fringe; 11.14°N 79.86°E, give or take 40 km). A paper in Current Science in 2020 reported that depth data (the GEBCO global grid and multibeam sonar) show a harbour and related structures on a drowned delta of the Kaveri about 30 km out to sea, a delta the authors put at 11,000 to 15,000 years old. Against: Nothing has been sampled or dated on the seabed; the shapes come from depth grids alone, and a harbour that old would predate any known port by thousands of years. Sources: [S. M. Ramasamy et al., "Detection of submerged harbour using GEBCO and MBES data, in the offshore region of ancient port city Poompuhar, South India", Current Science 119 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.18520/cs/v119/i3/526-534).

#### Port Royal (found)

Port Royal in Jamaica was one of the richest English ports in the Americas and a base for privateers. On 7 June 1692 an earthquake turned the sand under it to liquid and much of the town slid under the harbour. The sunken streets are the best preserved 17th century English town site anywhere and became a World Heritage Site in 2025. *Sunk 7 June 1692.*

What to look for: Brick buildings sunk upright with their contents in place, under a few metres of silt in the harbour. Found by probing, sonar and diver excavation.

- **Port Royal** (found here, confirmed; 17.94°N 76.84°W, give or take 1 km). The 1692 earthquake put much of the town under water and sand. Excavations since the 1950s have recorded whole buildings and thousands of everyday objects from the moment of the disaster. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "The Archaeological Ensemble of 17th Century Port Royal"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1595/); [Wikipedia, "Port Royal"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Royal).

#### Ravenser Odd (not found yet)

Ravenser Odd was a medieval port on a sandbank at the mouth of the Humber, near Spurn Point. It grew rich in the 13th century, sent two members to the Model Parliament of 1295, and was then abandoned and destroyed by storms, the last in 1362. Its site has never been found. *Founded in the 13th century CE; destroyed by 1362.*

What to look for: Stone footings of a sea wall, quays and buildings on or under the sandy seabed off Spurn, where the sand moves fast. High-resolution multibeam sonar is the tool being used.

- **Seabed off Spurn Point** (search area, plausible; 53.59°N 0.16°E, give or take 5 km). Daniel Parsons, a sedimentologist at the University of Hull, surveyed about 10 hectares of seabed off Spurn Point with high-resolution sonar in 2021. It did not find the town, but he believed the search was close and planned a second survey. Against: No remains have been confirmed, and the sands at the mouth of the Humber have shifted a great deal since the 14th century. Sources: [The Guardian, "Yorkshire's lost 'Atlantis' nearly found, says Hull professor" (2022)](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2022/mar/14/yorkshires-lost-atlantis-nearly-found-says-hull-professor); [Wikipedia, "Ravenser Odd"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenser_Odd).

#### Rungholt (found)

Rungholt was a medieval trading settlement in North Frisia that a storm surge destroyed in January 1362. Legend made it a rich city sunk for its sins, and its site was argued over for a century. In 2023 the Rungholt Project, which includes archaeologists from Kiel University, found its main church and many dwelling mounds under the mudflats near Hallig Südfall. *Destroyed by the storm surge of 1362 CE (the Grote Mandrenke); church located in 2023.*

What to look for: Dwelling mounds, drainage ditches, dykes and tide gates buried under tidal mud. They show in magnetic gradiometry, electromagnetic induction and seismic survey on the flats at low tide, and are checked by coring.

- **Tidal flats at Hallig Südfall** (found here, confirmed; 54.47°N 8.72°E, give or take 3 km). Magnetic and electromagnetic survey of more than ten square km of mudflat, with cores and finds, mapped up to 64 dwelling mounds, a sea dyke, drains and the foundations of a church about 40 m long. The team identifies it as the main church of the Edomsharde district and its centre, Rungholt. Sources: [D. Wilken, H. Hadler et al., "The discovery of the church of Rungholt", Scientific Reports 14 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-66245-0); [D. Wilken, H. Hadler et al., "Lost in the North Sea: Geophysical and geoarchaeological prospection of the Rungholt medieval dyke system", PLOS ONE 17 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0265463).

#### Saeftinghe (found)

Saeftinghe was a district of farms, villages and a castle on the south bank of the Western Scheldt. Floods in the 14th and 16th centuries drowned it, and the dykes cut during the Dutch Revolt in 1584 finished it. It is now a 3,500 hectare tidal marsh on the Dutch and Belgian border, with the lost villages somewhere under the mud. *Flooded in the 14th and 16th centuries CE; lost in 1584.*

What to look for: Brick foundations, dyke lines and field ditches under tidal mud and creeks. Electromagnetic and electrical surveys on the flats at low tide have been tried here; lidar shows the creek pattern.

- **Verdronken Land van Saeftinghe** (found here, confirmed; 51.35°N 4.17°E, give or take 5 km). The body that manages the marsh records that villages and a castle stood here in the Middle Ages and that floods returned the land to the tides. Geophysicists have tested several survey methods on the flats to look for what is buried. Sources: [Stichting Het Zeeuwse Landschap, "Verdronken Land van Saeftinghe" (in Dutch)](https://www.hetzeeuwselandschap.nl/natuurgebieden/verdronken-land-van-saeftinghe); [T. Missiaen et al., "Comparing Different Geophysical Methods in a Tidal Flat Area (Verdronken Land van Saeftinge, Westerschelde)", Near Surface 2006 (EAGE)](https://doi.org/10.3997/2214-4609.201402758).

#### Seven Pagodas of Mahabalipuram (disputed)

European sailors called Mahabalipuram, on the coast of Tamil Nadu, the Seven Pagodas, and local tradition says six temples besides the Shore Temple lie under the sea. Divers reported man-made stonework offshore in 2002, and the tsunami of December 2004 stripped sand from buried temple remains on the beach. The offshore structures have not been dated, so how much of the legend is true is still open. *Shore Temple built in the 8th century CE; offshore finds reported 2002 and 2005.*

What to look for: Cut granite blocks, wall lines and steps on the seabed within a kilometre of the shore, seen by divers and side-scan sonar, and brick or stone temple bases under beach sand. Natural granite outcrops here split into block shapes too, so each feature needs a diver.

- **Seabed off the Shore Temple** (claimed find, plausible; 12.62°N 80.20°E, give or take 2 km). In April 2002 divers from India's National Institute of Oceanography and the British Scientific Exploration Society reported ruins spread over a wide area of seabed off Mahabalipuram. After the 2004 tsunami the Archaeological Survey of India and the navy found more remains close to shore. Against: The structures are not dated and some may be natural rock; Graham Hancock, who was on the 2002 team, used them to argue for a far older lost civilisation, which archaeologists do not accept. Sources: [BBC News, "Lost city found off Indian coast" (2002)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/south_asia/1923794.stm); [Wikipedia, "Seven Pagodas of Mahabalipuram"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Pagodas_of_Mahabalipuram).
- **Temple remains uncovered by the 2004 tsunami** (found here, confirmed; 12.62°N 80.20°E, give or take 2 km). After the tsunami the Archaeological Survey of India dug on the beach and found the remains of a brick temple under a later Pallava one, with layers of shells and debris showing that earlier tsunamis had twice flattened the complex. Sources: [BBC News, "Tsunami reveals ancient temple sites" (2005)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/south_asia/4312024.stm).

#### Shicheng (Lion City) (found)

Shicheng was a county town in Zhejiang, China. In 1959 the government flooded its valley to make the reservoir of the Xin'an River dam, now Qiandao Lake, and the town was forgotten. Divers found it again in 2001, with its Ming and Qing walls, gates and carved arches standing whole about 40 m down. *Flooded 1959; explored by divers from 2001.*

What to look for: A whole walled town standing upright in fresh water: city walls, five gates, streets and timber buildings, clear in sonar of the lake bed and to divers.

- **Shicheng, Qiandao Lake** (found here, confirmed; 29.60°N 118.98°E, give or take 5 km). The town lies about 40 m under the lake that formed when the valley was dammed in 1959. Cold, still fresh water has kept its Ming and Qing dynasty stonework and woodwork in good order. Sources: [BBC Travel, "China's Atlantis of the East" (2014)](https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20140711-chinas-atlantis-of-the-east); [Wikipedia, "Shicheng, Zhejiang"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shicheng,_Zhejiang).

#### Sundaland (found)

Sundaland is the shelf that joined Sumatra, Java, Borneo and the Malay Peninsula into one landmass when Ice Age seas were low. It flooded as the ice melted, in steps that sediment cores from the shelf record in detail. It is a real drowned land; claims that it was Atlantis have no support. *Exposed during the last glacial period; flooded as the ice melted, from about 21,000 years ago.*

What to look for: Drowned river valleys running across the shelf between the islands, visible in bathymetry and seismic data. Mangrove peat and shoreline deposits in cores date each stage of the flooding.

- **Sunda Shelf** (found here, confirmed; 0.00°N 110.00°E, give or take 800 km). Cores from the shelf give a sea-level record from 21,000 to 14,000 years ago. They show the sea rising as much as 16 m in 300 years about 14,600 years ago, which drowned wide lowlands between the islands. Sources: [T. Hanebuth et al., "Rapid Flooding of the Sunda Shelf: A Late-Glacial Sea-Level Record", Science 288 (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.288.5468.1033); [Wikipedia, "Sundaland"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland).

#### Thonis-Heracleion (found)

Thonis-Heracleion was Egypt's main port on the Mediterranean before Alexandria was founded. It stood at the Canopic mouth of the Nile and sank in stages, the last of it in the 8th century CE. It was known only from texts until Franck Goddio's team found it in 2000 under the Bay of Abu Qir, 6.5 km from the present shore. *Founded about the 8th century BCE; sunk by the 8th century CE; found 2000.*

What to look for: The city lies under metres of sand and silt in murky water, so it was found with towed magnetometers, side-scan sonar and sub-bottom profilers, then checked by divers. Temple blocks, statues, quays and dozens of ship hulls mark the old channels.

- **Thonis-Heracleion (Bay of Abu Qir)** (found here, confirmed; 31.30°N 30.10°E, give or take 5 km). The European Institute for Underwater Archaeology (IEASM), led by Franck Goddio, located the city in 2000 after a geophysical survey of the bay. A stele found on the site showed that Greek Heracleion and Egyptian Thonis were one and the same town. Sources: [Franck Goddio / IEASM, "Thonis-Heracleion"](https://www.franckgoddio.org/projects/sunken-civilizations/heracleion/); [The Guardian, "Lost cities #6: how Thonis-Heracleion resurfaced after 1,000 years under water" (2016)](https://www.theguardian.com/cities/2016/aug/15/lost-cities-6-thonis-heracleion-egypt-sunken-sea).

#### Thule (not found yet)

The Greek explorer Pytheas of Massalia reported about 325 BCE a land called Thule, six days' sail north of Britain, near a frozen sea, where the summer sun hardly set. His book is lost and we have only quotations by later writers such as Strabo, who thought him a liar. Thule was a real report of a far northern land, but scholars still disagree on which one. *Voyage of Pytheas about 325 BCE.*

What to look for: This is a question of ancient texts and latitudes, not of remains: no site would prove it. Greek or Massaliote objects of the 4th century BCE in the far north would help, and none has been found.

- **Smøla and the coast of central Norway** (proposed, plausible; 63.36°N 8.03°E, give or take 300 km). Fridtjof Nansen argued in 1911 that Thule was the coast of Norway, where Pytheas could have met farmers who threshed grain indoors and made mead. In 2010 a team at the Technical University of Berlin corrected the distortions in Ptolemy's map and placed his Thule at the island of Smøla. Against: Pytheas called Thule an island, and a six day crossing from Britain fits Iceland as well. Sources: [F. Nansen, In Northern Mists, vol. 1 (1911), ch. 2, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/40633/40633-h/40633-h.htm); [Review of A. Kleineberg et al., Germania und die Insel Thule (2010), Isis 103 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1086/667485).
- **Iceland** (proposed, plausible; 65.00°N 19.00°W, give or take 250 km). Iceland lies about six days' sail north-west of Britain, close to the Arctic Circle and to drift ice, as Pytheas described. The Irish monk Dicuil, writing about 825, described clerics who had stayed on Thule and seen the midnight sun, and this is usually taken to be Iceland. Against: Pytheas spoke of people who farmed and kept bees, and Iceland had no known inhabitants before the 9th century CE. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Ultima Thule"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ultima-Thule); [Wikipedia, "Thule"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule).
- **Shetland** (proposed, plausible; 60.36°N 1.26°W, give or take 60 km). Tacitus says that the Roman fleet that sailed round Britain under Agricola conquered the Orkneys and sighted Thule, which can only be Shetland or Fair Isle. Some scholars think this is also the Thule of Pytheas. Against: Shetland is one day north of Orkney, not six days from Britain, and has no midnight sun. Sources: [Tacitus, Agricola 10 ("Thule was sighted")](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0081%3Achapter%3D10); [Wikipedia, "Thule"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule).

#### Vineta (legend)

North German and Polish legend tells of Vineta, a rich trading city on the Baltic that sank under the sea as a punishment for pride. Chroniclers of the 11th and 12th centuries describe a real great port on the south Baltic coast, called Jumne among other names. Most historians identify that port with the Viking Age town at Wolin; the sinking is legend. *Port described by Adam of Bremen about 1075; said to have been destroyed in the 12th century.*

What to look for: A Viking Age port leaves harbour timbers, ramparts, craft debris and coin hoards in waterlogged ground, found by coring, geophysics and excavation. Old river channels in lidar and historic maps show where the coast lay.

- **Wolin** (possible inspiration, leading view; 53.84°N 14.61°E, give or take 3 km). Adolf Hofmeister argued in 1931 and 1932 that Vineta, Jumne, Julin and Jomsborg are all names for the trading town at Wolin, at a mouth of the Oder. Excavations from the 1930s on uncovered a large early medieval settlement there, and his thesis is the mainstream view. Against: Wolin never sank; the chronicles report Danish campaigns against it, the last in 1170. Sources: [C. Broich, "The Wasting of Wolin: Environmental Factors in the Downfall of a Medieval Baltic Town", Environment and History 7 (2001)](https://doi.org/10.3197/096734001129342450); [Wikipedia, "Vineta"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineta).
- **Barth** (proposed, fringe; 54.37°N 12.73°E, give or take 10 km). Klaus Goldmann, a Berlin museum curator, and Günter Wermusch argued in 1999 that the Oder once had a western mouth and that Vineta lay in the lagoon near Barth. Against: No excavation has found a town there and most historians keep to Wolin. Sources: [Archaeology, "Letter from Germany: Atlantis of the Baltic" (2003)](https://archive.archaeology.org/0307/abstracts/letter.html); [wissenschaft.de, review of K. Goldmann and G. Wermusch, Vineta (1999)](https://wissenschaft.de/artikel/traeume-in-schlick-vineta-von-klaus-goldmann-guenter-wermusch).

#### Yonaguni Monument (disputed)

A terraced rock formation rises from about 25 m of water off Yonaguni, in the far south-west of Japan. Kihachiro Aratake, a local dive guide looking for sharks, found it in 1986. The marine geologist Masaaki Kimura says people carved it thousands of years ago; most geologists who have looked at it say the steps and flat faces are natural fractures in bedded sandstone. *Found in 1986.*

What to look for: Flat terraces and right angles in sandstone that splits along straight joints, seen by divers and in multibeam sonar. Tool marks, quarried blocks moved out of place or artefacts would show human work; natural joints explain the rest.

- **Yonaguni Monument** (claimed find, fringe; 24.44°N 123.01°E, give or take 1 km). Masaaki Kimura of the University of the Ryukyus has dived the site for years and reads it as a stepped monument with roads, a castle and carved faces, possibly sunk by an earthquake. Against: Robert Schoch of Boston University and others find that the sandstone breaks naturally along straight parallel joints, and neither Japan's cultural agency nor Okinawa Prefecture treats it as a cultural site. Sources: [National Geographic, "Japan's Ancient Underwater 'Pyramid' Mystifies Scholars" (2007)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/news/2007/9/yonaguni-jima-japan-underwater-city/); [Wikipedia, "Yonaguni Monument"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonaguni_Monument).

#### Ys (legend)

Breton legend tells of the city of Ys, built below sea level behind a dyke by King Gradlon. His daughter Dahut opened the sluice gates and the sea drowned it. The story is known from the late Middle Ages on and has no archaeological basis. *Set in the 5th century CE; written versions from the 15th century on.*

What to look for: A real town would leave walls, quays or a dyke on the floor of a shallow bay, within reach of multibeam sonar. None has been found in the Bay of Douarnenez.

- **Bay of Douarnenez** (proposed, fringe; 48.17°N 4.43°W, give or take 15 km). Most versions of the legend place the drowned city in this bay in Finistère, and local tradition points to old walls and roads near the shore as its remains. Against: No trace of a city has been found on the seabed, and the story took shape only between the 15th and 17th centuries. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Ys"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ys); [L. Spence, Legends and Romances of Brittany (1917), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30871/30871-h/30871-h.htm).

#### Zakynthos "lost city" (legend)

Snorkellers off the Greek island of Zakynthos reported what looked like paved floors and column bases on the seabed, and it was first taken for a lost harbour city. A study in 2016 showed they are natural: minerals that hardened around methane seeps in the sediment up to 5 million years ago. It is a useful example of how regular a natural rock can look. *Explained as natural in 2016.*

What to look for: Ring-shaped and slab-shaped stone on a shallow seabed with no pottery, no tool marks and no mortar around it. Mineral and isotope tests show whether stone grew in place.

- **Alikanas Bay, Zakynthos** (claimed find, rejected; 37.80°N 20.75°E, give or take 15 km). Tourists and then Greek divers took the discs and slabs in 2 to 5 m of water for the remains of an ancient harbour city. Against: Geologists from the University of East Anglia and Athens found the "columns" are dolomite concretions formed by microbes around old methane seeps, and there is no pottery or other trace of people at the site. Sources: [J. Andrews et al., "Exhumed hydrocarbon-seep authigenic carbonates from Zakynthos Island (Greece): Concretions not archaeological remains", Marine and Petroleum Geology 76 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpetgeo.2016.05.022); [University of East Anglia news release, "Underwater 'lost city' found to be geological formation" (EurekAlert, 2016)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/831103).

### Lost cities

#### Akkad (Agade) (not found yet)

Akkad was the capital that Sargon built about 2300 BCE for the first empire in Mesopotamia. Its name is on thousands of tablets and it gave its name to the Akkadian language, but the city itself has never been found. Texts place it near the Tigris and the Diyala, within about 30 km of modern Baghdad. *Capital of Sargon (reigned about 2334 to 2279 BCE) and his successors.*

What to look for: A large tell with Akkadian period brick and inscriptions, perhaps buried under river silt or under greater Baghdad. Old river courses traced in declassified CORONA images and radar elevation data narrow down where a capital could have stood.

- **Tigris and Diyala confluence (Tell Muhammad)** (search area, plausible; 33.30°N 44.47°E, give or take 30 km). Texts put Akkad near Eshnunna and Sippar, not far from Kish, near the Tigris and not far from the Diyala. Tell Muhammad, in the south-eastern suburbs of Baghdad near the confluence, is one of the mounds proposed. Against: No inscription from any candidate site names it as Akkad. Sources: [B. Foster, "Akkad (Agade)", The Encyclopedia of Ancient History (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah01005); [H. Weiss, "Kish, Akkad and Agade", Journal of the American Oriental Society 95 (1975)](https://doi.org/10.2307/599355).

#### Avaris (found)

Avaris was the capital of the Hyksos, kings of Levantine origin who ruled northern Egypt about 1650 to 1550 BCE until the Theban pharaoh Ahmose drove them out. Egyptian texts name it but its place was forgotten. Austrian excavations at Tell el-Dab'a, led by Manfred Bietak from 1966, proved the site and found wall paintings like those of Minoan Crete. *Hyksos capital about 1650 to 1550 BCE; excavated from 1966 CE.*

What to look for: Low mounds in delta farmland, with houses and tombs of Levantine type under them. Magnetic survey maps the mud-brick town and its harbour basins; old Nile branches show in satellite images and cores.

- **Tell el-Dab'a** (found here, confirmed; 30.79°N 31.82°E, give or take 3 km). Excavation showed a large town of the Middle Bronze Age with Levantine houses, temples and burials, and above it a palace district of the early 18th Dynasty. Sources: [Review of M. Bietak, Avaris, the Capital of the Hyksos: Recent Excavations at Tell el-Dab'a (1998)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3822227); [Wikipedia, "Avaris"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avaris).

#### Aztlán (legend)

Aztlán is the island homeland that the Mexica, the Aztecs, said they left to wander south and found Tenochtitlan. It is known only from their own histories, written down after the Spanish conquest. Scholars disagree on whether it was a real place in the north-west or a mythical mirror of Tenochtitlan itself. *Migration set in the 11th or 12th century CE; recorded in the 16th century.*

What to look for: The histories describe an island town in a lagoon with reeds and herons. A real candidate would need a settlement of the right date with Aztatlán or early Mexica pottery; none has been tied to the story.

- **Mexcaltitán** (proposed, fringe; 21.91°N 105.47°W, give or take 5 km). From the 1960s some Mexican scholars, Wigberto Jiménez Moreno among them, proposed that this small island town in a lagoon in Nayarit was Aztlán, because of its shape and setting. Against: No excavation supports it, and many scholars think it impossible to fix a mythical homeland on the map. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Aztlán"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztl%C3%A1n); [The Codex Mexicanus (2018), ch. 5, "A History of the Mexica People: From Aztlan to Tenochtitlan to New Spain"](https://doi.org/10.7560/316733-007).

#### Bosnian "pyramids" (hoax)

In October 2005 the businessman Semir Osmanagić announced that Visočica hill and other hills near Visoko in Bosnia are the largest and oldest pyramids on Earth. Geologists say they are natural: tilted layers of rock that erode into flat triangular faces. The European Association of Archaeologists called the project a cruel hoax on the public, and warned that the digging was damaging real medieval and Roman remains. *Claim made in 2005 CE.*

What to look for: A hill with flat, triangular faces is a common landform called a flatiron, made where hard rock layers dip at an angle. The faces follow the bedding of the rock, and cut blocks, mortar and tools are absent.

- **Visočica hill, Visoko** (claimed find, hoax; 43.98°N 18.18°E, give or take 2 km). Osmanagić calls the 220 m hill the Pyramid of the Sun and says man-made blocks lie under its soil. More than 400,000 people visited in the first years after his announcement in October 2005. Against: Geologists identify the hill as a natural flatiron of layered rock; Anthony Harding, then president of the European Association of Archaeologists, and other scholars condemned the project. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "The Mystery of Bosnia's Ancient Pyramids" (2009)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-mystery-of-bosnias-ancient-pyramids-148990462/); [Wikipedia, "Bosnian pyramid claims"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_pyramid_claims).

#### Cahokia (found)

Cahokia, across the Mississippi from modern St Louis, was the largest settlement north of Mexico before Europeans came. At its height between 1050 and 1150 CE it held 10,000 to 20,000 people and about 120 earthen mounds. It was abandoned by about 1400; its builders left no writing and its original name is unknown. *Occupied about 800 to 1400 CE; peak 1050 to 1150.*

What to look for: Flat-topped and conical earth mounds around levelled plazas, borrow pits and the post holes of palisades and timber circles. Lidar shows mounds that ploughing has nearly flattened; magnetometry finds houses and pits.

- **Cahokia Mounds** (found here, confirmed; 38.65°N 90.06°W, give or take 2 km). The site covered nearly 1,600 hectares. Monks Mound, its centre, is the largest prehistoric earthwork in the Americas: it covers over 5 hectares and stands 30 m high. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/198).

#### Camelot (legend)

Camelot, the court of King Arthur, first appears in passing in a French romance by Chrétien de Troyes in the 1170s, six hundred years after the time Arthur is supposed to have lived. Earlier Welsh and Latin sources give Arthur other courts. Most scholars regard Camelot as a literary invention, and no site can be shown to be it. *First named in the 1170s CE; Arthur is set about 500 CE.*

What to look for: What can be tested is whether a given hillfort or Roman town was a seat of power about 500 CE: rebuilt ramparts, a large timber hall, and wine jars and tableware imported from the Mediterranean.

- **Cadbury Castle, Somerset** (proposed, plausible; 51.02°N 2.53°W, give or take 1 km). John Leland wrote in 1542 that local people held this Iron Age hillfort to be Camelot. Leslie Alcock's excavation of 1966 to 1970 showed that it was refortified on a grand scale after the Romans left, with a great hall and pottery from the eastern Mediterranean: the stronghold of a major British ruler. Against: It proves a powerful lord of the right date, not Arthur, and the name Camelot is later fiction. Sources: [L. Alcock, "Excavations at Cadbury-Camelot, 1966-70", Antiquity 46 (1972)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00053114); [Wikipedia, "Camelot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelot).
- **Caerleon** (possible inspiration, plausible; 51.61°N 2.96°W, give or take 2 km). Geoffrey of Monmouth, writing about 1136, set Arthur's court at Caerleon, where the ruins of a Roman legionary fortress and amphitheatre still stood. It is Arthur's court in the Welsh tradition and may lie behind the later Camelot. Against: The ruins are Roman military buildings of the 1st to 3rd centuries CE. Sources: [Geoffrey of Monmouth, History of the Kings of Britain, book 9 (trans. A. Thompson and J. A. Giles), Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Six_Old_English_Chronicles/Geoffrey%27s_British_History/Book_9); [Wikipedia, "Camelot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelot).
- **Winchester** (proposed, rejected; 51.06°N 1.31°W, give or take 3 km). Thomas Malory, in the 15th century, said firmly that Camelot was Winchester, where a great Round Table hangs in the castle hall. Against: Tree-ring dating shows the table is medieval, far later than Arthur, and its paintwork was done for Henry VIII; Winchester has no claim older than the romances. Sources: [T. Malory, Le Morte d'Arthur, vol. 1 (1485), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1251/1251-h/1251-h.htm); [Wikipedia, "Camelot"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelot).

#### Capital of the Mali Empire (Niani) (disputed)

The Mali Empire of the 13th to 15th centuries was one of the richest states of its age; its capital was a great centre of the trade in gold and salt under Mansa Musa (reigned 1307 to 1332). The traveller Ibn Battuta stayed in the capital in the 14th century, but his account does not make clear where it was. Excavations in the 1960s at the village of Niani in Guinea were taken to have found it, but the dating and the texts leave room for doubt. *Capital in the 13th and 14th centuries CE.*

What to look for: A large settlement of the 13th and 14th centuries with a royal quarter and a separate Muslim merchants' quarter, a mosque of fired or mud brick, and North African imports such as glazed pottery, glass and copper.

- **Niani, Guinea** (proposed, plausible; 11.38°N 8.38°W, give or take 5 km). Niani, on the Sankarani river, is named in tradition as the capital founded by Sundiata Keita. Excavations there in the mid 1960s found a medieval settlement, which was taken to confirm it. Against: J. O. Hunwick argued from Ibn Battuta's route that the 14th century capital lay elsewhere, further down the Niger, and later scholars have questioned the dates from Niani. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Niani"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Niani); [J. O. Hunwick, "The mid-fourteenth century capital of Mali", The Journal of African History 14 (1973)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0021853700012512).

#### City of the Caesars (legend)

Colonial Chile and Argentina believed in a rich city somewhere in Patagonia, founded by shipwrecked Spaniards or by refugees from the Inca empire, Reports of it went round for two hundred years, and the Jesuit Nicolás Mascardi spent the years 1666 to 1673 looking for it from Chiloé and Nahuel Huapi. No evidence shows that it ever existed. *Reports from the 1520s CE; searches until the late 1700s.*

What to look for: Nothing has been found. The real traces behind the story would be small: camps of shipwreck survivors on the Strait of Magellan and short-lived Spanish forts.

- **Nahuel Huapi Lake** (search area, fringe; 41.09°S 71.34°W, give or take 150 km). Mascardi crossed the Andes from Chiloé and set up a mission on the shore of this lake, from which he and other Jesuits made journeys across Patagonia in search of the city. Against: Two centuries of searching found nothing. Sources: [R. Urbina, "La creencia en la ciudad de los Césares desde Chiloé en tiempos del jesuita Nicolás Mascardi, 1666-1673", Magallania 48 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.4067/s0718-22442020000100005); [Wikipedia, "Nicolás Mascardi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Mascardi).

#### Ciudad Blanca (White City) (legend)

Stories of a White City in the rainforest of La Mosquitia, in eastern Honduras, go back to the conquistadors and grew in the 20th century through explorers' tales. An airborne lidar survey in 2012 found real, unrecorded settlements in a remote valley there, and a ground team visited one in 2015. Archaeologists accept the sites but object to calling them the White City: the region holds many such sites, and no single legendary city. *Legend from the 16th century CE; lidar survey 2012; ground visit 2015.*

What to look for: Earthen mounds set around plazas, terraces, canals and roads under high rainforest canopy, visible only in lidar ground models. Stone sculpture lies on the surface at some sites.

- **Valley "T1" in La Mosquitia (location withheld)** (found here, confirmed; 15.25°N 84.73°W, give or take 80 km). A lidar survey flown in 2012 for a project led by the film maker Steve Elkins mapped plazas, mounds and terraces in remote valleys, and Christopher Fisher's team published one, called T1, in 2016. What is confirmed is a group of ancient settlements, not the city of the legend; the pin is the biosphere reserve because the team does not publish coordinates. Sources: [C. Fisher et al., "Identifying Ancient Settlement Patterns through LiDAR in the Mosquitia Region of Honduras", PLOS ONE 11 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0159890); [The Guardian, "Archaeologists condemn National Geographic over claims of Honduran 'lost cities'" (2015)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/mar/11/honduras-lost-cities-open-letter-national-geographic-report).

#### Ciudad Perdida (Teyuna) (found)

Ciudad Perdida is a Tairona town of stone terraces and stairways in the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta in Colombia. Looters hunting for gold found it in 1972, and its objects on the black market led archaeologists to it; it was revealed to the world in 1975. The indigenous peoples of the Sierra say they always knew it, and call it Teyuna. *Tairona town; looted from 1972; announced 1975.*

What to look for: Round stone terraces, paved paths and long stairways on steep forested ridges. Under canopy they show in lidar; from the ground they are a day or more on foot from any road.

- **Ciudad Perdida (Teyuna)** (found here, confirmed; 11.04°N 73.93°W, give or take 2 km). A gang of looters reached the terraces in 1972. Colombian archaeologists followed the trail of looted pieces, and the site was made public in 1975 as Ciudad Perdida. Sources: [Archaeology, "Lost City" (2004)](https://archive.archaeology.org/0409/abstracts/colombia.html); [Wikipedia, "Ciudad Perdida"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Perdida).

#### Dilmun (found)

Sumerian texts speak of Dilmun as a trading partner across the sea and, in myth, as a pure land without sickness or death, the home of the flood hero. It was a real place: the trading kingdom of the Gulf, centred on the island of Bahrain. A Danish expedition that dug from 1954 to 1972 found its capital at Qal'at al-Bahrain. *Named from about 3000 BCE; capital occupied from about 2300 BCE.*

What to look for: A tell beside an old harbour, with stamp seals, weights and pottery that tie it to both Mesopotamia and the Indus. Tens of thousands of burial mounds across the north of Bahrain mark the kingdom in air photos.

- **Qal'at al-Bahrain** (found here, confirmed; 26.23°N 50.52°E, give or take 1 km). The mound is a tell built up by continuous occupation from about 2300 BCE to the 16th century CE. It was the capital of Dilmun. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Qal'at al-Bahrain: Ancient Harbour and Capital of Dilmun"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1192).

#### El Dorado (legend)

El Dorado, "the gilded one", began as a Spanish report of a Muisca ceremony in the highlands of Colombia: a new chief, covered in gold dust, rode a raft on to Lake Guatavita and threw gold into the water. Within decades the man had become a golden city, and explorers looked for it across the Amazon and the Guianas for two centuries. The ceremony was real; the city never existed. *Ceremony reported in the 1530s CE; searches from 1541 into the 1700s.*

What to look for: The real trace is small gold offerings in highland lakes and caves, found by divers, by draining and by chance. The lake that mapmakers called Parime was most likely seasonal flooding of savannah, which shows in radar and optical satellite images of wet and dry seasons.

- **Lake Guatavita** (where it began, leading view; 4.98°N 73.78°W, give or take 1 km). This crater lake north-east of Bogotá is where chroniclers set the raft ceremony of the Muisca. Gold and silver offerings have come out of it, though every attempt to drain it since the 16th century has recovered little. Against: It is the source of the story, not a city or a hoard. Sources: [BBC News, "El Dorado: The truth behind the myth" (J. Cooper, 2013)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/magazine-20964114); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Eldorado"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Eldorado-legendary-country).
- **Pasca (find spot of the Muisca raft)** (found here, confirmed; 4.31°N 74.30°W, give or take 5 km). In 1969 three villagers found a gold model of a raft carrying a chief and attendants in a small cave in the hills south of Bogotá. It shows the scene the chroniclers described and is now in the Gold Museum in Bogotá. Sources: [BBC News, "El Dorado: The truth behind the myth" (2013)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/magazine-20964114); [Wikipedia, "Muisca raft"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_raft).
- **Lake Parime and Manoa (Guiana)** (proposed, rejected; 2.75°N 59.75°W, give or take 150 km). Walter Raleigh went up the Orinoco in 1595 to find Manoa, a golden city said to stand on a great lake called Parime. Maps showed the lake for two hundred years. Against: Later explorers, Alexander von Humboldt among them, showed there is no such lake; it was probably the seasonal flooding of the Rupununi savannah. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Eldorado"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Eldorado-legendary-country); [Wikipedia, "Lake Parime"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Parime).

#### Great Zimbabwe (found)

Great Zimbabwe is the largest ancient stone building complex in Africa south of the Sahara, the capital of a Shona state that traded gold to the Indian Ocean coast. It was built from the 11th century CE and abandoned in the 15th. European settlers long refused to believe that Africans had built it and gave it to Phoenicians or the Queen of Sheba; excavation in 1929 settled the matter. *Built 11th to 15th centuries CE.*

What to look for: Curving dry-stone walls up to several metres thick on a granite hill and in the valley below, with the remains of clay houses between them. The wider town shows as low platforms and middens in lidar and air photos.

- **Great Zimbabwe** (found here, confirmed; 20.27°S 30.93°E, give or take 2 km). Gertrude Caton Thompson's excavation in 1929 confirmed that the builders were African, and later work dates the main building to between the 11th and 15th centuries CE. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Great Zimbabwe National Monument"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364); [S. Chirikure and I. Pikirayi, "Inside and outside the dry stone walls: revisiting the material culture of Great Zimbabwe", Antiquity 82 (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00097726).

#### Gunung Padang "pyramid" (disputed)

Gunung Padang in West Java is a real megalithic site: terraces built of natural basalt columns on top of an old volcano, most likely within the last few thousand years. In 2023 a team led by the geologist Danny Hilman Natawidjaja published a paper claiming that the hill hides a man-made pyramid up to 27,000 years old. Archaeologists objected, the journal investigated, and the paper was retracted in March 2024. *Terraces of the last few thousand years; pyramid claim published 2023 and retracted 2024.*

What to look for: Radar, seismic and resistivity images show layers inside the hill, but layers alone do not show building. The claim failed because the dated soil samples held no artefacts or other sign of human work.

- **Gunung Padang, West Java** (claimed find, rejected; 6.99°S 107.06°E, give or take 1 km). The 2023 paper used ground-penetrating radar, seismic tomography and drill cores to argue that the hill was sculpted and built up by people in stages starting in the last Ice Age. Against: Wiley and the journal's editors retracted the paper in March 2024 because the radiocarbon dates came from soil with no artefacts or man-made features; the megalithic terraces on the summit are real and much younger. Sources: [D. H. Natawidjaja et al., "RETRACTED: Geo-archaeological prospecting of Gunung Padang buried prehistoric pyramid in West Java, Indonesia", Archaeological Prospection (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.1912); [Nature News, "A 27,000-year-old pyramid? Controversy hits an extraordinary archaeological claim" (2023)](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-03546-w).

#### Hanging Gardens of Babylon (legend)

Greek and Roman writers describe a terraced garden at Babylon, watered by machines, and count it among the Seven Wonders. It is the only one whose place has never been found: no Babylonian text mentions it and no remains have been identified in the ruins. Some think it a fiction; Stephanie Dalley argues it was real but stood at Nineveh, built by the Assyrian king Sennacherib. *Attributed to Nebuchadnezzar II (reigned 605 to 562 BCE); Sennacherib reigned 705 to 681 BCE.*

What to look for: Vaulted terraces with waterproofing and a water supply raised from a river or brought by canal. At Nineveh the aqueduct at Jerwan and the canal system are real and mapped; the garden itself is not.

- **Babylon** (proposed, plausible; 32.54°N 44.42°E, give or take 3 km). The classical writers, drawing on the Babylonian priest Berossus, place the gardens at Babylon and credit them to Nebuchadnezzar II. Against: No surviving Babylonian text mentions them and no definite archaeological evidence has been found at Babylon. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Babylon"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/278); [Wikipedia, "Hanging Gardens of Babylon"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_Gardens_of_Babylon).
- **Nineveh** (proposed, plausible; 36.37°N 43.15°E, give or take 3 km). Stephanie Dalley of Oxford argues that the gardens were the palace garden of Sennacherib at Nineveh, which his own inscriptions describe along with the canals and aqueduct that fed it, and that later writers confused the two cities. Against: The classical sources all say Babylon, and the garden at Nineveh has not been excavated. Sources: [S. Dalley, "Nineveh, Babylon and the Hanging Gardens: Cuneiform and Classical Sources Reconciled", Iraq 56 (1994)](https://doi.org/10.2307/4200384); [The Guardian, "Babylon's hanging garden: ancient scripts give clue to missing wonder" (2013)](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2013/may/05/babylon-hanging-garden-wonder-nineveh).

#### Hawaiki (legend)

Hawaiki is the homeland from which, in the traditions of the Māori and other Polynesian peoples, their ancestors set sail, and to which the spirits of the dead return. It is a mythical place and not one island: the name moved with the voyagers. But it has real roots, since Havai'i is the old name of Ra'iātea in the Society Islands, a centre from which eastern Polynesia was settled. *Traditions of the settlement of East Polynesia, about 1000 to 1300 CE.*

What to look for: The evidence is in language, genetics and the dating of first settlement on each island group, not in ruins. Temple platforms (marae) share forms and names across thousands of kilometres of ocean.

- **Ra'iātea and Taputapuātea** (possible inspiration, plausible; 16.84°S 151.36°W, give or take 20 km). Havai'i is the old name for Ra'iātea, and the marae of Taputapuātea on its coast was a religious centre from which stones were carried to found marae of the same name on other islands. UNESCO describes it as the ancestral homeland of Polynesian culture. Against: Other island groups have their own Hawaiki; the name also survives in Savai'i in Samoa and in Hawai'i. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Taputapuātea"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1529); [Wikipedia, "Hawaiki"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiki).

#### Iram of the Pillars (Ubar) (disputed)

The Quran speaks of Iram of the Pillars, a city of the people of Ad that God destroyed, and Arab tradition tells of a lost trading town called Ubar or Wabar in the sands of southern Arabia. In 1992 a team that had studied space shuttle radar and satellite images dug at Shisr in Oman and found an old fort and caravan station on the frankincense route. The team called it Ubar; scholars accept the site but mostly not the name. *Quranic story of the 7th century CE; NASA gives the site a life from about 2800 BCE to 300 CE; found 1992.*

What to look for: Old caravan tracks worn into the desert floor, which converge on water and show in radar and multispectral satellite images even where dunes have since crossed them. The site itself is too small to see from orbit; the tracks that lead to it are not.

- **Shisr (Wubar), Oman** (claimed find, plausible; 18.40°N 53.60°E, give or take 30 km). Space shuttle radar and other satellite images showed old tracks, some running under later dunes, converging near the well of Shisr. Excavation there from 1992 uncovered a fortress on the frankincense route, which NASA and the expedition announced as the lost city of Ubar. Against: The identification of Ubar with Shisr, and of either with Quranic Iram, is not generally accepted: Ubar in the old sources may be a region or a people, and Shisr is a small fort. Sources: [NASA/JPL Photojournal, "Space Radar Image of the Lost City of Ubar" (PIA01721)](https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA01721); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Land of Frankincense"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1010).

#### Itjtawy (not found yet)

Itjtawy was the capital founded by Amenemhat I, who ruled from about 1991 to 1962 BCE, and the seat of the kings of the 12th Dynasty. The pyramids of its first kings stand at Lisht, so the city must be near, under the fields of the Nile flood plain. It has never been excavated; satellite mapping and coring have narrowed down where it lies. *Founded in the 20th century BCE; capital during the Middle Kingdom.*

What to look for: A buried town under several metres of Nile silt. NASA elevation data shows where the river ran and where a slight rise marks old settlement; cores then bring up pottery and worked stone from the right period.

- **Flood plain east of Lisht** (search area, plausible; 29.57°N 31.23°E, give or take 10 km). Sarah Parcak's team used NASA topography data to map the flood plain and work out where the Nile once flowed, then cored a likely spot with Egyptian colleagues and found a layer of pottery under thick mud. The cemetery at Lisht, where a joint mission mapped 802 tombs, belongs to the city. Against: A layer of pottery in a core is not a city plan; no buildings of Itjtawy have been uncovered. Sources: [TED Ideas, "Gallery: Archaeological mysteries hidden in satellite images" (2016)](https://ideas.ted.com/gallery-archaeological-mysteries-hidden-in-satellite-images/); [National Geographic, "More Than 800 Ancient Egyptian Tombs Mapped in Lisht Necropolis" (2018)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/news-800-egyptian-tombs-lisht-middle-kingdom-parcak-archaeology).

#### Kapilavastu (disputed)

Kapilavastu was the capital of the Shakya clan, where Prince Siddhartha, the future Buddha, grew up. It never became a great pilgrim centre like nearby Lumbini, and its place was forgotten. Two sites on either side of the border of Nepal and India now claim to be it: Tilaurakot in Nepal and Piprahwa in India. *City of the 6th and 5th centuries BCE.*

What to look for: A walled and moated town of the mid first millennium BCE with a palace compound, and monastery remains with inscriptions naming the city. Geophysical survey at Tilaurakot has mapped a planned street grid under the grass.

- **Tilaurakot, Nepal** (proposed, plausible; 27.58°N 83.08°E, give or take 2 km). Nepal identifies this fortified town, with brick walls, a moat and gateways, as the Shakya capital and has put it on its World Heritage tentative list. Against: No inscription found there names Kapilavastu. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Tentative List, "Tilaurakot, the archaeological remains of ancient Shakya Kingdom"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/840/); [Wikipedia, "Kapilavastu (ancient city)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapilavastu_(ancient_city)).
- **Piprahwa, India** (proposed, plausible; 27.44°N 83.13°E, give or take 2 km). William Claxton Peppé opened a large stupa here in 1898 and found reliquaries, one with an inscription that mentions the Buddha and the Shakyas. Later digs found monastery sealings whose legend names Kapilavastu. Against: The seals belong to a monastery "of Kapilavastu", which may mean a community from the city and not the city itself; no town has been found at the site. Sources: [W. C. Peppé and V. A. Smith, "The Piprahwa Stupa, containing relics of Buddha", Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society (1898)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0035869x00025739); [Wikipedia, "Piprahwa"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piprahwa).

#### Karakorum (found)

Karakorum was the first capital of the Mongol Empire, built in the Orkhon valley in the 13th century and visited by envoys from across Eurasia. After the empire moved its centre to China the city declined, and the monastery of Erdene Zuu was built from its stones. Nikolai Yadrintsev identified the site in 1889, and a survey published in 2021 mapped the whole city for the first time. *A town from 1220 CE; built up as capital from 1235.*

What to look for: Faint earthworks of streets and house plots on steppe grassland, too low to see on the ground. Magnetic survey and a fine elevation model showed roads and buildings well beyond the old city wall.

- **Karakorum (Kharkhorin)** (found here, confirmed; 47.20°N 102.82°E, give or take 2 km). A German and Mongolian team made new magnetic and topographic surveys of the walled city and the land around it. The maps show the city in a detail not seen before and show that it spread well beyond its walls. Sources: [J. Bemmann, S. Linzen et al., "Mapping Karakorum, the capital of the Mongol Empire", Antiquity 96 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.153); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1081).

#### La Navidad (not found yet)

When the Santa María ran aground on Christmas Day 1492, Columbus used her timbers to build a small fort on the north coast of what is now Haiti and left 39 men there. He came back a year later to find it burned and the men dead. It was the first European settlement in the Americas since the Norse, and its exact site is not yet proved. *Built at Christmas 1492 CE; found destroyed on the second voyage.*

What to look for: Late 15th century European things inside a Taíno town: glass beads, hawk bells, Spanish pottery, pig and rat bones, and a burned structure with a well. Coring and magnetometry on the coastal plain guide the test pits.

- **En Bas Saline** (proposed, plausible; 19.69°N 72.02°W, give or take 5 km). This large Taíno town near Cap-Haïtien, lived in from about 1200 to 1530 CE, is thought to be the town of the chief Guacanagarí, beside which Columbus built the fort. Kathleen Deagan of the Florida Museum excavated there and found European material of the contact years. Against: No structure that can be shown to be the fort itself has been found. Sources: [Florida Museum of Natural History, "En Bas Saline"](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/histarch/research/haiti/en-bas-saline/); [Smithsonian Magazine, "The Lost Fort of Columbus" (2008)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-lost-fort-of-columbus-8026921/).

#### Land of Punt (not found yet)

For more than a thousand years the Egyptians sent ships to a land they called Punt and brought back incense, gold, ebony and live baboons. Queen Hatshepsut had her expedition carved on her temple at Deir el-Bahari in the 15th century BCE. Punt was real, but the Egyptians left no map; most scholars now place it on the African side of the southern Red Sea. *Expeditions from the 25th century BCE (Sahure) to the New Kingdom.*

What to look for: Egyptian pottery and inscriptions at a harbour on the southern Red Sea or the Horn of Africa would mark Punt itself. Chemistry already helps: isotopes in the teeth of baboons mummified in Egypt point to where the animals were born.

- **Mersa/Wadi Gawasis (the Egyptian port for Punt)** (where it began, confirmed; 26.56°N 34.04°E, give or take 2 km). Kathryn Bard and Rodolfo Fattovich excavated this Middle Kingdom harbour on the Red Sea and found ship timbers, rope, cargo boxes and inscriptions that name expeditions to Punt and Bia-Punt in the 12th Dynasty. Sources: [K. Bard and R. Fattovich, "The 12th Dynasty Punt/Bia-Punt Expeditions from Mersa/Wadi Gawasis" (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004379602_010); [Wikipedia, "Mersa Gawasis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersa_Gawasis).
- **Eritrea and the Horn of Africa** (proposed, leading view; 15.48°N 38.25°E, give or take 500 km). Oxygen and strontium isotopes in two hamadryas baboons mummified in New Kingdom Egypt show they were born in a region covering much of present-day Ethiopia, Eritrea and Djibouti and parts of Somalia and Yemen. The authors take this as evidence for where Punt lay. Against: The isotope region is wide and includes part of Yemen, so it does not settle between Africa and Arabia on its own. Sources: [N. Dominy, S. Ikram et al., "Mummified baboons reveal the far reach of early Egyptian mariners", eLife 9 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/elife.60860).

#### Lost City of the Kalahari (legend)

In 1885 the showman Guillermo Farini crossed part of the Kalahari and reported the ruins of a great walled city half buried in the sand. Dozens of expeditions went looking for it in the 20th century. In the 1960s A. J. Clement retraced Farini's route and concluded that he had seen a natural outcrop of weathered rock that looks like masonry. *Reported 1885 CE; explained in the 1960s.*

What to look for: Dolerite and similar rocks weather into flat-sided blocks that sit in rows like walls. On satellite images they follow the natural lines of the rock, and no pottery or other debris lies around them.

- **Southern Kalahari (Farini's route)** (claimed find, rejected; 23.00°S 22.00°E, give or take 300 km). Farini wrote, in the book of his journey, of the ruins of a walled city half buried in sand. Against: A. J. Clement found a rock formation on the route that matches the description and judged that the "city" exists only as a natural formation, about 180 million years old. Sources: [E. Haldeman, "Lost City of the Kalahari", The South African Archaeological Bulletin 13 (1958)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3887604); [G. A. Farini, Through the Kalahari Desert (1886), Smithsonian Libraries](https://doi.org/10.5479/sil.348906.39088005729488).

#### Lost City of Z (legend)

The British surveyor Percy Fawcett believed that the ruins of an old stone city, which he called Z, lay in the forest of Mato Grosso in Brazil. He went to look for it in 1925 with his son and a friend, and none of them came back. No stone city has been found, but archaeology in the Upper Xingu has since shown that large, planned settlements of earth and timber did exist in the region. *Fawcett vanished in 1925.*

What to look for: Not stone ruins but earthworks: ditches, raised roads, plaza rings and patches of dark fertile soil under forest. They show in lidar and, where forest is cleared, in satellite images.

- **Upper Xingu (Fawcett's last route)** (search area, fringe; 11.23°S 53.19°W, give or take 200 km). Fawcett wrote that his route would start from Dead Horse Camp, at 11°43' S, 54°35' W, and head east into the country of the Xingu headwaters. Many search parties have followed him there. Against: Fawcett's family later admitted that he gave false coordinates to throw off rivals, and no stone city of the kind he imagined has been found. Sources: [The New Yorker, "The Lost City of Z" (D. Grann, 2005)](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2005/09/19/the-lost-city-of-z).
- **Kuhikugu** (possible inspiration, plausible; 12.60°S 53.10°W, give or take 20 km). Michael Heckenberger, working with the Kuikuro people, mapped a cluster of large settlements here with plazas, ditches and roads, lived in from about 1200 to 1600 CE. Stories of them may have reached Fawcett. Against: These were towns of earth and wood built by ancestors of the people who still live there, not the stone city of an unknown race that Fawcett described. Sources: [M. Heckenberger et al., "Amazonia 1492: Pristine Forest or Cultural Parkland?", Science 301 (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1086112); [M. Heckenberger et al., "Pre-Columbian Urbanism, Anthropogenic Landscapes, and the Future of the Amazon", Science 321 (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1159769).

#### Lost Colony of Roanoke (not found yet)

In 1587 about 115 English settlers landed on Roanoke Island, in what is now North Carolina. Their governor, John White, sailed home for supplies and could not return until 1590, when he found the settlement empty and the word CROATOAN carved on a post. Where the colonists went has never been settled. *Settled 1587 CE; found abandoned 1590.*

What to look for: Late 16th century English things in Native American settings: pottery, gun parts, copper. Ground-penetrating radar and magnetometry guide the digging, and the colony's own village may have eroded into the sound.

- **Roanoke Island (Fort Raleigh)** (where it began, confirmed; 35.94°N 75.71°W, give or take 3 km). The colonists lived on the north end of Roanoke Island. A small earthwork and a metalworking area from the 1585 expedition have been excavated here, but the village of 1587 has not been found. Sources: [US National Park Service, Fort Raleigh National Historic Site](https://www.nps.gov/fora/index.htm); [Wikipedia, "Roanoke Colony"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony).
- **Hatteras Island (Croatoan)** (proposed, plausible; 35.41°N 75.49°W, give or take 20 km). Croatoan was the name of Hatteras Island and of its people, who were friendly to the English. Digs at Native sites on the island have found English objects, among them a ring with a lion crest once thought to be the gold signet of a colonist. Against: Tests in 2017 showed the ring is plain brass, a common trade item; the other English objects could also have come by trade. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "The Mystery of Roanoke Endures Yet Another Cruel Twist" (A. Lawler, 2017)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/mystery-roanoke-endures-yet-another-cruel-twist-180962837/); [National Geographic, "Newfound survivor camp may explain fate of the famed Lost Colony of Roanoke" (2020)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/newfound-survivor-camp-may-explain-lost-colony-roanoke).
- **Sites X and Y, head of Albemarle Sound (Bertie County)** (proposed, plausible; 36.06°N 76.96°W, give or take 25 km). A patch on John White's map in the British Museum hides a fort symbol 50 miles west of Roanoke. The First Colony Foundation dug there and found English pottery of the right kind on a bluff over Albemarle Sound, and argues a group of colonists moved inland. Against: Other archaeologists say the pottery could date from later English settlement in the area. Sources: [National Geographic, "Newfound survivor camp may explain fate of the famed Lost Colony of Roanoke" (2020)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/newfound-survivor-camp-may-explain-lost-colony-roanoke); [Wikipedia, "Roanoke Colony"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roanoke_Colony).

#### Loulan (found)

Loulan was an oasis kingdom on the Silk Road, beside the lake of Lop Nur in what is now Xinjiang; the Han renamed it Shanshan in 77 BCE. When the rivers shifted and the lake dried, the town was abandoned to the desert. The Swedish explorer Sven Hedin found its ruins, and wooden tablets and silks in the sand have since told much of its story. *Kingdom renamed Shanshan in 77 BCE; ruins found about 1900 CE.*

What to look for: Wind-scoured mud-brick and timber ruins on a dead lake plain, with dry river channels leading to them. The old channels are clear in optical and radar satellite images and show where water, and so people, once were.

- **Loulan ruins, Lop Nur** (found here, confirmed; 40.53°N 89.84°E, give or take 5 km). Sven Hedin discovered the ruined city and dug some of its houses. Remote sensing studies have since mapped the river system that fed the town in its lifetime. Sources: [L. Zhu et al., "Analysis of the ancient river system in Loulan period in Lop Nur region", SPIE Proceedings (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.910420); [Wikipedia, "Loulan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loulan).

#### Mabila (not found yet)

Mabila was a fortified town in what is now Alabama where, in October 1540, warriors of Chief Tuskaloosa ambushed the army of Hernando de Soto. The Spanish burned the town and killed most of its defenders. The site of the battle, a turning point of the expedition, has been looked for since the 19th century and not found. *De Soto reached Mabila on 18 October 1540 CE.*

What to look for: A small palisaded town with a thick burned layer and 16th century Spanish metal: crossbow bolt heads, chain mail links, horseshoe nails. Systematic metal detecting has been the method that works.

- **Marengo County, Alabama** (search area, plausible; 32.24°N 87.79°W, give or take 40 km). In late 2021 archaeologists announced Spanish objects from several Native American sites in this county and argued that they had found the province of Mabila, with the town itself within a few miles. Against: The town has not been found, and other researchers favour sites elsewhere in south-west Alabama. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Mabila"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabila); [V. J. Knight Jr (ed.), The Search for Mabila (2009)](https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.30346837).

#### Machu Picchu (found)

Machu Picchu is an Inca royal estate on a ridge 2,430 m above sea level, above the Urubamba river in Peru. The Spanish never recorded it, and it was known only to local farmers until one of them led Hiram Bingham there in 1911. Radiocarbon dates show it was in use from about 1420 to 1532 CE. *In use about 1420 to 1532 CE; made known to the outside world 1911.*

What to look for: Fine cut-stone buildings and farming terraces on a ridge top. Before clearing, forest hid most of it; lidar surveys of the surrounding sanctuary are still finding terraces and paths.

- **Machu Picchu** (found here, confirmed; 13.16°S 72.55°W, give or take 1 km). Hiram Bingham reached the ruins in 1911, guided by local people who knew them. New radiocarbon dates on burials published in 2021 show people lived there from about 1420, two decades earlier than the Spanish texts suggest. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Historic Sanctuary of Machu Picchu"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/274); [R. Burger et al., "New AMS dates for Machu Picchu: results and implications", Antiquity 95 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.99).

#### Mohenjo-daro (found)

Mohenjo-daro, on the Indus in Sindh, was one of the largest cities of the Indus civilisation, built of fired brick in the 3rd millennium BCE with a street grid, drains and a great bath. The civilisation was wholly forgotten until the 1920s. R. D. Banerji of the Archaeological Survey of India visited the mound in 1919 and 1920, and the excavations that followed from 1922 revealed a civilisation as old as those of Egypt and Mesopotamia. *Built in the 3rd millennium BCE; found 1922 CE.*

What to look for: A high citadel mound and a lower town of baked brick on a river plain. Much of the city is still unexcavated and lies below the water table; drilling and geophysics show it is far larger than the dug area.

- **Mohenjo-daro** (found here, confirmed; 27.33°N 68.14°E, give or take 2 km). The ruins, found in 1922, are the best preserved city of the Indus civilisation, built entirely of unbaked and baked brick in the 3rd millennium BCE. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/138).

#### Nan Madol (found)

Nan Madol is a group of more than 100 artificial islets built of basalt columns and coral rubble on the reef of Pohnpei, in Micronesia. It was the ritual and political seat of the Saudeleur dynasty, which ruled the island until about 1628. It was never lost to the people of Pohnpei; writers on lost continents have often tried to claim it, with no basis. *Megalithic building from about 1180 CE; seat of power until about 1628.*

What to look for: Walls of stacked basalt logs on rectangular islets divided by tidal canals, much of it under mangrove. Lidar through the canopy and uranium-thorium dating of coral fill gave its plan and its age.

- **Nan Madol, Pohnpei** (found here, confirmed; 6.84°N 158.33°E, give or take 2 km). Uranium-thorium dating of coral used as building fill, with chemical sourcing of the basalt, puts the start of monument building here at 1180 to 1200 CE, centuries before similar works on other Pacific islands. Sources: [M. McCoy, H. Alderson et al., "Earliest direct evidence of monument building at the archaeological site of Nan Madol (Pohnpei, Micronesia) identified using 230Th/U coral dating", Quaternary Research 86 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yqres.2016.08.002); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Nan Madol: Ceremonial Centre of Eastern Micronesia"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1503).

#### Norumbega (legend)

Norumbega appears on 16th century maps as a region, and then a rich city, on the coast of what is now New England. The name comes from Verrazzano's map of 1529. Samuel de Champlain sailed up the Penobscot in 1604 to look for it and found only a river and small native villages. *On maps from 1529 CE; searched for in 1604.*

What to look for: Nothing to find as a city. The lesson is in the maps: a native place name grew into a kingdom in the hands of European mapmakers.

- **Penobscot River (Bangor, Maine)** (proposed, leading view; 44.80°N 68.77°W, give or take 40 km). Champlain looked for Norumbega in 1604 and took the Penobscot for its great river, sailing as far as the rapids near modern Bangor. Historians place the name in this region. Against: He found no city. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Norumbega"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norumbega); [Review of American Beginnings: Exploration, Culture, and Cartography in the Land of Norumbega (1995)](https://doi.org/10.2307/366179).
- **Norumbega Tower (Weston, Massachusetts)** (claimed find, rejected; 42.35°N 71.26°W, give or take 1 km). Eben Norton Horsford decided in the late 19th century that Norumbega was a Norse settlement on the Charles River and built a stone tower in 1889 to mark the spot. Against: There is no Norse site on the Charles; archaeologists and historians rejected his evidence then and since. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Norumbega Tower"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norumbega_Tower); [Wikipedia, "Norumbega"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norumbega).

#### Ophir (not found yet)

The Hebrew Bible says the ships of King Solomon and Hiram of Tyre sailed from the Red Sea to Ophir and came back with gold, precious stones and almug wood. A potsherd of the 8th century BCE found at Tell Qasile, inscribed "gold of Ophir", shows the name was real to people of the time. Where it was is unknown; Arabia, East Africa and India have all been argued. *Voyages set in the 10th century BCE; inscription of the 8th century BCE.*

What to look for: Ancient gold workings: open cuts, shafts, grinding stones and slag near quartz veins, datable by pottery and charcoal. A Red Sea or Indian Ocean port of the right date with Levantine goods would be the other half of the proof.

- **Tell Qasile (the "gold of Ophir" ostracon)** (where it began, confirmed; 32.10°N 34.79°E, give or take 1 km). A potsherd found here in 1946, dated to the 8th century BCE, reads "gold of Ophir for Beth-Horon, 30 shekels". It is the only mention of Ophir outside the Bible. Sources: [B. Maisler (Mazar), "Two Hebrew Ostraca from Tell Qasile", Journal of Near Eastern Studies 10 (1951)](https://doi.org/10.1086/371052); [Wikipedia, "Ophir"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophir).
- **Mahd adh Dhahab, Saudi Arabia** (proposed, plausible; 23.50°N 40.86°E, give or take 10 km). In 1976 the US Department of the Interior announced that a joint US and Saudi geological team thought this ancient gold mine, in the Medina province of the Hejaz, could be the source of Solomon's gold. It was worked in antiquity and lies within reach of the Red Sea. Against: Nothing at the mine names Ophir, and old workings alone cannot settle a biblical place name. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Ophir"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophir); [R. Worl, "Ore controls at the Mahd adh Dhahab gold mine, Kingdom of Saudi Arabia", US Geological Survey Open-File Report 78-778 (1978)](https://doi.org/10.3133/ofr78778).
- **Great Zimbabwe and Sofala** (proposed, rejected; 20.27°S 30.93°E, give or take 5 km). Portuguese writers of the 16th century reasoned that Great Zimbabwe, the centre of the southern African gold trade, was Ophir. The German explorer Karl Mauch came upon the ruins while searching for Ophir. Against: Great Zimbabwe was built by ancestors of the Shona between the 11th and 15th centuries CE, two thousand years after Solomon. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Great Zimbabwe National Monument"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/364); [Wikipedia, "Ophir"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophir).

#### Paititi (legend)

Paititi is a legendary Inca refuge said to lie east of the Andes, in the rainforest where Peru, Bolivia and Brazil meet. Colonial reports spoke of a rich kingdom of that name, and Andean stories tell of the hero Inkarri withdrawing to it. Expeditions have found real Inca outposts in the forest, but no city. *Reports from the 16th and 17th centuries CE; expeditions to the present.*

What to look for: Inca stonework is regular (terraces, straight roads, rectangular platforms) and under cloud forest it shows best in lidar. Regular shapes in satellite images of this region have turned out before to be natural sandstone ridges.

- **Forests of Manú and the upper Madre de Dios** (search area, fringe; 12.14°S 71.66°W, give or take 150 km). Gregory Deyermenjian led expeditions here from 1984 to 2011 and recorded Inca remains at Mameria and elsewhere. Some versions of the legend place the city in the Pantiacolla hills of this region. Against: The finds are small outposts, and a set of pyramid shapes seen in satellite images of the area proved to be natural sandstone. Sources: [BBC News, "Dundee explorer's search for 'lost city' of Paititi" (2011)](https://www.bbc.com/news/uk-scotland-tayside-central-14387741); [Wikipedia, "Paititi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paititi).

#### Pi-Ramesses (found)

Pi-Ramesses was the great capital that Ramesses II built in the eastern Nile delta in the 13th century BCE. When its branch of the Nile silted up, later kings carried its statues and obelisks to Tanis, and for years archaeologists took Tanis for the lost capital. In the 1960s Manfred Bietak worked out where the vanished Nile branch had run, and digging and magnetic survey at Qantir showed the real city lies there under farmland. *Capital of Ramesses II (13th century BCE); abandoned as a residence during the 20th Dynasty.*

What to look for: Almost nothing shows on the surface. Mud-brick walls of palaces, temples, stables and whole street grids appear in caesium magnetometer maps of the fields, because fired and unfired Nile mud differ in magnetism from the soil around them.

- **Qantir** (found here, confirmed; 30.80°N 31.84°E, give or take 3 km). Excavations and pottery survey at Qantir and nearby Tell el-Dab'a located the Ramesside capital. Nothing of it stood above ground; its plan is known mainly from geophysics. Sources: [M. Bietak and E. Pusch, "Piramesse", The Encyclopedia of Ancient History (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah15434); [Wikipedia, "Pi-Ramesses"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi-Ramesses).
- **Tanis (the earlier identification)** (proposed, rejected; 30.98°N 31.88°E, give or take 2 km). Pierre Montet, who dug Tanis in the 1930s, found masses of stonework with the names of Ramesses II and identified the site as Pi-Ramesses. Against: It was later recognised that none of the Ramesside monuments at Tanis began there: they had been brought from elsewhere for reuse. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Pi-Ramesses"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi-Ramesses); [M. Bietak and E. Pusch, "Piramesse", The Encyclopedia of Ancient History (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781444338386.wbeah15434).

#### Quivira (found)

After Cíbola failed him, Coronado followed a guide's promise of a golden land called Quivira and crossed the plains to it in 1541. It turned out to be the country of the Wichita people in what is now Kansas, with large villages of grass houses and no gold. Archaeologists place it near the Great Bend of the Arkansas River in central Kansas. *Reached by Coronado in 1541 CE and by Oñate in 1601.*

What to look for: Ancestral Wichita villages: storage pits, house floors, ditched circles and a scatter of Spanish chain mail and iron. Magnetic gradiometry, thermal imaging and drone lidar show the ditches and pits under pasture.

- **Wichita villages of central Kansas (Rice County)** (found here, leading view; 38.35°N 98.20°W, give or take 50 km). Coronado reached Quivira in 1541 and found the Wichita living there, without gold. Remains of several villages have been found near Lyons, in Rice County, along Cow Creek and the Little Arkansas River, together with objects of Spanish make. Against: No document fixes the name to one site; the match rests on the Spanish accounts of the route and on finds of Spanish make. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Quivira"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Quivira); [Wikipedia, "Quivira"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quivira).
- **Etzanoa (Arkansas City, Kansas)** (found here, leading view; 37.07°N 97.01°W, give or take 10 km). Donald Blakeslee of Wichita State University matched the Spanish account of a "Great Settlement" seen in 1601 to sites at Arkansas City. In 2020 Jesse Casana's team used multi-sensor drone survey to find a large earthwork under a cattle pasture there. Against: The identification rests on fitting the Spanish descriptions to the ground; excavation is still at an early stage. Sources: [J. Casana et al., "A Council Circle at Etzanoa? Multi-sensor Drone Survey at an Ancestral Wichita Settlement in Southeastern Kansas", American Antiquity 85 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1017/aaq.2020.49); [Science News, "Drones find signs of a Native American 'Great Settlement' beneath a Kansas pasture" (2020)](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/drones-native-american-settlement-etzanoa-kansas-pasture).

#### Rhapta (not found yet)

Rhapta was the last market town on the coast of East Africa known to Greek and Roman traders. The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, written in the 1st century CE, says it exported ivory and tortoise shell, and Ptolemy calls it a metropolis. It was somewhere on the coast of Tanzania and has never been found. *Described in the 1st and 2nd centuries CE.*

What to look for: Roman glass, beads and pottery in a coastal or river mouth settlement of the early first millennium CE. River deltas here shift and bury sites, so coring and survey of old channels seen in satellite images matter more than surface finds.

- **Rufiji delta** (proposed, plausible; 8.00°S 39.33°E, give or take 60 km). G. W. B. Huntingford listed five proposed sites and preferred the last: somewhere in the Rufiji delta, opposite Mafia Island. The sailing distances in the Periplus fit this stretch of coast. Against: No town of the right date has been excavated there; other scholars argue for sites near Dar es Salaam or Pangani. Sources: [B. A. Datoo, "Rhapta: the Location and Importance of East Africa's first Port", Azania 5 (1970)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00672707009511528); [L. P. Kirwan, "Rhapta, Metropolis of Azania", Azania 21 (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00672708609511370).

#### Sak Tz'i' (found)

Sak Tz'i', "White Dog", was a Classic Maya kingdom named in the inscriptions of its neighbours and on looted monuments, and every known mention of it came from other cities or from looted stones. Fieldwork in 2018 with the local community identified the site of Lacanjá Tzeltal, in Chiapas, as its capital. *Classic period Maya kingdom; identified 2018, published 2020.*

What to look for: A royal centre with monumental buildings and carved monuments. What settled the identification was epigraphy: inscriptions on stones still at the site that name the kingdom.

- **Lacanjá Tzeltal (Lacandon forest, Chiapas)** (found here, leading view; 16.46°N 91.16°W, give or take 60 km). Charles Golden, Andrew Scherer and colleagues worked with the local community in 2018 and identified the site as the capital of the kingdom, known until then only from looted inscriptions. The pin is the region; the site is on private land. Against: The identification rests on inscriptions read at the site by the team that found it, and excavation has only begun. Sources: [C. Golden, A. Scherer et al., "Centering the Classic Maya Kingdom of Sak Tz'i'", Journal of Field Archaeology 45 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00934690.2019.1684748).

#### Sarai (disputed)

Sarai was the capital of the Golden Horde, the Mongol state that ruled Russia and the western steppe, founded by Batu Khan (ruled 1227 to 1255) on the lower Volga. Timur sacked it in the winter of 1395 to 1396. Two great ruin fields on the Akhtuba river, at Selitrennoye and Tsarev, have both been claimed for it, and which was Old Sarai and which New is still argued. *Founded under Batu Khan (died 1255 CE); sacked 1395 to 1396.*

What to look for: Spreads of brick rubble, glazed tile and kiln waste over several square km of river terrace, with no standing walls. Air and satellite images show the street pattern as soil marks.

- **Selitrennoye** (proposed, leading view; 47.17°N 47.45°E, give or take 5 km). The ruin field at Selitrennoye, on the Akhtuba branch of the lower Volga, is one of the two great sites identified with Sarai. Against: Since the 19th century Sarai has also been sought at Tsarev, some 250 km upstream, and the written sources do not match the two sites neatly. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Sarai (city)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarai_(city)); [M. Favereau and R. Pochekaev, "The Golden Horde, c. 1260-1502", The Cambridge History of the Mongol Empire (2023)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-the-mongol-empire/golden-horde-c-12601502/194D20494453E0AC8373BE9ADFB8B8D6).

#### Seven Cities of Cíbola (legend)

Spanish stories told of seven rich cities to the north of Mexico. The friar Marcos de Niza claimed in 1539 to have seen one, called Cíbola, and in 1540 Francisco Vázquez de Coronado marched north to take it. He found the stone and adobe pueblos of the Zuni, which had no gold; they were the real places behind the tale. *Reports of 1539 CE; Coronado expedition 1540 to 1542.*

What to look for: The ruins of the Zuni towns Coronado saw: rubble mounds of multi-storey room blocks on low rises, with Spanish metal such as crossbow points and nails from the 1540 fight. Metal detecting and surface survey have found such traces along his route.

- **Hawikuh** (possible inspiration, leading view; 34.93°N 108.98°W, give or take 2 km). Hawikuh was one of the Zuni pueblos that Coronado's army conquered in 1540; Spanish chroniclers called it Cevola or Cibola. Its ruin lies on the Zuni reservation in New Mexico, 19 km south-west of Zuni Pueblo. Against: It was a farming town of stone and mud, not a city of gold. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Seven Cities of Cíbola"](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Seven-Cities-of-Cibola); [Wikipedia, "Hawikuh Ruins"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawikuh_Ruins).

#### Shambhala and Shangri-La (legend)

Shambhala is a hidden kingdom in Tibetan Buddhist texts, a pure land ringed by snow mountains from which a future king will ride out. James Hilton borrowed the idea for Shangri-La, the valley monastery of his 1933 novel Lost Horizon. Neither is a real place, though several towns in the Himalaya have claimed the name for the sake of visitors. *Shambhala in texts from about the 10th century CE; Lost Horizon published 1933.*

What to look for: Nothing: these are a religious ideal and a novel. The lesson is how a story can be pinned to a real town by decree.

- **Shangri-La City (Zhongdian), Yunnan** (claimed find, rejected; 27.82°N 99.70°E, give or take 10 km). In 2001 the Chinese government renamed Zhongdian county, in the Tibetan part of Yunnan, as Shangri-La (Xianggelila) after a campaign that argued it was the place Hilton had in mind. Against: Hilton's valley is an invention in a novel; the renaming was a tourism policy. Sources: [Å. Kolås, "Tourism and the Making of Place in Shangri-La", Tourism Geographies 6 (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1080/1461668042000249610); [Wikipedia, "Shangri-La City"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangri-La_City).

#### Site Q (La Corona) (found)

In the 1960s finely carved Maya panels from an unknown city appeared on the art market. Scholars called their source Site Q and looked for it for decades. It proved to be La Corona, a small ruin in northern Guatemala: in 2005 Marcello Canuto found a panel still in place there that matched the looted ones. *Looted in the 1960s; located 1996; confirmed 2005.*

What to look for: Looters' trenches cut into small pyramids are the mark of a plundered Maya site, clear in lidar. The proof came from inscriptions: place names and royal names on a panel in the ground that matched pieces in museums.

- **La Corona** (found here, confirmed; 17.50°N 90.40°W, give or take 15 km). La Corona was located in 1996, and Ian Graham and David Stuart of Harvard's Peabody Museum went to study the heavily looted site; Stuart saw that its texts matched the Site Q panels. Canuto's find of a matching panel in the ground in 2005 confirmed it. Sources: [M. Canuto and T. Barrientos Q., "La Corona" (2020)](https://doi.org/10.5744/florida/9780813066226.003.0009); [Wikipedia, "La Corona"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Corona).

#### Sodom and Gomorrah (legend)

Genesis tells how God destroyed Sodom, Gomorrah and the other cities of the plain with fire and brimstone for their wickedness. The story may keep a memory of real ruined towns near the Dead Sea, but no site has been shown to be either city. Most scholars treat it as a moral tale, and the archaeological evidence for any candidate is inconclusive. *Set in the age of Abraham, traditionally the early 2nd millennium BCE.*

What to look for: Walled Bronze Age towns near the Dead Sea with thick burned destruction layers. Claims of a cosmic airburst need shocked quartz and melt glass that stand up to independent testing, and so far they have not.

- **Bab edh-Dhra and Numeira** (proposed, plausible; 31.25°N 35.53°E, give or take 5 km). These two walled towns on the south-east shore of the Dead Sea were destroyed and abandoned in the Early Bronze Age, with burned layers and a vast cemetery. The excavators Walter Rast and Thomas Schaub suggested they lie behind the story. Against: They fell centuries before the usual date for Abraham, and nothing at either site names it. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Sodom and Gomorrah"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodom_and_Gomorrah); [Wikipedia, "Bab edh-Dhra"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_edh-Dhra).
- **Tall el-Hammam** (claimed find, fringe; 31.84°N 35.67°E, give or take 3 km). Steven Collins of Trinity Southwest University, who has dug here since 2005, argues that this large Bronze Age city north-east of the Dead Sea is Sodom. In 2021 members of his team published a paper in Scientific Reports claiming that a cosmic airburst destroyed it. Against: Scientific Reports retracted the airburst paper on 24 April 2025, after an expert in research misconduct showed that images in it had been altered, and most archaeologists do not accept the site as Sodom. Sources: [T. Bunch et al., "RETRACTED ARTICLE: A Tunguska sized airburst destroyed Tall el-Hammam a Middle Bronze Age city in the Jordan Valley near the Dead Sea", Scientific Reports 11 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-97778-3); [Wikipedia, "Tell el-Hammam"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_el-Hammam).

#### Tanis (found)

Tanis, in the north-east Nile delta, was the capital of Egypt in the 21st and 22nd Dynasties. Its mounds were always known; Pierre Montet dug there from 1929 and in 1939 found royal tombs with their gold and silver intact. In 2011 Sarah Parcak showed that infrared satellite images reveal the street plan of the buried mud-brick town around the temples. *Capital in the 21st and 22nd Dynasties (11th to 8th centuries BCE); royal tombs found 1939 CE; satellite plan 2011.*

What to look for: Mud-brick is denser than the soil around it and holds water differently, so buried walls show as lines in near-infrared satellite images taken at the right season. Houses, streets and suburbs appear without digging.

- **Tanis (San el-Hagar)** (found here, confirmed; 30.98°N 31.88°E, give or take 2 km). An infrared satellite image shows the streets and houses of ancient Tanis, which cannot be seen on the ground. The same survey of Egypt, led by Sarah Parcak, reported 17 possible lost pyramids, more than 1,000 tombs and 3,000 ancient settlements. Sources: [BBC News, "Egyptian pyramids found by infra-red satellite images" (2011)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-13522957); [Wikipedia, "Tanis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanis).

#### Tarhuntassa (not found yet)

In the early 13th century BCE the Hittite king Muwatalli II moved his capital from Hattusa to a new city in the south called Tarhuntassa. It later became the seat of a rival line of kings. Treaties describe its borders, but the city has not been found; it lay somewhere in south-central Turkey. *Hittite capital in the early 13th century BCE.*

What to look for: A very large mound with a lower town, Late Bronze Age pottery on the surface and Luwian hieroglyphic inscriptions. Regional survey, satellite images of old water courses and magnetic survey of the lower town are the tools in use.

- **Türkmen-Karahöyük** (proposed, plausible; 37.62°N 33.03°E, give or take 3 km). A survey of the Konya plain found this mound to be a major Bronze and Iron Age centre, and in 2019 a farmer's tip led the team to a stone carved with a Luwian inscription of the Great King Hartapu. The surveyors tentatively suggest the city was Tarhuntassa in the Late Bronze Age. Against: The authors themselves call the identification tentative; other scholars place Tarhuntassa nearer the Mediterranean coast. Sources: [M. Massa, C. Bachhuber et al., "A landscape-oriented approach to urbanisation and early state formation on the Konya and Karaman plains, Turkey", Anatolian Studies 70 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0066154620000034); [P. Goedegebuure, T. van den Hout et al., "Türkmen-Karahöyük 1: a new Hieroglyphic Luwian inscription from Great King Hartapu", Anatolian Studies 70 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0066154620000022).

#### Tartessos (not found yet)

Greek writers knew Tartessos as a kingdom rich in silver beyond the Pillars of Heracles, ruled by the long-lived king Arganthonios, and some speak of a city of that name at the mouth of a great river. The Tartessian culture of south-west Spain in the early first millennium BCE is real and well dug. No single capital city has been found, and many archaeologists doubt there was one. *Culture of the early first millennium BCE.*

What to look for: Mud-brick buildings deliberately buried under mounds, hoards of gold jewellery, and Phoenician imports. River mouths here have silted up by many kilometres, so the old coast must be mapped by coring before the search for a port can start.

- **Huelva** (proposed, leading view; 37.25°N 6.95°W, give or take 5 km). Finds under the modern port city, including great quantities of early Phoenician and Greek pottery, led J. M. Luzón and others to identify Huelva with the trading town of Tartessos. Against: The remains lie under a living city and no palace or city wall has been found. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Tartessos"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartessos); [Review of S. Celestino and C. López-Ruiz, Tartessos and the Phoenicians in Iberia (Oxford, 2016), American Journal of Archaeology 123 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.3764/ajaonline1233.fernandezgotz).
- **Mouth of the Guadalquivir (Doñana)** (search area, rejected; 37.02°N 6.44°W, give or take 20 km). The German historian Adolf Schulten, whose publications from 1922 drew attention to Tartessos, held that the city lay buried under the shifting wetlands at the mouth of the Guadalquivir, the ancient Baetis. Against: No Tartessian city has been found in the marshes, then or since. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Tartessos"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartessos); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Tartessus"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tartessus).
- **Casas del Turuñuelo** (found here, confirmed; 38.95°N 6.06°W, give or take 2 km). Excavation of this large Tartessian building in the Guadiana valley began in 2015. It has a courtyard used for animal sacrifice, and in April 2023 the excavators announced five carved busts of the 5th century BCE, the first human images known from the culture. Sources: [S. Celestino Pérez and E. Rodríguez González, "Un espacio para el sacrificio: el patio del yacimiento tartésico de Casas del Turuñuelo (Guareña, Badajoz)", Complutum 30 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.5209/cmpl.66337); [Wikipedia, "Turuñuelo"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turu%C3%B1uelo).

#### Thinis (not found yet)

Thinis was the home town of the first kings of a united Egypt, according to the historian Manetho, and the capital of its province for three thousand years. The kings were buried nearby at Abydos. The town itself has never been located; Egyptologists place it at or near modern Girga. *Capital of the first two dynasties, about 3100 to 2700 BCE.*

What to look for: An Early Dynastic town mound, probably buried under Nile silt or a modern town. Deep coring, and satellite mapping of where the river ran 5,000 years ago, are the realistic ways to find it.

- **Girga and El-Birba** (search area, leading view; 26.33°N 31.90°E, give or take 15 km). The mainstream view places Thinis close to Abydos, at modern Girga or the neighbouring village of El-Birba. Against: Nothing has been excavated that proves it; the Nile has moved and the old town may lie under metres of silt. Sources: [A. Othman, "Tni (Thinis), the lost capital of Egypt: a study of its history, civilizational role, and possible location" (2023)](https://doi.org/10.21608/abidus.2023.343563); [Wikipedia, "Thinis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinis).

#### Tollan (found)

Tollan is the splendid city of the Toltecs in Aztec tradition, home of the priest-king Quetzalcoatl and the source of all fine craft. Most scholars identify the historical Tollan with the ruins at Tula in Hidalgo. The name also meant any great city, and some argue the first Tollan was Teotihuacan. *Tula flourished about 850 to 1150 CE.*

What to look for: A planned ceremonial centre with pyramids, colonnaded halls and ball courts, and a wide spread of houses around it, mapped by survey and air photos.

- **Tula (Hidalgo)** (found here, leading view; 20.06°N 99.34°W, give or take 2 km). The ruins at Tula, with their pyramid topped by warrior columns, are identified as Tollan, the Toltec capital. Against: The site is smaller and plainer than the city of the Aztec stories, which were part legend. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Tula"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tula-ancient-city-Mexico); [Wikipedia, "Tula (Mesoamerican site)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tula_(Mesoamerican_site)).
- **Teotihuacan** (proposed, plausible; 19.69°N 98.84°W, give or take 2 km). Tollan was also a title for great cities, and Teotihuacan, far older and larger than Tula, seems to have been the first to bear it. Against: The Toltec dynasty of the histories belongs to the centuries after Teotihuacan fell. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Tollan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollan); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Pre-Hispanic City of Teotihuacan"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/414).

#### Troy (found)

Troy is the city of Homer's Iliad, which by the 18th century many thought pure legend. Frank Calvert and then Heinrich Schliemann, who dug from 1870, showed that the mound of Hisarlık near the Dardanelles holds a walled city rebuilt many times over 4,000 years, and that is accepted as Troy. Whether there was a Trojan War is a separate question. *Occupied about 3000 BCE to 500 CE; excavated from 1870 CE.*

What to look for: A tell: a mound built of one town on top of another. Later excavations found a lower town of Troy VI and VII beyond the citadel, ringed by a wide defensive ditch.

- **Hisarlık** (found here, leading view; 39.96°N 26.24°E, give or take 1 km). Excavations begun by Heinrich Schliemann in 1870 found a fortified citadel with many building levels. The levels of the Late Bronze Age, Troy VI and VII, are the ones usually linked to the Troy of the epic. Against: Nothing found on the mound names it as Troy; the identification rests on its place, size and dates, and nearly all scholars accept it. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Archaeological Site of Troy"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/849); [Wikipedia, "Troy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy).

#### Vijayanagara (Hampi) (found)

Vijayanagara was the capital of the last great Hindu empire of south India, a city that amazed Persian and Portuguese visitors in the 15th and 16th centuries. An alliance of sultanates defeated its army in 1565, sacked the city and left it empty. Its ruins at Hampi cover a wide granite landscape and were surveyed from the 19th century on. *Capital from the 14th century CE; sacked 1565.*

What to look for: Temples, palace platforms, market streets, aqueducts and miles of fortification wall spread among boulder hills. The wider suburbs and canals show in satellite images and lidar.

- **Hampi** (found here, confirmed; 15.34°N 76.46°E, give or take 3 km). The site holds more than 1,600 surviving remains of the capital, among them forts, temples, shrines, pillared halls and water works. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Group of Monuments at Hampi"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241).

#### Vilcabamba (found)

Vilcabamba was the last capital of the Incas, a refuge in the forested foothills north-west of Cusco from which they held out against Spain until 1572. The Incas burned it when the Spanish came, and its place was forgotten. Hiram Bingham thought he had found it at Machu Picchu in 1911; in 1964 Gene Savoy showed it was the ruin at Espíritu Pampa, which Bingham had also seen. *Inca refuge 1539 to 1572 CE; identified 1964.*

What to look for: Low Inca walls and platforms under tall forest, with burned layers from 1572. Little of it rises above the undergrowth, so lidar and clearing are needed to see the plan.

- **Espíritu Pampa** (found here, confirmed; 12.90°S 73.21°W, give or take 3 km). Gene Savoy identified this ruin as Vilcabamba in 1964, and archaeologists widely accept it. Excavations have since found burned Inca buildings and, lower down, rich tombs of the much older Wari culture. Sources: [The Guardian, "Peru's last Incan city reveals its secrets" (2018)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/sep/28/perus-last-incan-city-reveals-its-secrets-its-genuinely-a-marvel); [B. Bauer et al., Vilcabamba and the Archaeology of Inca Resistance (Cotsen Institute, 2015), "An Introduction to the Site of Espíritu Pampa"](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvdjrr4b.11).
- **Machu Picchu (Bingham's mistake)** (proposed, rejected; 13.16°S 72.55°W, give or take 1 km). Hiram Bingham, who was looking for Vilcabamba in 1911, was led to Machu Picchu and argued for years that it was the last Inca capital. Against: Machu Picchu is a royal estate of the 15th century in the wrong place; the Spanish accounts of the 1572 campaign fit Espíritu Pampa. Sources: [Wikipedia, "Vilcabamba, Peru"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilcabamba,_Peru); [R. Burger et al., "New AMS dates for Machu Picchu: results and implications", Antiquity 95 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.99).

#### Vinland (found)

Icelandic sagas tell how Leif Eriksson and others sailed from Greenland about 1000 CE to a land of timber and wild grapes they called Vinland. For centuries nobody knew if it was true. In 1960 Helge Ingstad and Anne Stine Ingstad found a Norse site at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland, which proves the voyages; where the Vinland of the grapes lay, further south, is still open. *Voyages about 1000 CE; Norse wood at the site cut in 1021 CE; found 1960.*

What to look for: Turf-walled halls show as low rectangular banks, which satellite images can flag, but only digging tells a Norse wall from a natural one. Proof needs Norse objects: iron boat rivets, slag from bog iron, a bronze cloak pin.

- **L'Anse aux Meadows** (found here, confirmed; 51.60°N 55.53°W, give or take 1 km). Excavation after the discovery in 1960 uncovered eight timber-framed turf buildings like those of Norse Greenland and Iceland. In 2021 a marker left in tree rings by a solar storm in 993 CE dated wood cut with metal tools at the site to exactly 1021 CE. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "L'Anse aux Meadows National Historic Site"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/4); [M. Kuitems et al., "Evidence for European presence in the Americas in AD 1021", Nature 601 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03972-8).
- **Point Rosee** (claimed find, rejected; 47.84°N 59.38°W, give or take 2 km). Sarah Parcak picked out this headland in south-west Newfoundland from satellite images in 2015. A test dig found what looked like a turf wall and a hearth with roasted bog iron, and the press announced a possible second Norse site. Against: Two summers of digging found no evidence of Norse activity; the team's own report to the province says so. Sources: [CBC News, "Archeological quest for Codroy Valley Vikings comes up short" (2018)](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/newfoundland-labrador/codroy-valley-vikings-report-1.4684066); [National Geographic, "Discovery Could Rewrite History of Vikings in New World" (2016)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/160331-viking-discovery-north-america-canada-archaeology).

#### Washukanni (not found yet)

Washukanni was the capital of Mitanni, the Hurrian kingdom that ruled northern Syria and Mesopotamia about 1500 to 1300 BCE and wrote to the pharaohs as an equal. The Hittites sacked it and the Assyrians took it over. Its site is not proved; the leading candidate is Tell Fekheriye at the head of the Khabur river. *Capital about 1500 to 1300 BCE.*

What to look for: A large mound with a Mitanni period palace and, with luck, an archive of clay tablets that names the city. Clay analysis of Mitanni royal letters found in Egypt has been used to test candidate sites.

- **Tell Fekheriye** (proposed, plausible; 36.84°N 40.07°E, give or take 3 km). This largely unexcavated mound near Tell Halaf, at the head of the Khabur, was proposed as Washukanni in the early 20th century. Excavations by a German team led by Mirko Novák and Dominik Bonatz from 2006 gave the idea new support. Against: Many seasons of digging have not produced proof, and the claim is unconfirmed; many scholars have opposed it. Sources: [D. Bonatz, "Tell Fekheriye", in Archaeological Explorations in Syria 2000-2011 (Archaeopress, 2018)](https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.15136051.15); [Wikipedia, "Waššukanni"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wa%C5%A1%C5%A1ukanni).

#### Xanadu (Shangdu) (found)

Xanadu is the English name, from Marco Polo and Coleridge, for Shangdu, the summer capital that Kublai Khan founded in 1256 on the grasslands of Inner Mongolia. The site was abandoned in 1430. Its walls and mounds were never truly lost, and the site became a World Heritage Site in 2012. *Founded 1256 CE; abandoned 1430.*

What to look for: Three nested rectangles of earth walls, with palace platforms and a grid of streets, plain in satellite images of open steppe.

- **Site of Xanadu** (found here, confirmed; 42.36°N 116.19°E, give or take 2 km). The remains cover 25,000 hectares north of the Great Wall: the palace city, imperial city and outer city of Kublai Khan's capital, laid out in 1256. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Site of Xanadu"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1389).

#### Yamatai (not found yet)

The Chinese Records of Wei, written in the 3rd century CE, describe a country in the Japanese islands called Yamatai, ruled by a shaman queen named Himiko, who led a league of more than 30 small states. The directions in the text cannot be followed on a map. Scholars have argued for three hundred years over whether Yamatai was in northern Kyushu or in the Kinai region around Nara. *Described for the 3rd century CE.*

What to look for: A large planned settlement of the early 3rd century with a palace-like compound, goods from all over Japan, Chinese bronze mirrors and a great mound tomb. Tree-ring and radiocarbon dates for the earliest keyhole tombs are the key evidence.

- **Makimuku and Hashihaka (Nara)** (proposed, plausible; 34.55°N 135.84°E, give or take 3 km). Supporters of the Kinai theory point to the large settlement of Makimuku, which grew up suddenly in the early 3rd century, and to the nearby Hashihaka mound, which some propose as the tomb of Himiko. Against: The Imperial Household Agency does not allow Hashihaka to be excavated, and the dating of the mound is disputed. Sources: [J. E. Kidder, Himiko and Japan's Elusive Chiefdom of Yamatai (University of Hawaii Press, 2007), ch. 12, "Makimuku and the Location of Yamatai"](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780824862848-016); [W. Edwards, "In Pursuit of Himiko: Postwar Archaeology and the Location of Yamatai", Monumenta Nipponica 51 (1996)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2385316).
- **Yoshinogari (Saga, Kyushu)** (proposed, plausible; 33.33°N 130.38°E, give or take 3 km). Supporters of the Kyushu theory point to large moated Yayoi settlements in the north of the island, of which Yoshinogari, under full excavation since 1986, is the best known. Kyushu is where the Wei envoys would have landed. Against: Yoshinogari was at its height earlier than Himiko, and nothing there names Yamatai. Sources: [Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Yamatai"](https://www.britannica.com/place/Yamatai); [Wikipedia, "Yoshinogari site"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshinogari_site).

#### Zerzura (legend)

Zerzura is a white city or oasis said to lie hidden in the desert west of the Nile, full of treasure and guarded by a sleeping king. It appears in medieval Arabic writings, among them a lost treasure hunters' manual, the Kitab al-Kanuz. In the 1920s and 1930s British and Hungarian desert explorers made a sport of looking for it and mapped the Gilf Kebir plateau on the way. *Named in medieval Arabic texts; expeditions 1929 to 1934 CE.*

What to look for: Dry valleys with living trees in a plateau that is otherwise bare, which show as dark threads in satellite images. Rock art in the region records a time when the Sahara was green.

- **Wadis of the Gilf Kebir** (claimed find, fringe; 23.44°N 25.84°E, give or take 60 km). Flights by the expedition of László Almásy and Patrick Clayton in 1932 found two valleys with trees in the northern Gilf Kebir, and Almásy later reached a third. He argued that these three green wadis were the Zerzura of the legend. Against: They are seasonal pastures, not a city, and the treasure of the Arabic texts is fable. Sources: [O. Wingate, "In Search of Zerzura", The Geographical Journal 83 (1934)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1786487); [N. B. de Lancey Forth, "More Journeys in Search of Zerzura", The Geographical Journal 75 (1930)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1783754).

### Found from above

#### Aguada Fénix (found)

Aguada Fénix, in Tabasco, Mexico, is the oldest and largest monumental structure known in the Maya area: a raised earthen platform 1,400 m long, built between 1000 and 800 BCE. Nobody knew it was there. It is so wide and low that from the ground it looks like natural terrain, and Takeshi Inomata's team saw it only in lidar. *Built about 1000 to 800 BCE; reported 2020.*

What to look for: A long rectangle of raised ground, 10 to 15 m high, with straight causeways running out from it: too regular to be natural and too big to notice on foot. Low-resolution public lidar was enough to see it.

- **Aguada Fénix** (found here, confirmed; 17.80°N 91.20°W, give or take 10 km). An airborne lidar survey and excavation found a previously unknown site with an artificial plateau 1,400 m long and 10 to 15 m high, with nine causeways radiating from it. Radiocarbon dates put its building between 1000 and 800 BCE. Sources: [T. Inomata et al., "Monumental architecture at Aguada Fénix and the rise of Maya civilization", Nature 582 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-020-2343-4).

#### Ahramat branch of the Nile (found)

Most of Egypt's pyramids stand in a line along the edge of the desert, kilometres from the modern Nile. In 2024 Eman Ghoneim's team used radar satellite images, geophysics and deep soil cores to trace a silted-up branch of the river that once ran along the foot of that line. They named it the Ahramat Branch and argue that it was the waterway by which stone and workers reached the building sites. *Active in the Old and Middle Kingdoms; published 2024 CE.*

What to look for: A buried river channel under farmland and sand, which radar from orbit can trace and cores then confirm. Pyramid causeways that end at the channel bank mark the old harbours.

- **Pyramid fields from Lisht to Giza** (found here, leading view; 29.80°N 31.23°E, give or take 40 km). The team combined radar satellite imagery with geophysical data and deep soil coring to trace the buried channel. Many Old and Middle Kingdom pyramids have causeways that lead to it and end at valley temples, which may have served as river harbours. Against: Independent teams still need to date the channel along its whole length to show it flowed at each pyramid's time. Sources: [E. Ghoneim et al., "The Egyptian pyramid chain was built along the now abandoned Ahramat Nile Branch", Communications Earth & Environment 5 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-024-01379-7).

#### Angkor lidar surveys (found)

The temples of Angkor in Cambodia were never lost, but the wooden cities around them rotted away and forest covered the ground. Airborne lidar flown in 2012 showed that Angkor Wat and the other temples stood inside a planned grid of streets, canals and house mounds. It confirmed that Angkor was the largest settlement complex of the pre-industrial world. *Khmer capitals of the 9th to 15th centuries CE; lidar flown 2012.*

What to look for: Under forest, a grid of low banks and ditches: city blocks, each with a house mound and a pond. Also canals, dykes and the faint outlines of reservoirs and quarries.

- **Angkor** (found here, confirmed; 13.43°N 103.83°E, give or take 10 km). Damian Evans and colleagues used lidar to make ground models under the forest and found a formally planned urban landscape, not recorded before, into which Angkor Wat and the other great temples were built. Sources: [D. Evans et al., "Uncovering archaeological landscapes at Angkor using lidar", PNAS 110 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1306539110); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Angkor"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/668).
- **Mahendraparvata (Phnom Kulen)** (found here, confirmed; 13.61°N 104.11°E, give or take 10 km). Inscriptions said that an early capital of the Khmer Empire, Mahendraparvata, stood on the Kulen plateau north-east of Angkor. An expedition led by Jean-Baptiste Chevance and Damian Evans mapped it with lidar in 2012, and a 2019 paper set out its full plan. Sources: [J.-B. Chevance, D. Evans et al., "Mahendraparvata: an early Angkor-period capital defined through airborne laser scanning at Phnom Kulen", Antiquity 93 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2019.133); [Wikipedia, "Mahendraparvata"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendraparvata).

#### Borgring (found)

Borgring is a Viking Age ring fortress south of Copenhagen, one of the perfectly circular forts built in Denmark in the tenth century. Søren Sindbæk and Nanna Holm suspected a missing fort in the area and found a faint ring in a high-resolution lidar map. Geophysical survey and small trenches in 2014 confirmed it: the first such fortress found in Denmark in over six decades. *Built in the 10th century CE; confirmed 2014.*

What to look for: A perfect circle 145 m across, ploughed almost flat, that shows as a faint rise in lidar. Excavation in 2014 found the north and east gates exactly where the standard plan of such forts puts them.

- **Borgring** (found here, confirmed; 55.47°N 12.12°E, give or take 1 km). High-resolution lidar mapping, geophysical survey and targeted small excavation showed that the site is a Trelleborg-type ring fortress of the tenth century. Sources: [H. Goodchild, N. Holm and S. Sindbæk, "Borgring: the discovery of a Viking Age ring fortress", Antiquity 91 (2017)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2017.118); [Wikipedia, "Borgring"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgring).

#### Calakmul lidar survey (found)

Calakmul, in the forest of southern Campeche, was the seat of the Snake kings, the great rivals of Tikal. A lidar survey released in 2022 by the Bajo Laberinto project and INAH showed how big it really was: a dense spread of large housing compounds, temples, canals and terraces under the canopy for kilometres around the known centre. *At its height about 700 CE; lidar results announced 2022.*

What to look for: Apartment-like compounds of dozens of buildings round shared courtyards, grouped near temples and possible markets, with canals, terraces, walls and dams filling the land between.

- **Calakmul** (found here, confirmed; 18.11°N 89.81°W, give or take 10 km). The survey, led by Kathryn Reese-Taylor with INAH Campeche, found large residential compounds throughout the scanned area, some with as many as 60 buildings, around temples, shrines and possible marketplaces. It makes Calakmul one of the largest cities in the Americas in 700 CE. Sources: [University of Calgary, "UCalgary research collaboration reveals new urbanization and landscape modifications at ancient Maya city" (2022)](https://news.ucalgary.ca/news/ucalgary-research-collaboration-reveals-new-urbanization-and-landscape-modifications-ancient-maya); [Archaeology (Archaeological Institute of America), "Lidar Survey Reveals Urban Sprawl of Ancient Maya City" (2022)](https://archaeology.org/news/2022/10/27/221028-mexico-maya-calakmul/).

#### Caracol lidar survey (found)

Caracol is a large Classic Maya city in the forest of western Belize, first reported in 1937 and dug for decades by Arlen and Diane Chase. In 2009 they had it scanned by airborne lidar, the first time the method was used on a whole Maya city. In a few days of flying it showed the full extent of the city under the canopy, with its causeways, reservoirs and farming terraces. *Classic Maya city, lived in until about 950 CE; lidar flown 2009.*

What to look for: In a lidar ground model: thousands of low terraces following the hill contours, straight raised causeways joining plaza groups, and square reservoirs. None of it shows in satellite photos of the canopy.

- **Caracol** (found here, confirmed; 16.76°N 89.12°W, give or take 5 km). The Chases had lidar flown over the city in the dry season of 2009. It gave a fast view of the whole site and its surroundings, about 200 square km, and became the model for lidar work across the Maya lowlands. Sources: [A. Chase, D. Chase et al., "Airborne LiDAR, archaeology, and the ancient Maya landscape at Caracol, Belize", Journal of Archaeological Science 38 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2010.09.018); [Wikipedia, "Caracol"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caracol).

#### Casarabe settlements of the Llanos de Mojos (found)

The Casarabe culture lived in the seasonally flooded savannah of the Bolivian Amazon between about 500 and 1400 CE. Mounds there had been dug for years, but lidar showed that the largest, Cotoca and Landívar, were centres of a planned settlement system with platforms, pyramids, causeways and canals. It was the first clear case of low-density urbanism in Amazonia. *About 500 to 1400 CE; lidar published 2022.*

What to look for: Stepped platforms topped by U-shaped buildings and conical mounds, ringed by ramparts, with straight causeways and canals running for kilometres to smaller sites and reservoirs.

- **Cotoca and Landívar (near Casarabe)** (found here, confirmed; 14.90°S 64.50°W, give or take 30 km). Lidar data for six areas showed two very large settlement sites, Cotoca and Landívar, each the hub of a network of smaller sites joined to it by causeways that still run straight across the savannah. Sources: [H. Prümers et al., "Lidar reveals pre-Hispanic low-density urbanism in the Bolivian Amazon", Nature 606 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04780-4).

#### Desert kites of Arabia (found)

Desert kites are huge hunting traps: two low stone walls, sometimes kilometres long, that funnel gazelle into a walled pen. Royal Air Force pilots first saw them from the air in the 1920s and gave them their name. More than 6,000 are now known from Arabia to Central Asia, almost all found in satellite images, and the oldest are at least 9,000 years old. *Oldest at least 9,000 years old; first seen from the air in the 1920s.*

What to look for: From above: a V or funnel of thin lines closing on an enclosure with small cells on its rim; on the ground the walls are a few stones high and almost invisible. Free satellite imagery is enough to map them.

- **Harrat al-Sham (Black Desert), Jordan** (found here, confirmed; 32.63°N 36.76°E, give or take 120 km). The basalt desert of eastern Jordan and southern Syria holds the densest group of kites, the first to be seen from aircraft. In June 2015 a stone engraved with a plan of a kite, drawn to scale, was found on a site in south-eastern Jordan. Sources: [R. Crassard et al., "The oldest plans to scale of humanmade mega-structures", PLOS ONE 18 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0277927); [Wikipedia, "Desert kite"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_kite).
- **Khaybar, Saudi Arabia** (found here, confirmed; 25.70°N 39.29°E, give or take 60 km). Kites are thick on the lava fields of north-western Arabia, and the 2023 study illustrates plans of them from Khaybar. At Jebel az-Zilliyat, also in Saudi Arabia, the team found a boulder engraved with scale plans of kites. Sources: [R. Crassard et al., "The oldest plans to scale of humanmade mega-structures", PLOS ONE 18 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0277927).

#### El Mirador basin causeways (found)

The forest around El Mirador, in the far north of Guatemala, hides some of the earliest and largest Maya cities, built between about 1000 BCE and 150 CE. Lidar published in 2022 by Richard Hansen's team identified more than 775 settlements in the basin, many joined by raised causeways into one web. *Preclassic Maya, about 1000 BCE to 150 CE; lidar published 2022.*

What to look for: White plaster causeways, now tree-covered ridges up to tens of metres wide, running straight between cities for many kilometres across seasonal swamp. Triadic pyramid groups and reservoirs mark the centres.

- **El Mirador** (found here, confirmed; 17.75°N 89.92°W, give or take 20 km). The study identified more than 775 ancient Maya settlements in the basin and 189 more on the ridge around it, with a concentration of Preclassic sites connected by causeways. The authors read the web of causeways as a sign of early political union, a point other Maya archaeologists debate. Sources: [R. Hansen et al., "LiDAR analyses in the contiguous Mirador-Calakmul Karst Basin, Guatemala", Ancient Mesoamerica 34 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956536122000244).

#### Falerii Novi radar survey (found)

Falerii Novi is a Roman town in Lazio, north of Rome, now under fields. In 2020 a team from Cambridge and Ghent universities published the first high-resolution ground-penetrating radar survey of a complete Roman town. It showed the buried buildings of the whole town in depth, without digging. *Founded after 241 BCE; radar survey published 2020 CE.*

What to look for: Radar time slices show wall footings, floors, drains and water pipes at each depth, so a town plan can be read level by level. Dry, stone-built sites under pasture give the clearest results.

- **Falerii Novi** (found here, confirmed; 42.30°N 12.36°E, give or take 1 km). The survey is presented as the first high-resolution radar map of an entire Roman town, and as a method that could change how buried cities are studied. Sources: [L. Verdonck et al., "Ground-penetrating radar survey at Falerii Novi: a new approach to the study of Roman cities", Antiquity 94 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2020.82); [Wikipedia, "Falerii Novi"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falerii_Novi).

#### Garamantian towns of the Fezzan (found)

The Garamantes were a Saharan people known to the Romans as desert raiders. Survey in the oases of the Libyan Fezzan, much of it with satellite images, has shown that they built real towns, fortified villages and underground water channels between the late first millennium BCE and the early first millennium CE. Their capital was Garama, modern Germa. *Late first millennium BCE to early first millennium CE; satellite survey reported from 2011.*

What to look for: Mud-brick forts (qsur) with square towers, walled villages, field systems and long lines of shaft openings from foggara water tunnels. All show sharply in high-resolution satellite images of bare desert.

- **Germa (Garama) and the Fezzan oases** (found here, confirmed; 26.53°N 13.07°E, give or take 150 km). David Mattingly and Martin Sterry show that the oasis settlements, though modest in size, meet the tests for towns, and argue that trans-Saharan trade and not farming alone called them into being. Sources: [D. Mattingly and M. Sterry, "The first towns in the central Sahara", Antiquity 87 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00049097); [Wikipedia, "Garamantes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garamantes).

#### Geoglyphs of Acre (found)

When cattle ranchers cleared forest in the Brazilian state of Acre from the 1970s on, perfect circles and squares of ditch and bank appeared in the pasture, some 300 m across. Hundreds are now known, most found in satellite images. A lidar study in 2023 found 24 more under standing forest and estimated that over 10,000 such earthworks remain hidden across Amazonia. *Built about 2,000 to 700 years ago; recognised from the 1970s.*

What to look for: Ditches about 10 m wide laid out as exact circles, squares and octagons, joined by straight avenues. In cleared land they show in any satellite image; under forest only lidar finds them.

- **Eastern Acre** (found here, confirmed; 9.03°S 70.52°W, give or take 250 km). Martti Pärssinen, Denise Schaan and Alceu Ranzi described the earthworks as the work of a previously unknown society that built precise geometric enclosures joined by straight roads. Brazil has put the geoglyphs on its World Heritage tentative list. Sources: [M. Pärssinen, D. Schaan and A. Ranzi, "Pre-Columbian geometric earthworks in the upper Purús", Antiquity 83 (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00099373); [V. Peripato et al., "More than 10,000 pre-Columbian earthworks are still hidden throughout Amazonia", Science 382 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade2541).

#### Kweneng (found)

Kweneng is a large Tswana town of stone-walled homesteads, 30 km south of Johannesburg, lived in for several centuries up to the 1800s. Its ruins were known in part from air photos but thick bush hid most of them. Lidar studied by Karim Sadr of the University of the Witwatersrand showed a town with traces of about 900 homesteads. *About the 15th to 19th centuries CE; lidar study published 2019.*

What to look for: Clusters of dry-stone circles: cattle pens ringed by house enclosures, linked by walled cattle tracks, with ash heaps at the gates. Lidar strips away the bush and shows the whole street plan.

- **Kweneng (Suikerbosrand)** (found here, confirmed; 26.50°S 28.20°E, give or take 15 km). Sadr describes Kweneng as a wide aggregation of stone-walled ruins that represent a pre-colonial Tswana capital, 30 km south of Johannesburg. Sources: [K. Sadr, "Kweneng: A Newly Discovered Pre-Colonial Capital Near Johannesburg", Journal of African Archaeology 17 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1163/21915784-20190001); [The Guardian, "Laser technology shines light on South African 'lost city' of Kweneng" (2019)](https://www.theguardian.com/cities/2019/jan/08/laser-technology-shines-light-on-south-african-lost-city-of-kweneng).

#### Mustatils of north-west Arabia (found)

Mustatils are long rectangles of low stone wall, tens to hundreds of metres long, scattered in their hundreds across the lava fields and sandstone of north-western Saudi Arabia. They were noticed in satellite images and called "gates". Fieldwork in AlUla and Khaybar counties has shown that they are ritual monuments of the late sixth millennium BCE, linked to an early cult of cattle. *Late sixth millennium BCE.*

What to look for: From above: a long narrow rectangle with a thicker platform at one end and a small entrance at the other, often in groups. Helicopter and satellite survey find them; excavation of the end chamber dates them.

- **AlUla and Khaybar counties** (found here, confirmed; 25.88°N 39.39°E, give or take 150 km). Survey and excavation show the mustatils are more complex in build than was thought and served as ritual sites, with the earliest evidence for a cattle cult in the Arabian Peninsula. Sources: [H. Thomas et al., "The mustatils: cult and monumentality in Neolithic north-western Arabia", Antiquity 95 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.51).

#### Nazca geoglyphs found with AI (found)

The Nazca lines of southern Peru have been studied for a century, in which time 430 figurative geoglyphs were found. In 2024 a team from Yamagata University and IBM Research reported 303 new figurative geoglyphs, found in six months of fieldwork after an AI model had flagged candidates in high-resolution aerial photographs. The new ones are small figures of animals and people beside old footpaths. *Made about 2,000 years ago; AI survey published 2024.*

What to look for: Faint relief-type figures about 9 m across, made by clearing dark stones from pale ground. They are too small and worn for the eye to find across 629 square km of desert; a model trained on few examples narrowed the search for people to check on the ground.

- **Nazca Pampa** (found here, confirmed; 14.70°S 75.14°W, give or take 20 km). An AI system applied to the whole Nazca region led to the discovery of 303 new figurative geoglyphs within six months of field survey, nearly doubling the number known. Every candidate was checked on site by archaeologists. Sources: [M. Sakai et al., "AI-accelerated Nazca survey nearly doubles the number of known figurative geoglyphs and sheds light on their purpose", PNAS 121 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2407652121); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Lines and Geoglyphs of Nasca and Palpa"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/700).

#### Ocomtún (found)

Ocomtún is a Maya city in the Balamkú ecological reserve in Campeche, Mexico, in a block of forest of some 3,000 square km that archaeologists had never entered. Ivan Šprajc's team reached it in 2023 after airborne lidar showed concentrations of buildings there. Mexico's heritage institute INAH announced it in June 2023. *Pottery points to about 600 to 800 CE (Late Classic); found 2023.*

What to look for: Pyramid mounds more than 15 m high and plazas on a rise surrounded by wetlands, picked out in lidar and then reached on foot along cut trails.

- **Balamkú ecological reserve, Campeche** (found here, confirmed; 18.80°N 89.90°W, give or take 30 km). INAH said that airborne laser scanning by the University of Houston helped the team spot many concentrations of pre-Hispanic structures. On the ground they found several pyramid-like buildings more than 15 m high and the many stone columns that gave the site its name, which means "stone column". Sources: [BBC News, "Maya civilisation: Archaeologists find ancient city in jungle" (2023)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-65974439); [Wikipedia, "Ocomtún"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocomt%C3%BAn).

#### PACUNAM lidar survey of the Maya lowlands (found)

In 2016 the Guatemalan foundation PACUNAM paid for the largest lidar survey then flown for archaeology: 2,144 square km of forest in northern Guatemala. The results, published in Science in 2018, showed that the Classic Maya lowlands were a network of densely settled, defended cities fed by intensive farming, and gave new population estimates for the region. *Classic Maya, about 250 to 900 CE; lidar flown 2016; published 2018.*

What to look for: House platforms in their tens of thousands, raised causeways, defensive walls and ditches, canals and field grids in swampy ground. Looters' trenches show too, as notches cut into mounds.

- **Northern Petén (Maya Biosphere Reserve)** (found here, confirmed; 17.49°N 89.09°W, give or take 120 km). The 2016 survey mapped the natural ground and the archaeological features across 2,144 square km of northern Guatemala. It gave population estimates, measures of farming and evidence of large works that reshaped the landscape. Sources: [M. Canuto, F. Estrada-Belli et al., "Ancient lowland Maya complexity as revealed by airborne laser scanning of northern Guatemala", Science 361 (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau0137).
- **Tikal (hidden Teotihuacan-style compound)** (found here, confirmed; 17.22°N 89.62°W, give or take 3 km). In the lidar of Tikal, one of the most studied Maya cities, researchers picked out a precinct that probably copies the Ciudadela and Temple of the Feathered Serpent at Teotihuacan, the great city of central Mexico. Sources: [S. Houston, E. Román Ramírez et al., "A Teotihuacan complex at the Classic Maya city of Tikal detected by lidar", Antiquity 95 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.140).

#### Poverty Point (found)

Poverty Point in Louisiana is a set of earthworks built by hunter-gatherers between 3,700 and 3,100 years ago: five mounds and six concentric half-rings of ridge around a plaza. People had farmed across the ridges for a century without seeing the pattern. Its design was recognised only when archaeologists looked at aerial photographs in the 1950s. *Built 3700 to 3100 BP (about 1700 to 1100 BCE); pattern recognised in the 1950s CE.*

What to look for: Curved ridges only a metre or two high and over a kilometre across: invisible from the ground, obvious from the air and in lidar hillshade.

- **Poverty Point** (found here, confirmed; 32.64°N 91.41°W, give or take 2 km). The complex has five mounds, six concentric semi-elliptical ridges and a central plaza, made and used by a society of hunter fisher-gatherers. The ridges are so large that the geometric design was not recognised until researchers studied aerial photographs. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Monumental Earthworks of Poverty Point"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1435); [Wikipedia, "Poverty Point"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Point).

#### Qalatga Darband (found)

Qalatga Darband is a fortified town of the 2nd and 1st centuries BCE at a mountain pass in Iraqi Kurdistan, on a route from Mesopotamia into Iran. It was first noticed in declassified Corona spy satellite photographs of the 1960s. A British Museum team confirmed it from 2016 with drone photographs of crop marks, geophysics and excavation. *Mainly mid 2nd and 1st centuries BCE; investigated from 2016 CE.*

What to look for: Old spy satellite photos show sites before modern farming and building hid them. Here a large square feature in a Corona frame was followed up with drone images of crop marks, where wheat grows differently over buried walls.

- **Qalatga Darband** (found here, confirmed; 36.21°N 44.98°E, give or take 2 km). Excavation of a square feature seen in the Corona image, with crop marks recorded by drone and geophysical mapping, confirmed a large fortified building. Pottery shows the site was mainly lived in during the early Parthian period. Sources: [British Museum, "Iraq Scheme: Darband-i Rania"](https://www.britishmuseum.org/our-work/international/iraq-scheme/darband-i-rania-archaeological-project).

#### Roman forts in Cold War spy photographs (found)

In the 1920s Father Antoine Poidebard recorded hundreds of Roman forts in the Near East from the air and took them for a line of defence against attack from the east. In 2023 Jesse Casana's team searched declassified CORONA and HEXAGON spy satellite photographs and found 396 more. Their wide spread does not fit a single defensive line. *Roman period forts; study published 2023.*

What to look for: Square enclosures 50 to 200 m across with corner towers, pale against steppe soil in old black and white satellite film. Many have since been ploughed out or built over, so the old photographs are the only record.

- **Northern Fertile Crescent (Syria and Iraq)** (found here, confirmed; 33.40°N 38.75°E, give or take 400 km). Using declassified CORONA and HEXAGON images the authors identified a further 396 forts spread widely across the northern Fertile Crescent, and question Poidebard's idea that the forts formed a defensive line. Sources: [J. Casana, D. Goodman and C. Ferwerda, "A wall or a road? A remote sensing-based investigation of fortifications on Rome's eastern frontier", Antiquity 97 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2023.153).

#### Stonehenge Hidden Landscapes (found)

Stonehenge is the most studied monument in Britain, yet the fields around it were largely unmapped. The Stonehenge Hidden Landscapes Project surveyed them with fast ground-based geophysics. Among the finds is a ring of huge pits, each 20 m or more across and at least 5 m deep, around the henge at Durrington Walls. *Neolithic and Bronze Age monuments; project described 2012; pit ring published 2020.*

What to look for: Filled-in ditches, pits and post holes show as weak magnetic anomalies and radar reflections under grass. Very large features can be missed because they look like natural hollows until many are mapped together.

- **Durrington Walls pit ring** (found here, leading view; 51.19°N 1.79°W, give or take 3 km). A series of massive geophysical anomalies south of Durrington Walls, found in fluxgate gradiometer survey, proved on coring to be circular pits with a surface diameter of 20 m or more and a depth of at least 5 m. The authors read them as part of a late Neolithic circuit of pits around the henge. Against: The pits were tested by coring, not full excavation, and whether they form one planned monument has been questioned. Sources: [V. Gaffney et al., "A Massive, Late Neolithic Pit Structure associated with Durrington Walls Henge", Internet Archaeology 55 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.11141/ia.55.4); [C. Gaffney et al., "The Stonehenge Hidden Landscapes Project", Archaeological Prospection 19 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.1422).

#### Tongatapu lidar survey (found)

Tongatapu was the seat of an archaic state whose influence reached across the south-west Pacific between the 13th and 19th centuries CE. Lidar of the whole island has mapped thousands of earth mounds, roads and ditches. Phillip Parton and Geoffrey Clark used it to argue that the island was a low-density city long before Europeans arrived. *State of the 13th to 19th centuries CE; lidar study published 2024.*

What to look for: Round and rectangular earth mounds, some faced with coral slabs, sunken roads between raised banks, and the big ditch and bank of the royal centre at Mu'a. They are low and under gardens, so lidar counts what ground survey cannot.

- **Tongatapu** (found here, confirmed; 21.21°S 175.15°W, give or take 20 km). The study joins lidar mapping with methods from urban science to read the built landscape of Tongatapu, home of an archaic state, as low-density urbanisation. Sources: [P. Parton and G. Clark, "Low-Density Urbanisation: Prestate Settlement Growth in a Pacific Society", Journal of Archaeological Method and Theory 31 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10816-024-09647-8).

#### Tugunbulak and Tashbulak (found)

Tugunbulak and Tashbulak are two medieval towns high in the mountains of south-eastern Uzbekistan, at 2,000 to 2,300 m, where no one expected cities. A team led by Michael Frachetti and Farhod Maksudov mapped them with lidar carried on a drone. Tugunbulak covers 120 hectares, one of the largest highland towns known on the Silk Routes. *Medieval Silk Routes, about the 8th to 11th centuries CE; published 2024.*

What to look for: Grass-covered mounds and lumps on a mountain pasture. Centimetre-scale drone lidar, processed to pick out wall lines, shows fortification walls, a citadel, terraces and hundreds of buildings.

- **Tugunbulak** (found here, confirmed; 39.70°N 68.30°E, give or take 15 km). Drone lidar gave a detailed plan of monumental fortifications and buildings over 120 hectares, at an altitude where towns of this size were not thought to exist. Sources: [M. Frachetti et al., "Large-scale medieval urbanism traced by UAV-lidar in highland Central Asia", Nature 634 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08086-5).

#### Upano Valley sites (found)

In the Upano valley of Amazonian Ecuador, at the foot of the Andes, lidar revealed a dense system of towns built on earthen platforms and joined by wide straight roads. Excavation had gone on there for years; the lidar showed the whole pattern. People lived there from about 500 BCE to between 300 and 600 CE, which makes it the oldest urban network known in the Amazon. *About 500 BCE to 300 to 600 CE; published 2024.*

What to look for: Clusters of rectangular earth platforms around plazas, drained fields between them, and dug roads that run dead straight for kilometres across ravines.

- **Upano Valley** (found here, confirmed; 2.10°S 78.10°W, give or take 20 km). Fieldwork and lidar analysis showed clusters of monumental platforms, plazas and streets set among farm drainage, with a road system running over great distances. Sources: [S. Rostain et al., "Two thousand years of garden urbanism in the Upper Amazon", Science 383 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi6317).

#### Valeriana (found)

Valeriana is a Classic Maya city in Campeche, Mexico, that nobody had recorded. Luke Auld-Thomas, a doctoral student at Tulane, found it in 2024 in a lidar survey flown in 2013 by an environmental group to monitor forest. It has two centres with pyramids and plazas, causeways and a reservoir, and lies 15 minutes' walk from a main road. *Classic Maya; lidar flown 2013; reported 2024.*

What to look for: The lesson is where to look: in lidar already flown for forestry, carbon or flood mapping. In the ground model the city shows as pyramid mounds on plazas, house platforms, causeways and a dammed reservoir.

- **Valeriana (near Xpujil, Campeche)** (found here, confirmed; 18.90°N 89.30°W, give or take 20 km). In a 2013 forest monitoring lidar dataset of 122 square km, Auld-Thomas and Marcello Canuto counted 6,764 buildings across three areas. One held a city of about 16.6 square km with two monumental centres 2 km apart, not yet excavated, which they named Valeriana after a nearby lagoon. Sources: [L. Auld-Thomas, M. Canuto et al., "Running out of empty space: environmental lidar and the crowded ancient landscape of Campeche, Mexico", Antiquity 98 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2024.148); [BBC News, "PhD student finds lost city in Mexico jungle by accident" (2024)](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crmznzkly3go).

### Treasure

#### Amber Room (not found yet)

The Amber Room was a chamber lined with several tons of carved amber, given by Prussia to Peter the Great in 1716 and installed in the Catherine Palace near St Petersburg. German troops took it down in 1941 and put it on show in Königsberg Castle, where it was crated at the end of 1943 and never seen again after the castle was bombed in August 1944. Many think the amber burned in the castle; a replica was finished in 2003. *Made in the early 18th century; looted 1941; lost by 1945.*

What to look for: Amber burns, so what could remain is the stone mosaics that were set in the panels. One of the four, "Feel and Touch", turned up in Germany in 1997.

- **Catherine Palace, Tsarskoye Selo** (where it began, confirmed; 59.72°N 30.40°E, give or take 1 km). The room stood here from the 18th century until German soldiers took it down within 36 hours in 1941 and shipped it west in 27 crates. A reconstruction begun in 1979 and finished 25 years later is in the same hall. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "A Brief History of the Amber Room" (J. Blumberg, 2007)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/a-brief-history-of-the-amber-room-160940121/).
- **Königsberg Castle (Kaliningrad)** (search area, leading view; 54.71°N 20.51°E, give or take 2 km). The panels were reinstalled in the castle museum, and in late 1943 the museum director was advised to take them down and crate them. In August 1944 Allied bombing destroyed the city and left the castle museum in ruins, and there the trail ends. Against: The simplest reading is that the amber was destroyed in the castle, whose ruins the Soviet government demolished in 1968; no search has found the crates. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "A Brief History of the Amber Room" (J. Blumberg, 2007)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/a-brief-history-of-the-amber-room-160940121/); [Wikipedia, "Amber Room"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Room).
- **Wreck of the steamer Karlsruhe (off Ustka, Poland)** (proposed, plausible; 54.58°N 16.86°E, give or take 60 km). Divers of the Baltictech group found the wreck of the Karlsruhe in 2020. She sailed from Königsberg in 1945 with a heavy cargo and was sunk by Soviet aircraft; the divers saw military vehicles and many unopened crates in her holds and suggested the Amber Room could be among them. Against: Nothing from the Amber Room has been recovered from the wreck. Sources: [The Guardian, "Nazi shipwreck found off Poland may solve Amber Room mystery" (2020)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/oct/01/nazi-shipwreck-found-off-poland-may-solve-amber-room-mystery).

#### Bactrian Gold (Tillya Tepe) (found)

In 1978 the Soviet archaeologist Viktor Sarianidi dug six graves of nomad nobles at Tillya Tepe, "Golden Hill", in northern Afghanistan, and took out about 20,600 ornaments and other objects, most of gold, dating to around the 1st century BCE or CE. War followed and the hoard was thought lost or melted. In 2003 it was revealed that museum staff had hidden it in a vault under the presidential palace in Kabul and kept the secret through the civil war and Taliban rule. *Graves of about the 1st century BCE to 1st century CE; found 1978; rediscovered 2003.*

What to look for: A low mound on a plain, the ruin of a much older temple, into which the graves were cut. Such mounds are common and are being looted across the region; satellite images show the pits.

- **Tillya Tepe** (found here, confirmed; 36.70°N 65.79°E, give or take 2 km). Sarianidi published the find as "The Treasure of Golden Hill". The objects survived decades of war in a vault deep under the presidential palace, thanks to a few men who knew and said nothing, even at gunpoint; the store of 22,000 pieces was revealed in 2003. Sources: [V. Sarianidi, "The Treasure of Golden Hill", American Journal of Archaeology 84 (1980)](https://doi.org/10.2307/504260); [BBC News, "Afghan gold: How the country's heritage was saved" (2011)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-south-asia-12599726).

#### Beale ciphers (legend)

A pamphlet printed in Virginia in 1885 tells of a man named Thomas Beale who buried gold and silver in Bedford County about 1820 and left three coded messages. One, solved with the Declaration of Independence as its key, describes the hoard; the other two, said to give the place and the heirs, have never been read. Many cryptographers think the whole story is a hoax by the pamphlet's author. *Story set in 1819 to 1822; published 1885.*

What to look for: Nothing has ever been found. The tests are textual: analysis of the writing style points to the pamphlet's own author as the writer of the "Beale" letters.

- **Bedford County, Virginia** (search area, fringe; 37.31°N 79.53°W, give or take 30 km). The solved cipher says the vault is "in the county of Bedford, about four miles from Buford's", and treasure hunters have dug around the old Buford tavern site for over a century. Against: No treasure has been found, no record of Beale exists, and studies of the wording argue that the pamphlet is fiction. Sources: [The Beale Papers (1885), Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Beale_Papers); [Wikipedia, "Beale ciphers"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beale_ciphers).

#### Captain Kidd's treasure (found)

William Kidd, a privateer turned pirate, buried part of his loot on Gardiners Island off Long Island in 1699 as he sailed to New York to bargain for a pardon. The governor had it dug up within weeks and used it as evidence; Kidd was hanged in London in 1701. The rumour that he hid more has fed treasure hunts ever since. *Buried and recovered 1699 CE.*

What to look for: Documents, not maps: the inventory of what was dug up on Gardiners Island survives. Claims of more Kidd treasure have rested on metal bars and wrecks, which chemistry and timber dating can test.

- **Gardiners Island, New York** (found here, confirmed; 41.10°N 72.10°W, give or take 3 km). Kidd left part of his treasure on the island in 1699, hoping to use it to bargain. The Earl of Bellomont, the governor who had first commissioned him, had him arrested in Boston that July and sent for the treasure; the papers, with John Gardiner's statement, survive. Sources: [J. F. Jameson (ed.), Privateering and Piracy in the Colonial Period: Illustrative Documents (1923), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/24882/24882-h/24882-h.htm); [Wikipedia, "William Kidd"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kidd).
- **Île Sainte-Marie, Madagascar (2015 claim)** (claimed find, rejected; 16.90°S 49.90°E, give or take 10 km). In May 2015 the American explorer Barry Clifford raised a 50 kg bar from a wreck off this former pirate base and presented it to the president of Madagascar as silver from Kidd's ship Adventure Galley. Against: A UNESCO scientific and technical advisory body reported in July 2015 that tests showed the ingot was lead, not silver. Sources: [BBC News, "Pirate Captain Kidd's 'treasure' found in Madagascar" (2015)](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-africa-32621444); [Wikipedia, "William Kidd"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Kidd).

#### Copper Scroll treasure (not found yet)

Among the Dead Sea Scrolls is one unlike the rest: a list, punched into sheets of copper, of 64 places where gold and silver were hidden. Archaeologists found it in 1952 at the back of Cave 3 at Qumran. Scholars disagree on whether it records a real treasure, perhaps from the Jerusalem Temple, or an imaginary one; none of its hiding places has been identified for certain. *Written about the 1st century CE; found 1952.*

What to look for: The entries give directions such as "in the cistern", "under the steps", "at the tomb", with depths in cubits. Matching them needs ancient place names tied to real ruins, tombs and water systems around Jerusalem, Jericho and the Judaean desert.

- **Qumran, Cave 3 (where the scroll was found)** (where it began, confirmed; 31.74°N 35.46°E, give or take 3 km). The scroll, in two rolls of copper, was found on 14 March 1952 and is now in the Jordan Museum in Amman. It is a list of 64 places where items of gold and silver were buried or hidden. Sources: [J. Høgenhaven, The Cave 3 Copper Scroll: A Symbolic Journey (Brill, 2020), "Translation of 3Q15"](https://doi.org/10.1163/9789004429581_009); [Wikipedia, "Copper Scroll"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Scroll).

#### Florentine Diamond (found)

The Florentine Diamond is a 137 carat yellow stone that probably belonged to the Medici before it came to the Habsburgs. It vanished when the last emperor, Charles I, left Vienna about 1918, and for a century it was thought stolen or recut. In November 2025 members of the Habsburg family told the New York Times that it had lain all along in a bank vault in Canada, put there on the orders of Empress Zita. *Missing from about 1918; revealed November 2025.*

What to look for: A case solved by family papers, not by search. The lesson for other lost jewels is that "lost" can mean kept quiet in a safe deposit box.

- **Vienna (Habsburg treasury)** (where it began, confirmed; 48.21°N 16.37°E, give or take 2 km). The diamond was part of the Habsburg jewels in Vienna until the end of the monarchy. It disappeared when Charles I fled abroad about 1918. Sources: [Euronews, "Habsburg family's Florentine diamond found in bank vault after a century" (2025)](https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/06/habsburg-familys-florentine-diamond-found-in-bank-vault-after-a-century).
- **Bank vault in Canada (place not public)** (found here, leading view; 56.00°N 109.00°W, give or take 1500 km). Three Habsburg relatives confirmed to the New York Times in 2025 that the stone had been kept in a Canadian bank vault for a century on Empress Zita's orders. The family said the jewels should now be exhibited. Against: The account comes from the family through the press; the dataset has not found a published gemmological report. Sources: [Euronews, "Habsburg family's Florentine diamond found in bank vault after a century" (2025)](https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/06/habsburg-familys-florentine-diamond-found-in-bank-vault-after-a-century).

#### Forrest Fenn's treasure (found)

Forrest Fenn, an art dealer in Santa Fe, said he hid a chest of gold and jewels in the Rocky Mountains and put the clues in a 24-line poem in his 2010 memoir. Hundreds of thousands of people searched, and at least four died. In June 2020 Fenn announced that it had been found; the finder, Jack Stuef, said it was in Wyoming and has not revealed the spot. *Hidden about 2010; found June 2020.*

What to look for: This was a puzzle hunt, solved by reading the poem, not by survey. It is in the dataset as a rare modern case of a hidden treasure that was real and was found.

- **Wyoming (exact spot not public)** (found here, confirmed; 43.00°N 107.50°W, give or take 300 km). Fenn confirmed the find in June 2020 and died that September. The finder was later named as Jack Stuef, who has refused to say where in Wyoming the chest lay. Sources: [The Guardian, "$1m treasure in Rocky Mountains has been found, says Forrest Fenn" (2020)](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2020/jun/07/forrest-fenn-treasure-rocky-mountains-found); [Wikipedia, "Fenn treasure"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenn_treasure).

#### Hoxne Hoard (found)

The Hoxne Hoard is the largest hoard of late Roman gold and silver found in Britain: 14,865 coins with jewellery, spoons and tableware, packed in a wooden chest and buried in Suffolk in the early 5th century. Eric Lawes found it in 1992 with a metal detector while looking for a friend's lost hammer. He reported it at once, and archaeologists excavated the whole hoard in a single day. *Buried after 407 CE; found 16 November 1992.*

What to look for: A tight cluster of coins and plate that kept the shape of the rotted box around it. Because the finder left it in the ground, excavators could record the packing, the box fittings and even traces of straw and cloth.

- **Hoxne, Suffolk** (found here, confirmed; 52.35°N 1.20°E, give or take 3 km). Lawes was hunting for a hammer dropped in a muddy field when his detector found the hoard, the largest treasure of late Roman gold and silver from Britain. Sources: [The Guardian, "Golden hoard and silver too at British Museum" (2003)](https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2003/nov/15/arts.education); [Review of C. Johns, The Hoxne Late Roman Treasure: Gold Jewellery and Silver Plate (British Museum Press, 2010), American Journal of Archaeology 115 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.3764/ajaonline1152.gerrard).

#### King Solomon's Mines (legend)

The Bible credits Solomon with great wealth but names no mines; the phrase comes from H. Rider Haggard's adventure novel of 1885, set in southern Africa. In the 1930s the archaeologist Nelson Glueck linked the copper district of Timna, in the Arabah valley, to Solomon. Radiocarbon dating has since shown that copper was indeed produced there on an industrial scale in the 10th century BCE, though by whom is argued. *Solomon is dated to the 10th century BCE; the novel is from 1885 CE.*

What to look for: Black slag heaps, smelting furnaces, mine shafts and walled camps in a desert valley, plain in satellite images. Short-lived seeds and charcoal inside the slag layers give the dates.

- **Timna Valley** (proposed, plausible; 29.79°N 34.99°E, give or take 5 km). The Timna mines were long dated to the Egyptian New Kingdom. Erez Ben-Yosef's team redated the main smelting camps and showed that work peaked in the Iron Age, around the 10th century BCE. Against: The dates fit Solomon's century but nothing links the mines to Jerusalem; they may have been run by the local kingdom of Edom. Sources: [E. Ben-Yosef et al., "A New Chronological Framework for Iron Age Copper Production at Timna (Israel)", BASOR 367 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.5615/bullamerschoorie.367.0031); [Wikipedia, "Timna Valley"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timna_Valley).
- **Khirbat en-Nahas, Jordan** (proposed, plausible; 30.68°N 35.44°E, give or take 3 km). Thomas Levy's excavation of this very large smelting site in the Faynan district produced high-precision radiocarbon dates showing industrial copper production in the 10th and 9th centuries BCE. Against: As at Timna, who controlled the work is disputed. Sources: [T. Levy, T. Higham et al., "High-precision radiocarbon dating and historical biblical archaeology in southern Jordan", PNAS 105 (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0804950105).

#### Montezuma's treasure (legend)

When the Spanish fled Tenochtitlan on the night of 30 June 1520, they carried the gold they had taken from the palace of the emperor Moctezuma, and much of it was lost in the canals as they were cut down. Legends say the Aztecs hid the rest. One piece of the lost loot is real: a gold bar found in Mexico City in 1981, which tests in 2020 matched to that night. *Lost on the Noche Triste, 30 June 1520 CE.*

What to look for: The Spanish melted Aztec goldwork into bars for carrying, and such a bar, from the bed of an old canal on the line of their retreat, is what was found. Analysis of its mix of gold, silver and copper lets it be compared with dated Aztec finds.

- **Mexico City (the 1981 gold bar)** (found here, confirmed; 19.43°N 99.13°W, give or take 3 km). A gold bar weighing 1.93 kg was found in Mexico City in 1981, where a canal of Tenochtitlan once ran. In 2020 Mexico's heritage institute INAH reported that its composition matches other Aztec gold of the period and confirmed it as loot dropped in the Spanish retreat of 1520. Sources: [AFP via Phys.org, "Gold bar found in Mexico was Aztec treasure: study" (2020)](https://phys.org/news/2020-01-gold-bar-mexico-aztec-treasure.html).

#### Nazi gold train (legend)

A local legend in Lower Silesia says that in 1945 a German armoured train full of gold and looted goods was hidden in a tunnel near Wałbrzych. In August 2015 two men said they had found it with ground-penetrating radar, and a Polish deputy minister said he was "99% convinced". A university survey that December found no train, and a dig in 2016 hit only natural ground. *Said to have been hidden in 1945; claim made 2015; dig 2016.*

What to look for: A buried train would be a huge mass of iron and should show strongly in magnetic survey, not only as a radar reflection. The geological survey by the Kraków team found no train, and the dig showed the radar "image" was natural ground.

- **Wałbrzych** (claimed find, rejected; 50.77°N 16.28°E, give or take 5 km). Piotr Koper and Andreas Richter told the authorities in 2015 that they knew the place and had radar images of a train with gun turrets. Deputy culture minister Piotr Żuchowski said the images had left him 99% sure. Against: A geological survey by Professor Janusz Madej of the AGH University of Science and Technology in Kraków found no train, and the excavation of 2016 was stopped when the anomaly proved to be natural geology. Sources: [BBC News, "Nazi gold train: 'No evidence' of discovery in Poland" (2015)](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-europe-35104117); [Wikipedia, "Nazi gold train"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_gold_train).

#### Oak Island Money Pit (legend)

In 1795 a boy named Daniel McGinnis found a hollow in the ground on Oak Island, Nova Scotia, and with two friends dug into what seemed to be a filled-in shaft. Syndicates have dug there ever since, in the belief that pirates, Templars or others buried something below. The digging has cratered the island and cost lives, and no treasure has been found. *Digging began in 1795 CE and continues.*

What to look for: Two centuries of shafts, tunnels and backfill have destroyed any original layers, so nothing dug up now can be tied to an early deposit. The island lies over water-filled cavities in anhydrite rock, which may explain both the "pit" and its flooding as natural sinkholes.

- **Oak Island, Nova Scotia** (search area, fringe; 44.51°N 64.29°W, give or take 1 km). The first diggers reported a shaft with layers of logs. Later companies reported flood tunnels from the shore and scraps of chain and parchment brought up by drills. Against: Four treasure hunters died in an accident in 1965, millions have been spent, and no treasure has been found. Sources: [The Canadian Encyclopedia, "Oak Island"](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/oak-island); [Wikipedia, "Oak Island mystery"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Island_mystery).

#### Panagyurishte Treasure (found)

Three brothers digging clay for bricks near Panagyurishte, Bulgaria, in December 1949 turned up nine vessels of solid gold: a great dish, an amphora with centaur handles and seven drinking horns shaped as animal heads and Amazons. They are thought to be a Thracian royal ceremonial set of about 300 BCE, 6.164 kg of gold in all. *Made about the turn of the 4th and 3rd centuries BCE; found 1949 CE.*

What to look for: A single buried cache with no building or grave around it, found by chance. Hoards like this are hidden in a hurry in open ground and leave nothing to see.

- **Panagyurishte** (found here, confirmed; 42.50°N 24.18°E, give or take 3 km). The set was found at the Merul tile works near the town on 8 December 1949 by the brothers Pavel, Petko and Michail Deikov. Sources: [Review of I. Venedikov, The Panagyurishte Gold Treasure (Sofia, 1961), Journal of Hellenic Studies 84 (1964)](https://doi.org/10.2307/627788); [Wikipedia, "Panagyurishte Treasure"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagyurishte_Treasure).

#### Priam's Treasure (found)

Heinrich Schliemann found a cache of gold diadems, cups and jewellery at Troy in 1873 and named it after Homer's King Priam, though it came from Troy II, a level many centuries older than any Trojan War. He smuggled it out of the Ottoman Empire, and it went to Berlin, where it vanished when the Red Army took the city. In 1994 the Pushkin Museum in Moscow admitted that it had the gold. *Early Bronze Age (Troy II); found 1873 CE; lost 1945; revealed 1994.*

What to look for: The gold has been found twice. What is still argued is where exactly on the mound Schliemann dug it up and whether it was one deposit, which depends on his notebooks and plans.

- **Troy (where it was found)** (where it began, confirmed; 39.96°N 26.24°E, give or take 1 km). Schliemann found the hoard in 1873 beside the wall of the second city of Troy, an Early Bronze Age level. Sources: [D. F. Easton, "Priam's Gold: the Full Story", Anatolian Studies 44 (1994)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3642994); [Archaeology, "Calvert's Heirs Claim Schliemann Treasure" (1996)](https://archive.archaeology.org/9601/newsbriefs/calvert.html).
- **Pushkin Museum, Moscow (where it is now)** (found here, confirmed; 55.75°N 37.61°E, give or take 1 km). The gold was believed lost with the fall of Berlin until it was shown to be in the Pushkin Museum, where foreign archaeologists saw the Trojan objects in 1994. Sources: [Archaeology, "Calvert's Heirs Claim Schliemann Treasure" (1996)](https://archive.archaeology.org/9601/newsbriefs/calvert.html); [Wikipedia, "Priam's Treasure"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priam's_Treasure).

#### Staffordshire Hoard (found)

The Staffordshire Hoard is the largest find of Anglo-Saxon gold and silver metalwork ever made: more than 1,500 pieces at first count, almost all of them fittings stripped from swords and war gear of the 7th century. Terry Herbert found it with a metal detector in a ploughed field near Hammerwich in July 2009. It was buried in the kingdom of Mercia, for reasons nobody knows. *Buried in the 7th century CE; found July 2009.*

What to look for: Small gold and garnet fittings scattered in plough soil over a few square metres. A metal detector found them; archaeologists then dug the spot and later re-surveyed the field, finding more pieces in 2012.

- **Field near Hammerwich, Staffordshire** (found here, confirmed; 52.67°N 1.90°W, give or take 3 km). Herbert, an amateur detectorist, found the first pieces in a farm field in July 2009; the hoard holds about 5 kg of gold, three times the amount in the Sutton Hoo ship burial. A second search of the same field in 2012 found more fragments. Sources: [The Guardian, "Largest ever hoard of Anglo-Saxon gold found in Staffordshire" (2009)](https://www.theguardian.com/uk/2009/sep/24/anglo-saxon-treasure-hoard-gold-staffordshire-metal-detector); [BBC News, "Staffordshire Hoard: Gold fragments found in Hammerwich" (2012)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-stoke-staffordshire-20764132).

#### Treasure of Lima (legend)

The story goes that in 1820, with rebel armies closing on Lima, the Spanish authorities loaded the city's church and state treasure on to the ship Mary Dear, and that her captain, William Thompson, killed the guards, sailed to Cocos Island off Costa Rica and buried it. Expeditions have gone to the island to dig since at least the 1850s and none is known to have found it. No document from the time proves the treasure was ever shipped. *Said to have been buried in 1820 CE.*

What to look for: The island is steep, wet and prone to landslides, so a pit dug in 1820 would be hard to find and easy to lose. It is now a national park where treasure hunting needs permission.

- **Cocos Island** (search area, fringe; 5.53°N 87.06°W, give or take 5 km). Cocos Island, far off the Pacific coast of Costa Rica, is where the legend puts the hoard. UNESCO notes that the island is also known as Treasure Island and that pirates, whalers and sailors long used it for shelter and water. Against: Generations of searches have recovered nothing, and the story rests on tales told long afterwards. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Cocos Island National Park"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/820); [Wikipedia, "Treasure of Lima"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_of_Lima).

#### Yamashita's gold (legend)

A persistent story in the Philippines says that Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita buried a vast hoard of looted gold in caves and tunnels there before their surrender in 1945. It has drawn treasure hunters for eighty years, some of whom have died digging. Most experts and Filipino historians say there is no credible evidence that the hoard exists. *Said to have been buried in 1944 to 1945.*

What to look for: Wartime tunnels and caves are real and show in lidar and ground radar, but so do unexploded bombs. No survey has produced evidence of bullion.

- **Luzon** (search area, fringe; 16.00°N 121.00°E, give or take 300 km). Most digging has been in the mountains and old military sites of Luzon, where Yamashita's army made its last stand. Against: Historians such as Ricardo Jose of the University of the Philippines doubt the story, and the digging is dangerous: in 1998 a wartime Japanese bomb killed five treasure hunters who had dug it up. Sources: [BBC News, "WWII Japanese bomb kills Philippines treasure hunters" (1998)](https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/asia-pacific/68345.stm); [Wikipedia, "Yamashita's gold"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamashita's_gold).

### Shipwrecks

#### 1715 Treasure Fleet (found)

Eleven ships of a Spanish treasure fleet bound from Havana to Spain were driven on to the east coast of Florida by a hurricane in July 1715. Spain salvaged much of the cargo at the time. The treasure hunter Kip Wagner found the first of what was left in 1961, and the wrecks gave this stretch of shore the name Treasure Coast. *Wrecked 31 July 1715; treasure found again from 1961.*

What to look for: Piles of ballast stones and iron cannon close inshore, in the surf, with coins spread through the sand around them. Storms uncover and bury them; beach finds after storms pointed to each wreck.

- **Treasure Coast, Florida (Sebastian to Fort Pierce)** (found here, confirmed; 27.70°N 80.40°W, give or take 60 km). All eleven ships of the fleet were lost in a hurricane along the east coast of Florida on 31 July 1715. The Urca de Lima, a flat-bottomed storeship, is the best known of the wrecks. Sources: [University of South Florida, Florida Center for Instructional Technology, "Florida's Shipwrecks and Treasures"](https://fcit.usf.edu/florida/lessons/shipwrecks/shipwrecks.htm); [Wikipedia, "1715 Treasure Fleet"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1715_Treasure_Fleet).

#### Antikythera wreck (found)

In 1900 sponge divers sheltering at the Greek island of Antikythera found a Roman period ship on the seabed, loaded with bronze and marble statues. Among the finds was a lump of corroded bronze gears, the Antikythera mechanism, a geared device that modelled the motions of the sun and moon and predicted eclipses. It is technically more complex than any device known for at least a thousand years afterwards. *Sank in the 1st century BCE; found 1900 CE.*

What to look for: Statues, pottery and hull timbers on a steep rocky slope at the limit of early diving. Pottery in the cargo dates the voyage, and X-ray tomography reads the text inside the mechanism.

- **Antikythera wreck** (found here, confirmed; 35.89°N 23.31°E, give or take 1 km). Sponge divers discovered the wreck off Point Glyphadia in 1900. It gave up statues, coins and the remains of the mechanism, which X-ray study shows was an astronomical calculator that could predict eclipses. Sources: [T. Freeth et al., "Decoding the ancient Greek astronomical calculator known as the Antikythera Mechanism", Nature 444 (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05357); [T. Freeth, "Eclipse Prediction on the Ancient Greek Astronomical Calculating Machine Known as the Antikythera Mechanism", PLOS ONE 9 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0103275).

#### Batavia (found)

The Dutch East India Company ship Batavia struck a reef in the Houtman Abrolhos, off Western Australia, in June 1629. While her commander sailed for help, a group of mutineers murdered more than 100 of the survivors on the islands. A lobster fisherman first sighted the wreck in 1963, it was excavated in the 1970s, and graves of the victims are still being found on Beacon Island. *Wrecked 4 June 1629; found 1963.*

What to look for: Cannon, anchors and a section of hull under coral on a reef flat, and on the nearby islands shallow graves and the stone shelters of the survivors. Old journals gave the latitude; local fishermen knew the reef.

- **Morning Reef and Beacon Island, Houtman Abrolhos** (found here, confirmed; 28.49°S 113.79°E, give or take 3 km). The ship was wrecked on the reef in 1629. Archaeologists working on Beacon Island have uncovered burials from the massacre that followed, in which more than 100 survivors were killed by mutineers. Sources: [J. Green, "The VOC ship Batavia wrecked in 1629 on the Houtman Abrolhos, Western Australia" (1975)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.1975.tb00902.x); [BBC News, "Australia dig unearths Batavia mutiny skeleton" (2015)](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-australia-31123952).

#### Battleship Musashi (found)

Musashi and her sister Yamato were the largest battleships ever built. American carrier aircraft sank Musashi in the Sibuyan Sea, in the Philippines, in October 1944 during the Battle of Leyte Gulf. In March 2015 Paul Allen announced that his team had found the wreck on the sea floor after a search of eight years. *Sunk October 1944; found March 2015.*

What to look for: A very large steel hull broken into pieces on a deep sea floor. Historical records and seabed mapping narrowed the area; an autonomous vehicle and then a remotely operated vehicle with cameras confirmed her by the chrysanthemum crest on the bow.

- **Sibuyan Sea** (found here, confirmed; 12.67°N 122.50°E, give or take 80 km). Pictures taken on the floor of the Sibuyan Sea by a team from Allen's vessel Octopus showed the bow with the chrysanthemum crest of the Japanese imperial family. Sources: [The Guardian / AFP, "US billionaire Paul Allen discovers wreck of Japan's biggest warship Musashi" (2015)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/mar/04/us-billionaire-paul-allen-discovers-wreck-of-japans-biggest-warship-musashi).

#### Belitung shipwreck (found)

A ship of the 9th century CE, found off the Indonesian island of Belitung in 1998, carried some 60,000 pieces of Chinese pottery with gold and silver. Her planks were sewn together in the manner of Arab and Indian ships, the first hard evidence that such ships sailed direct between the Gulf and China. The cargo was raised by a commercial company, and the way it was dug drew criticism that led a museum to postpone its exhibition. *Sank after 826 CE (a date written on one bowl); found 1998.*

What to look for: A low mound of stacked ceramics on a shallow reef flat, with hull planks pierced by stitch holes and no iron nails. In these waters fishermen find most wrecks, and cargoes are often sold before archaeologists hear of them.

- **Off Belitung island (Gelasa Strait)** (found here, confirmed; 2.86°S 107.92°E, give or take 40 km). The well preserved wreck was excavated in 1998 and 1999. Her planks were joined by stitching, and the timber is of Arabian and Indian kinds; the cargo was almost all Chinese ceramics, mostly from the Changsha kilns. Sources: [M. Flecker, "A ninth-century AD Arab or Indian shipwreck in Indonesia: First evidence for direct trade with China", World Archaeology 32 (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00438240120048662); [Wikipedia, "Belitung shipwreck"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belitung_shipwreck).

#### Bom Jesus (Oranjemund shipwreck) (found)

The Bom Jesus was a Portuguese ship bound for India that left Lisbon in March 1533 and disappeared. In April 2008 a worker at a diamond mine on the coast of Namibia found copper ingots and ivory in sand that had been sea floor until the mine walled it off: her cargo of copper, elephant tusks and more than 2,000 gold and silver coins. It is the oldest known shipwreck found on the west coast of sub-Saharan Africa. *Sailed 7 March 1533; found 1 April 2008.*

What to look for: A wreck buried in beach sand, found only because miners had drained the shore behind a sea wall. A trident-shaped mark stamped on the copper ingots and the dates of the coins pointed to the ship.

- **Oranjemund, Namibia (restricted mining area)** (found here, confirmed; 28.55°S 16.43°E, give or take 30 km). Kaapanda Shadika, a worker for the mining company Namdeb, found the first ingots and tusks on 1 April 2008. The rescue dig recovered 1,845 copper ingots, over 2,000 gold and silver coins and more than 100 elephant tusks. Sources: [E. Mowa, W. Engelbrecht et al., "Challenges in Rescue Excavation and Long-Term Conservation: The Case of the Oranjemund (Bom Jesus) Shipwreck, Namibia" (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10761-026-00830-z); [Wikipedia, "Bom Jesus (ship)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bom_Jesus_(ship)).

#### Bonhomme Richard (not found yet)

Bonhomme Richard was the ship in which John Paul Jones fought HMS Serapis off Flamborough Head, Yorkshire, on 23 September 1779, the most famous sea fight of the American Revolution. Jones won, took the British ship, and watched his own sink a day and a half later. Searches have gone on for decades, and a find announced off Filey in 2018 has not been confirmed. *Sank 25 September 1779 CE.*

What to look for: The remains of a burned wooden ship of the 1700s on a seabed crowded with later wrecks and scoured by trawls: iron guns, ballast and concretions. Each candidate needs guns or fittings that can be dated to her French origin.

- **Off Filey and Flamborough Head, Yorkshire** (claimed find, plausible; 54.21°N 0.29°W, give or take 30 km). The land and sea search firm Merlin Burrows announced in December 2018 that it had found the remains of the ship off the coast at Filey, closer inshore than earlier searches had looked. Against: No independent body has confirmed the identification, and earlier surveys had pointed to other wreck sites further out off Flamborough Head. Sources: [BBC News, "Remains of USS Bonhomme Richard found off Yorkshire coast" (2018)](https://www.bbc.com/news/av/uk-england-york-north-yorkshire-46474363/remains-of-uss-bonhomme-richard-found-off-yorkshire-coast); [Wikipedia, "USS Bonhomme Richard (1765)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Bonhomme_Richard_(1765)).

#### Clotilda (found)

The schooner Clotilda carried captive Africans from Dahomey to Alabama in 1860, long after the United States had banned the import of slaves, and her captain had her burned and sunk in the Mobile River delta to hide the crime. The freed survivors founded Africatown, and their descendants kept the story. The Alabama Historical Commission confirmed in May 2019 that the wreck had been found. *Burned and sunk 1860; wreck confirmed May 2019.*

What to look for: A burned wooden hull in river mud, one of many abandoned vessels in the delta. Sonar and magnetometer found candidates; the ship's recorded size, her rare hull design, the timber and nails, and signs of fire picked out the right one.

- **Mobile River delta, Alabama** (found here, confirmed; 30.66°N 88.03°W, give or take 20 km). A wreck that a reporter found in 2018 proved too large to be the Clotilda, but the search it set off led archaeologists from the Alabama Historical Commission and SEARCH Inc. to the real ship, which they authenticated in 2019. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "The 'Clotilda,' the Last Known Slave Ship to Arrive in the U.S., Is Found" (2019)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/clotilda-last-known-slave-ship-arrive-us-found-180972177/); [US National Park Service, "Twelvemile Island Shipwreck"](https://www.nps.gov/articles/twelvemile-island-shipwreck.htm).

#### Endurance (found)

Endurance was the ship of Ernest Shackleton's Antarctic expedition. Sea ice in the Weddell Sea crushed her and she sank in 1915; Shackleton and his men escaped on foot and in small boats. In March 2022 an expedition found her almost intact, 3,008 m deep. *Sank 1915; found March 2022.*

What to look for: An intact wooden ship on the deep sea floor under sea ice, found by submersibles launched from an icebreaker that swept a set search area. No wood-eating worms live there, which is why she is whole.

- **Weddell Sea** (found here, confirmed; 68.70°S 52.44°W, give or take 10 km). The Falklands Maritime Heritage Trust mounted the search from the South African icebreaker Agulhas II, and its submersibles combed a set search area for more than two weeks before finding the ship 3,008 m down. The wreck is a designated monument under the Antarctic Treaty and must not be disturbed. Sources: [BBC News, "Endurance: Shackleton's lost ship is found in Antarctic" (2022)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-60662541).

#### Esmeralda (Vasco da Gama's fleet) (found)

Esmeralda and São Pedro were two ships of Vasco da Gama's second fleet to India, commanded by his uncles Vicente and Brás Sodré. A storm wrecked them in May 1503 at an island off the coast of Oman, where they had gone to raid Arab shipping. A wreck site found there in 1998 and excavated in 2013 and 2014 is identified as probably Esmeralda. *Wrecked May 1503; found 1998; excavated from 2013.*

What to look for: No hull, only heavy things caught in gullies of a shallow rocky bay: stone shot, lead and copper-alloy objects, coins and a ship's bell. Scientific dating and study of the finds made the identification.

- **Al Hallaniyah island, Oman** (found here, leading view; 17.51°N 56.02°E, give or take 10 km). David Mearns and Oman's Ministry of Heritage and Culture report a wreck off the north-east coast of the island that fits the ships lost there in May 1503, and identify it from its finds as Vicente Sodré's Esmeralda. Against: The paper is an interim report; the authors give the identification as probable, not proved. Sources: [D. Mearns, D. Parham and B. Frohlich, "A Portuguese East Indiaman from the 1502-1503 Fleet of Vasco da Gama off Al Hallaniyah Island, Oman: an interim report", International Journal of Nautical Archaeology 45 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1095-9270.12175).

#### Galleon San José (found)

The San José was the flagship of a Spanish treasure fleet, carrying gold, silver, gems and jewellery from the South American colonies, when a British squadron sank her off Cartagena in June 1708. Almost all of her crew of about 600 died. Colombia announced in December 2015 that the wreck had been found, and has kept the position secret while Colombia, Spain, a salvage company and indigenous groups dispute who owns the cargo. *Sunk 8 June 1708; found November 2015.*

What to look for: A scatter of bronze cannon, ceramics and coins on the seabed 600 m down. She was found with a REMUS 6000 autonomous underwater vehicle running side-scan sonar, and identified from photographs of her cannon.

- **Off Cartagena, near the Rosario Islands (position secret)** (found here, confirmed; 10.18°N 75.75°W, give or take 50 km). President Juan Manuel Santos announced the find on 5 December 2015; the wreck had been located the month before, 600 m down, by a REMUS 6000 robot submarine operated by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. It is said to lie close to the Rosario Islands, and officials will not reveal the position. Sources: [BBC News, "Colombia treasure-laden San Jose galleon 'is found'" (2015)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-latin-america-35014600); [BBC Travel, "A shipwreck worth billions off the coast of Cartagena" (2019)](https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20190908-a-shipwreck-worth-billions-off-the-coast-of-cartagena).

#### HMS Erebus and HMS Terror (found)

Sir John Franklin sailed in 1845 with two ships and 129 men to find the Northwest Passage, and none came back. Searches went on for 170 years. Parks Canada found Erebus in 2014 with side-scan sonar, in the area where Inuit testimony had always said a ship sank, and Terror was found in 2016 in Terror Bay after a tip from an Inuk crew member. *Abandoned 1848; Erebus found September 2014; Terror found September 2016.*

What to look for: Two wooden ships sitting upright in cold Arctic water. Side-scan sonar towed in the few ice-free weeks found the first; Inuit oral history said where to tow it.

- **HMS Erebus (Wilmot and Crampton Bay)** (found here, confirmed; 68.18°N 98.75°W, give or take 30 km). Sonar images from the waters off King William Island showed a ship on the sea floor in September 2014; it was identified as Erebus. The site is the first national historic site managed jointly by Inuit and Parks Canada. Sources: [BBC News, "Sir John Franklin: Fabled Arctic ship found" (2014)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-us-canada-29131757); [Parks Canada, "Wrecks of HMS Erebus and HMS Terror National Historic Site"](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nu/epaveswrecks).
- **HMS Terror (Terror Bay)** (found here, confirmed; 68.90°N 98.95°W, give or take 30 km). A team from the Arctic Research Foundation found Terror on 3 September 2016 in the middle of Terror Bay, on the south-west coast of King William Island, with her hatches closed. They went there on the word of an Inuk crew member who had once seen a mast standing out of the ice. Sources: [The Guardian, "Ship found in Arctic 168 years after doomed Northwest Passage attempt" (P. Watson, 2016)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2016/sep/12/hms-terror-wreck-found-arctic-nearly-170-years-northwest-passage-attempt).

#### HMS Hood (found)

The battlecruiser HMS Hood, for twenty years the pride of the Royal Navy, blew up and sank in the Denmark Strait on 24 May 1941 under the guns of the German battleship Bismarck. Of 1,415 men on board, three lived. The wreck was found in 2001, 2,800 m down, and her bell was raised in 2015 as a memorial. *Sunk 24 May 1941; found 2001.*

What to look for: Wreckage in several large pieces spread over a wide debris field, which itself records how the ship broke up. It was found by deep-sea survey and filmed by a remotely operated vehicle, and it is a protected war grave.

- **Denmark Strait, between Greenland and Iceland** (found here, confirmed; 67.00°N 24.00°W, give or take 150 km). The deep-sea search company Blue Water Recoveries found the wreck in 2001, 2,800 m under water. The bell was seen lying on the seabed away from the hull, and a first attempt to recover it in 2012 was called off. Sources: [BBC News, "Recovery of HMS Hood's ship's bell abandoned" (2012)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-hampshire-19479556); [Wikipedia, "HMS Hood"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Hood).

#### Kublai Khan's lost fleet (found)

Kublai Khan sent fleets against Japan in 1274 and 1281, and Japanese tradition says a divine wind, the kamikaze, destroyed them. For a long time only the legend remained. Underwater archaeologists at the island of Takashima have since found the fleet's weapons, ceramic bombs and broken ship timbers. *Fleet lost in the storm of 1281 CE.*

What to look for: Timbers, stone-and-wood anchors, helmets and round ceramic shells buried a metre or more deep in bay mud. Sub-bottom profilers find the buried shapes; divers then dig by hand in near darkness.

- **Takashima, Imari Bay** (found here, confirmed; 33.43°N 129.77°E, give or take 5 km). Underwater archaeologists led by Kenzo Hayashida excavated broken remains of the invasion fleet off Takashima, including a Mongol helmet, iron arrow tips and a tetsuhau, a ceramic bomb, the first proof that such weapons existed so early. Later surveys have searched the bay floor for the ships themselves. Sources: [Archaeology (Archaeological Institute of America), "Relics of the Kamikaze" (J. Delgado, 2003)](https://archive.archaeology.org/0301/etc/kamikaze.html); [Y. Ikeda (Yoshifumi), R. Sasaki and J. Kimura, "Recent Underwater Investigations at Takashima: Searching for the Lost Fleet of the Mongol Empire", Current Science 117 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.18520/cs/v117/i10/1635-1639).

#### Mary Rose (found)

The Mary Rose was a warship of Henry VIII that sank in the Solent on 19 July 1545, in sight of the king, as she went out to fight a French invasion fleet. Divers salvaged guns from her in the 1830s and she was lost again. She was found in 1971 after years of search, and in 1982 half of her hull, kept whole by the silt, was raised. *Sank 19 July 1545; found 1971; raised 11 October 1982.*

What to look for: A wooden hull buried on its side in soft mud, so that only the side that sank into the silt survives. A sonar scan by Harold Edgerton in 1967 and 1968 showed a buried feature before divers found timbers.

- **Mary Rose Museum, Portsmouth (the Solent wreck site is close by)** (found here, confirmed; 50.80°N 1.11°W, give or take 5 km). The wreck was located in 1971 and raised on 11 October 1982 by the Mary Rose Trust. The hull and thousands of objects found in her are in the museum at Portsmouth Historic Dockyard. Sources: [Mary Rose Trust, "History of the Mary Rose"](https://maryrose.org/discover/history/); [Wikipedia, "Mary Rose"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Rose).

#### Merchant Royal (not found yet)

The Merchant Royal was an English ship that sank in rough weather off Land's End on 23 September 1641, when her pumps failed on the way home from Cádiz with bullion on board. She has not been found. When a salvage firm raised half a million coins from a secret Atlantic site in 2007, many guessed it was her; it was a Spanish frigate. *Sunk 23 September 1641 CE.*

What to look for: A 17th century wooden hull is long gone; what is left is anchors, iron guns and a low mound of concreted cargo. The western approaches to the Channel are deep, tidal and crowded with later wrecks, so each sonar target needs a camera.

- **Atlantic off the Isles of Scilly and Land's End** (search area, plausible; 49.92°N 6.30°W, give or take 80 km). The shipwreck historian Richard Larn told the BBC in 2007 that the Merchant Royal, a Dartmouth-based ship laden with bullion, sank off the Isles of Scilly in 1641. Against: The sinking position is known only roughly from the accounts of survivors, and no wreck has been identified. Sources: [BBC News, "Record wreck 'found off Cornwall'" (2007)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/england/cornwall/6671975.stm); [Wikipedia, "Merchant Royal"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Royal).

#### Nuestra Señora de Atocha (found)

The Atocha was a galleon of the Spanish treasure fleet that sank in a hurricane off the Florida Keys in September 1622, on the way home from Havana. Spanish salvors never found her. The American treasure hunter Mel Fisher searched for sixteen years and found the main part of her cargo in 1985, after a long court fight with the State of Florida over who owned it. *Sunk 6 September 1622; main cargo found 1985.*

What to look for: A trail of ballast stones, silver bars and coins scattered for miles across sand, because storms broke the hull and dragged it. Towed magnetometers found the iron of her anchors and guns; the trail then led to the main pile.

- **Off the Marquesas Keys, Florida** (found here, confirmed; 24.57°N 82.12°W, give or take 40 km). The Tierra Firme fleet of 1622 was caught by a hurricane and the Atocha and the Santa Margarita went down, with 380 lives lost. In 1985 the salvager Mel Fisher found the Atocha in the Florida Keys with tons of treasure. Sources: [University of South Florida, Florida Center for Instructional Technology, "Florida's Shipwrecks and Treasures"](https://fcit.usf.edu/florida/lessons/shipwrecks/shipwrecks.htm); [Wikipedia, "Nuestra Señora de Atocha"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuestra_Se%C3%B1ora_de_Atocha).

#### Nuestra Señora de las Mercedes ("Black Swan") (found)

The Spanish frigate Nuestra Señora de las Mercedes blew up in a British attack off the south coast of Portugal in October 1804, with the loss of about 250 lives and half a million silver and gold coins. In 2007 the American firm Odyssey Marine Exploration secretly raised the coins and flew them to Florida under the code name Black Swan. Spain sued in the US courts and won; the treasure went to Spain in 2012. *Sunk 5 October 1804; salvaged 2007; returned to Spain 2012.*

What to look for: A debris field of coins in clumps on a deep seabed, with no hull. The case is in the dataset for its outcome: a warship stays the property of its flag state however deep it lies.

- **Off Cape Santa Maria, Portugal (wreck site)** (found here, confirmed; 36.90°N 7.89°W, give or take 60 km). A British squadron opened fire on four Spanish frigates here in 1804 and the Mercedes exploded. Odyssey found the scattered wreck and took about half a million coins to Florida in 2007, refusing to say where the site was. Sources: [BBC News, "True-life treasure hunt that turned into a comic book" (J. Badcock, 2018)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-europe-46439189); [BBC News, "Record wreck 'found off Cornwall'" (2007)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/england/cornwall/6671975.stm).
- **National Museum of Underwater Archaeology, Cartagena (where the coins are)** (found here, confirmed; 37.60°N 0.98°W, give or take 1 km). After Spain won its case in the US courts the coins were flown home. They are now in the national underwater archaeology museum in Cartagena, on the south-east coast of Spain. Sources: [BBC News, "True-life treasure hunt that turned into a comic book" (J. Badcock, 2018)](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-europe-46439189); [Wikipedia, "Black Swan Project"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Swan_Project).

#### Queen Anne's Revenge (found)

Queen Anne's Revenge, the flagship of the pirate Blackbeard, had been the French slave ship La Concorde until he took her in 1717. In May 1718 he ran her aground at what is now Beaufort Inlet in North Carolina. A private firm found the wreck in 1996 and the state has been excavating it since. *Run aground May 1718; found 1996.*

What to look for: Cannon, anchors and ballast stones in a pile on a sandy sea floor in 8.5 m of water, a mile offshore. Dates on a cannon and a bronze bell, and a French medical syringe, helped to confirm the ship.

- **Beaufort Inlet, North Carolina** (found here, confirmed; 34.69°N 76.67°W, give or take 3 km). Before the pirates took her the ship was La Concorde and carried 1,265 African men, women and children into slavery. The wreck site off Beaufort was discovered in 1996 and is the second oldest shipwreck found in North Carolina. Sources: [North Carolina Department of Natural and Cultural Resources, Queen Anne's Revenge Project, "History"](https://www.qaronline.org/history); [M. Wilde-Ramsing and L. Carnes-McNaughton, "Blackbeard's Queen Anne's Revenge and Its French Connection", in Pieces of Eight (University Press of Florida, 2016)](https://doi.org/10.5744/florida/9780813061580.003.0002).

#### RMS Titanic (found)

The liner Titanic struck an iceberg and sank on 15 April 1912 on her first voyage, and more than 1,500 people died. The wreck lay unfound for 73 years. A joint American and French expedition led by Robert Ballard and Jean-Louis Michel found it on 1 September 1985, 3,800 m down. *Sank 15 April 1912; found 1 September 1985.*

What to look for: A ship that breaks up on the way down strews a field of wreckage far wider than the hull, which is easier to cross with a towed camera or sonar than the hull itself. The wreck lies in two main pieces, 3,800 m down.

- **Wreck of the Titanic** (found here, confirmed; 41.73°N 49.95°W, give or take 2 km). The wreck was discovered on 1 September 1985 by an expedition led by Robert Ballard of the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and Jean-Louis Michel of the French institute IFREMER. It lies in international waters off Newfoundland at a depth of 12,500 feet (3,800 m). Sources: [NOAA, "R.M.S. Titanic: History and Significance"](https://www.noaa.gov/gc-international-section/rms-titanic-history-and-significance); [J.-L. Michel and R. Ballard, "The RMS Titanic 1985 discovery expedition", Proceedings of OCEANS'94 (IEEE)](https://doi.org/10.1109/oceans.1994.364185).

#### Santa María (not found yet)

The Santa María, the largest of Columbus's three ships, ran on to a reef off the north coast of Hispaniola on 25 December 1492, and her timbers were stripped to build the fort of La Navidad. What was left has never been found. In 2014 the explorer Barry Clifford said a wreck off Cap-Haïtien was the ship; a UNESCO mission examined it and said it was not. *Wrecked 25 December 1492 CE.*

What to look for: Little would be left: ballast stones, a few iron fittings and no fastenings of copper, which ships did not use until centuries later. Columbus's own log gives distances from the wreck to the shore that any candidate must fit.

- **Reefs off Cap-Haïtien** (search area, leading view; 19.75°N 72.20°W, give or take 20 km). Columbus's journal puts the wreck on a bank off this coast, close to the village of the chief Guacanagarí. Barry Clifford announced in May 2014 that a ballast pile he had found here was the Santa María. Against: UNESCO's experts reported that the wreck Clifford identified is of a later date: its fastenings show a way of building ships that belongs to the late 17th or 18th century, and it lies further from the shore than the accounts allow. Sources: [The Guardian, "Haiti shipwreck is not Columbus's Santa Maria, says Unesco" (2014)](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2014/oct/06/haiti-shipwreck-not-christopher-columbus-santa-maria); [Wikipedia, "Santa María (ship)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_(ship)).

#### SS Waratah (not found yet)

The British liner Waratah vanished in a storm off the east coast of South Africa in July 1909, between Durban and Cape Town, with all 211 people on board. No wreckage was ever identified. The wreck hunter Emlyn Brown searched for 22 years; a wreck reported as hers in 1999 proved to be a cargo ship sunk by a U-boat, and he gave up in 2004. *Disappeared July 1909 CE.*

What to look for: A large steel hull on the seabed off the Wild Coast, where the strong Agulhas current makes survey slow. Sonar finds wrecks there; the hard part, as the 1999 mistake showed, is telling one steamer from another.

- **Off the Mbhashe River mouth, Wild Coast** (search area, plausible; 32.25°S 28.90°E, give or take 150 km). The last confirmed sighting of the Waratah was about ten miles off the delta of the Mbhashe (Bashee) River. Emlyn Brown led expeditions to this coast from 1983. Against: Brown's last expedition ended in failure in 2004 and he said he had no more ideas; where the ship lies is unknown. Sources: [The Guardian, "'Titanic' hunt draws a blank" (2004)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2004/may/04/southafrica.andrewmeldrum); [Wikipedia, "SS Waratah"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Waratah).

#### Uluburun shipwreck (found)

The Uluburun ship sank off the south coast of Turkey about 1320 BCE with a rich cargo: copper and tin ingots, glass, ivory, gold jewellery and luxury goods from many lands. A Turkish sponge diver saw the "metal biscuits with ears" in 1982, and the Institute of Nautical Archaeology dug the wreck from 1984 to 1994. It is the richest picture we have of trade in the Late Bronze Age. *Sank about 1320 BCE; found 1982 CE; excavated 1984 to 1994.*

What to look for: Rows of four-handled copper ingots, stone anchors and big storage jars on a steep rocky slope. A sponge diver's report led to it; excavation took more than 22,000 dives to depths of over 150 feet (45 m).

- **Uluburun, near Kaş** (found here, confirmed; 36.13°N 29.69°E, give or take 1 km). A sponge diver reported the wreck in 1982 near Kaş. Excavation from 1984 to 1994 recovered a cargo of raw materials and finished luxury goods from across the eastern Mediterranean, dated to about 1320 BCE. Sources: [Institute of Nautical Archaeology, "Uluburun Late Bronze Age Shipwreck Excavation"](https://nauticalarch.org/projects/uluburun-late-bronze-age-shipwreck-excavation/); [C. Pulak, "The Uluburun shipwreck: an overview", International Journal of Nautical Archaeology 27 (1998)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.1998.tb00803.x).

#### USS Indianapolis (found)

The cruiser USS Indianapolis had just delivered parts of the first atomic bomb when a Japanese submarine torpedoed her in the Philippine Sea on 30 July 1945. Only 316 of her crew survived, many of the rest dying in the water before rescue came. The wreck was found on 19 August 2017 by a team funded by Paul Allen, 5.5 km down. *Sunk 30 July 1945; found 19 August 2017.*

What to look for: A steel warship on abyssal mud, found by an autonomous vehicle with sonar after fresh work in naval records narrowed the search box. The site is a war grave and its position is not published.

- **Philippine Sea, between Guam and Leyte (position not published)** (found here, confirmed; 12.00°N 134.80°E, give or take 150 km). The ship was found 18,000 feet (5.5 km) under the Pacific, 72 years after she sank somewhere between Guam and Leyte. The civilian search team was led by the Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen. Sources: [BBC News, "Lost WW2 warship USS Indianapolis found after 72 years" (2017)](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-us-canada-40991326); [Wikipedia, "USS Indianapolis (CA-35)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Indianapolis_(CA-35)).

#### Vasa (found)

The Swedish warship Vasa heeled over and sank in Stockholm harbour in 1628, minutes into her first voyage; she was built too tall and too narrow. Her guns were salvaged in the 17th century and the wreck was forgotten. Anders Franzén found her again in the 1950s, and in 1961 the hull was raised almost whole after 333 years. *Sank 10 August 1628; raised 1961.*

What to look for: The Baltic is too cold and too fresh for shipworm, so wooden wrecks there stand whole for centuries. Anders Franzén reasoned this in the early 1950s and went looking for old wrecks in Stockholm harbour.

- **Vasa Museum, Stockholm (she sank in the harbour here)** (found here, confirmed; 59.33°N 18.09°E, give or take 2 km). The ship was salvaged in 1961 with her hull largely intact after 333 years on the seabed of a busy shipping lane in Stockholm harbour, and is shown in a museum built for her. Sources: [Review of C. O. Cederlund and F. Hocker (eds), Vasa I: The Archaeology of a Swedish Warship of 1628 (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-9270.2007.163_1.x); [Wikipedia, "Vasa (ship)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_(ship)).

#### Whydah Gally (found)

The Whydah Gally was a slave ship that the pirate Samuel Bellamy captured on her first voyage and made his flagship. On 26 April 1717 a storm drove her on to a sandbar off Cape Cod and she broke up; only two of her crew lived. Barry Clifford found the wreck in 1984 under several metres of sand. *Wrecked 26 April 1717; found 1984.*

What to look for: Iron guns, coins and tools locked in hard lumps of concretion under 3 to 15 m of shifting sand in the surf zone. Magnetometers find the iron; X-rays show what is inside each lump.

- **Off Marconi Beach, Wellfleet, Cape Cod** (found here, confirmed; 41.90°N 70.00°W, give or take 10 km). The ship was driven aground at what is now Marconi Beach at Wellfleet, Massachusetts, on 26 April 1717. The wreck was found in 1984, buried under 3 to 15 m of sand. Sources: [Review of K. Kinkor and G. Binder, The Treasures of the Pirate Ship "Whydah", The Public Historian 15 (1993)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3378742); [Wikipedia, "Whydah Gally"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whydah_Gally).

### Tombs

#### Avalon and the grave of King Arthur (legend)

Avalon is the island where Arthur was taken to be healed of his wounds, in Geoffrey of Monmouth's 12th century history. In 1191 the monks of Glastonbury Abbey announced that they had dug up the bodies of Arthur and Guinevere, with a lead cross naming the place as Avalon. Historians regard the find as staged: the abbey had burned down in 1184 and needed pilgrims. *Avalon first named about 1136 CE; "grave" found 1191.*

What to look for: A pit in a monastic cemetery would prove that monks dug, not what they found, and the lead cross they described is lost. Re-study of the abbey's excavation archive gives no support to an Arthurian burial.

- **Glastonbury Abbey** (claimed find, rejected; 51.15°N 2.72°W, give or take 1 km). The monks said they found two skeletons in their cemetery in 1191, under a stone with a lead cross that named King Arthur and the Isle of Avalon. Glastonbury has been equated with Avalon ever since. Against: A full re-study of the abbey excavations of 1904 to 1979, published in 2015, overturned older readings of the site; the link with Arthur was promoted by the medieval monks. Sources: [R. Gilchrist and C. Green, Glastonbury Abbey: Archaeological Investigations 1904-79 (Society of Antiquaries, 2015)](https://doi.org/10.26530/oapen_619339); [Wikipedia, "Glastonbury Abbey"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Abbey).

#### Grave of Richard III (found)

Richard III was killed at Bosworth in 1485 and buried without ceremony in the church of the Grey Friars in Leicester. The friary was pulled down at the Reformation and the grave was lost; a story grew that his bones had been thrown in the river. In 2012 archaeologists from the University of Leicester dug in a council car park where old maps placed the church and found his skeleton on the first day. *Buried August 1485; found August 2012.*

What to look for: A lost church in a built-up town: map regression, which lays old maps over new ones, said where to trench. The skeleton had a curved spine and battle wounds, a radiocarbon date of the right decades and DNA that matches living relatives in the female line.

- **Greyfriars, Leicester** (found here, confirmed; 52.63°N 1.14°W, give or take 1 km). Excavation in 2012 found the choir of the friary church and in it a hastily dug grave with the skeleton of a man with severe scoliosis and fatal head wounds. Genetic and genealogical study published in 2014 concluded that the evidence that it is Richard III is overwhelming. Sources: [R. Buckley et al., "'The king in the car park': new light on the death and burial of Richard III in the Grey Friars church, Leicester, in 1485", Antiquity 87 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00049103); [T. King et al., "Identification of the remains of King Richard III", Nature Communications 5 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6631).

#### Sutton Hoo ship burial (found)

In the summer of 1939, on the eve of war, the largest barrow on a Suffolk estate was opened and found to hold the remains of a great ship with an untouched burial chamber at its centre. The helmet, gold buckle, shoulder clasps and silver in it make it one of the richest finds ever made in England. Coins date the burial to about 625, and the dead man is thought to be Rædwald, king of East Anglia. *Buried about 625 CE; excavated 1939.*

What to look for: The ship survived only as rows of iron rivets and a hard crust of sand where its planks had been. Barrow cemeteries like this show as low mounds in lidar; robbers' pits in their tops show too.

- **Sutton Hoo** (found here, confirmed; 52.09°N 1.34°E, give or take 1 km). The Great Ship Burial was found in 1939. Who lay in it cannot be proved; the leading theory is King Rædwald of East Anglia. Sources: [National Trust, "History of Sutton Hoo"](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/suffolk/sutton-hoo/history-of-sutton-hoo); [R. Bruce-Mitford, "The Sutton Hoo Ship-Burial", Nature 165 (1950)](https://doi.org/10.1038/165339a0).

#### Tomb of Alexander the Great (not found yet)

Alexander the Great died in Babylon in 323 BCE, and his body was brought to Egypt, where it lay in a mausoleum in Alexandria. The tomb drops out of the record in late antiquity and its site is lost under the modern city. Egypt's antiquities service has recognised more than 140 searches for it. *Died 323 BCE; tomb lost in late antiquity.*

What to look for: A royal mausoleum in the palace quarter of ancient Alexandria, now 5 to 10 m under streets and buildings and partly under the harbour. Finds come from rescue digs and boreholes when buildings are replaced.

- **Central Alexandria** (search area, leading view; 31.20°N 29.89°E, give or take 3 km). Ancient writers place the tomb in the royal quarter of Alexandria. Searches have centred on the old crossroads of the city and on sites such as the Nabi Daniel mosque. Against: More than 140 officially recognised searches have failed; earthquakes, the sea, riots and rebuilding have destroyed or buried the ancient royal quarter. Sources: [Archaeology (Archaeological Institute of America), "Hunting Alexander's Tomb" (R. Bianchi)](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/alexander/tomb.html); [A. Erskine, "Life after Death: Alexandria and the Body of Alexander", Greece and Rome 49 (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1093/gr/49.2.163).
- **Siwa Oasis (1995 claim)** (claimed find, rejected; 29.20°N 25.52°E, give or take 10 km). The Greek archaeologist Liana Souvaltzi dug from 1989 at a temple ruin at El Maraqi in the Siwa Oasis and announced in 1995 that she had found Alexander's tomb. Against: Greek officials doubted the claim at once and it has not been accepted; the American archaeological magazine that reviewed the searches lists it among "fanciful attempts". Sources: [Archaeology (Archaeological Institute of America), "Hunting Alexander's Tomb" (R. Bianchi)](https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/alexander/tomb.html); [Wikipedia, "Tomb of Alexander the Great"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Alexander_the_Great).

#### Tomb of Antony and Cleopatra (not found yet)

Cleopatra VII and Mark Antony died in Alexandria in 30 BCE, and ancient writers say Octavian let them be buried together in the mausoleum she had built there. It has never been found. Most scholars think it stood in the royal quarter, much of which is now under the harbour; since 2005 Kathleen Martínez has dug instead at the temple of Taposiris Magna, 45 km to the west. *Died 30 BCE.*

What to look for: In Alexandria: masonry of a Ptolemaic royal building on the sunken shore, mapped by divers and sonar. At Taposiris Magna: rock-cut shafts and tunnels under a temple, traced by ground-penetrating radar and excavation.

- **Royal quarter of Alexandria (now partly under the harbour)** (search area, leading view; 31.21°N 29.90°E, give or take 3 km). When Octavian's army entered Alexandria in 30 BCE, Cleopatra shut herself in her mausoleum in the city, and that is where the sources say she and Antony were buried. The palace district has since sunk into the eastern harbour. Against: Nothing identified as the mausoleum has been found among the sunken ruins. Sources: [National Geographic Magazine, "The Search for Cleopatra" (C. Brown, 2011)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/search-for-cleopatra); [Wikipedia, "Antirhodos"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antirhodos).
- **Taposiris Magna** (proposed, fringe; 30.95°N 29.52°E, give or take 2 km). Kathleen Martínez, a lawyer turned archaeologist from the Dominican Republic, proposed in 2004 that Cleopatra chose this temple of Osiris for her burial. Her digs have found coins with Cleopatra's name and image, a cemetery and, in 2022, a rock-cut tunnel about 1,300 m (4,300 feet) long under the temple. Against: Two decades of work have found no royal tomb, and the ancient accounts place the mausoleum in Alexandria. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "Archaeologists Discover 4,300-Foot-Long Tunnel Under Ancient Egyptian Temple" (2022)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/archaeologists-discover-4300-foot-long-tunnel-under-ancient-egyptian-temple-180981099/); [National Geographic Magazine, "The Search for Cleopatra" (C. Brown, 2011)](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/search-for-cleopatra).

#### Tomb of Genghis Khan (not found yet)

Genghis Khan died in 1227 and was buried in secret, by tradition near the sacred mountain Burkhan Khaldun in north-east Mongolia. No tomb has been found, and many Mongolians think it wrong to look for the grave at all. A crowdsourced search of satellite images flagged sites for checking on the ground without disturbing them. *Died August 1227 CE.*

What to look for: Nobody knows what the grave looks like, which is the problem the satellite project took on: thousands of volunteers tagged anything that looked man-made, and agreement between them picked out real features such as old burial mounds. Mongolian law and custom rule out digging.

- **Burkhan Khaldun and the Khentii mountains** (search area, plausible; 48.78°N 109.17°E, give or take 100 km). UNESCO records that the mountain is believed to be the place of Genghis Khan's birth and burial. In the National Geographic "Valley of the Khans" project more than 10,000 online volunteers examined 6,000 square km of high-resolution satellite images of the region and tagged possible sites. Against: No burial has been identified, and searching is taboo for many Mongolians, who hold that he did not want to be found. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Great Burkhan Khaldun Mountain and its surrounding sacred landscape"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1440); [A. Lin et al., "Crowdsourcing the Unknown: The Satellite Search for Genghis Khan", PLOS ONE 9 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0114046).

#### Tomb of Herod the Great (found)

The historian Josephus says that Herod, king of Judea, was buried in 4 BCE at Herodium, the fortress palace he built south of Jerusalem. Ehud Netzer dug there from 1972 and searched for the tomb for more than thirty years. In 2007 he found a mausoleum on the hillside with the pieces of a carved limestone sarcophagus that had been smashed in antiquity. *Died 4 BCE; tomb announced May 2007 CE.*

What to look for: The tomb was not at the foot of the hill, where Netzer looked for decades, but half way up the slope: a podium of fine ashlar, with fragments of carved sarcophagi scattered below it.

- **Herodium** (found here, leading view; 31.67°N 35.24°E, give or take 1 km). After a search of more than 30 years Ehud Netzer of the Hebrew University announced that he had located the tomb at Herodium. The team found a smashed limestone sarcophagus about 2.5 m long, decorated with rosettes. Against: No inscription names Herod; the identification rests on the place, the date and the quality of the mausoleum and sarcophagus, and it has been questioned. Sources: [BBC News, "King Herod's ancient tomb 'found'" (2007)](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/middle_east/6633979.stm); [Wikipedia, "Herodium"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodium).

#### Tomb of Nefertiti (not found yet)

No tomb or mummy of Queen Nefertiti, wife of Akhenaten, has been identified for certain. In 2015 the Egyptologist Nicholas Reeves argued from laser scans of the walls of Tutankhamun's tomb that two sealed doorways lead to further rooms, and that Nefertiti lies behind one. A radar survey later that year seemed to show voids behind the walls, but follow-up radar surveys in 2016 and 2018 found none. *Nefertiti died about 1330 BCE; claim made 2015; tested 2015 to 2018.*

What to look for: A void behind a plastered rock wall. Ground-penetrating radar should show a flat reflection where rock gives way to air, but cracks and natural cavities in limestone give false echoes, which is why three surveys were needed.

- **KV62, tomb of Tutankhamun (Valley of the Kings)** (claimed find, rejected; 25.74°N 32.60°E, give or take 1 km). Reeves read straight lines under the painted plaster of the north and west walls as blocked doorways, and proposed that the tomb was first made for Nefertiti and that her burial is still there, walled off. Against: Follow-up radar examinations in 2016 and 2018 found no such voids; Wikipedia reports a 2026 microgravity study that argues hidden chambers are still possible, which has not been checked for this dataset. Sources: [L. Sambuelli et al., "The third KV62 radar scan: Searching for hidden chambers adjacent to Tutankhamun's tomb", Journal of Cultural Heritage 39 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.culher.2019.04.001); [Wikipedia, "Tomb of Tutankhamun"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Tutankhamun).

#### Tomb of Philip II of Macedon (found)

Aigai was the first capital of the Macedonian kings, and its site was forgotten until it was identified with the village of Vergina. In 1977 and 1978 Manolis Andronikos dug into the Great Tumulus there and found royal tombs that had never been robbed, with gold chests, armour and wall paintings. One of them is identified as the tomb of Philip II, father of Alexander the Great. *Philip II was murdered in 336 BCE; tombs found 1977 to 1978 CE.*

What to look for: A great man-made mound raised over older burials, in a cemetery of more than 300 tumuli. Andronikos was convinced the mound hid royal tombs and dug until he reached them.

- **Great Tumulus, Vergina (Aigai)** (found here, leading view; 40.49°N 22.32°E, give or take 1 km). UNESCO records that one of the royal tombs in the Great Tumulus is identified as that of Philip II, and that the tombs were discovered in 1977 and 1978. A 2015 study of the cremated bones in Tomb II supports the view that the man is Philip. Against: Which tomb is Philip's is still argued: another reading gives him Tomb I and puts his son Philip III in Tomb II. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Archaeological Site of Aigai (modern name Vergina)"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/780); [T. Antikas and L. Wynn-Antikas, "New Finds from the Cremains in Tomb II at Aegae Point to Philip II and a Scythian Princess", International Journal of Osteoarchaeology 26 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/oa.2459).

#### Tomb of Qin Shi Huang (found)

Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of a united China, died in 210 BCE and was buried under a great mound near Xi'an; the historian Sima Qian wrote that mercury was used in the tomb to model the rivers and seas of the empire. The pits of the Terracotta Army that guards it were found in 1974. The tomb mound itself has been explored by remote methods and remains largely unexcavated. *Died 210 BCE; terracotta pits found 1974 CE.*

What to look for: A great earth mound with a walled precinct and many buried pits around it; the Terracotta Army lies about 1.5 km east of the mound. A number of non-invasive techniques have been used to explore the underground palace without digging.

- **Mausoleum mound at Lintong, Xi'an** (found here, confirmed; 34.38°N 109.25°E, give or take 2 km). The site was not discovered until 1974, and thousands of statues still wait to be unearthed. The emperor lies under a mound that remains largely unexcavated, so the account of what the tomb holds rests on Sima Qian. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/441); [Wikipedia, "Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Qin_Shi_Huang).

#### Tomb of Thutmose II (found)

Thutmose II, husband of Hatshepsut, was the last king of Egypt's 18th Dynasty whose tomb was unknown. A British and Egyptian team led by Piers Litherland found a tomb entrance in the Western Wadis near Luxor in October 2022. In February 2025 it was announced that alabaster fragments naming the king prove it is his: the first pharaoh's tomb found since Tutankhamun's in 1922. *Reigned about 1493 to 1479 BCE; entrance found 2022 CE; identified 2025.*

What to look for: A rock-cut stair and corridor at the foot of a cliff in a side valley, choked with flood debris. Floods had packed the tomb with debris; a blue ceiling with yellow stars, used only for kings, and fragments of alabaster jars with the names of Thutmose II and Hatshepsut identified it.

- **Wadi C-4, Western Wadis, Luxor** (found here, confirmed; 25.74°N 32.58°E, give or take 3 km). The team located the tomb in the Western Valleys of the Theban necropolis. Part of the ceiling was painted blue with yellow stars, which the field director Piers Litherland notes are found only in kings' tombs. Sources: [BBC News, "Last undiscovered tomb of Tutankhamun dynasty found" (2025)](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c5ym30v356po); [Wikipedia, "Tomb of Thutmose II"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Thutmose_II).

#### Varna Necropolis gold (found)

An excavator operator, Raycho Marinov, cut into a Copper Age cemetery at Varna, Bulgaria, in October 1972. Its graves held the oldest gold treasure in the world, most of it in a handful of very rich burials. Radiocarbon dates show the cemetery was in use from about 4560 to 4450 BCE. *About 4560 to 4450 BCE; found 1972 CE.*

What to look for: Flat graves with no mound, found by chance in building work. Nothing marks such a cemetery on the surface; rescue excavation ahead of construction is how they come to light.

- **Varna Necropolis** (found here, confirmed; 43.21°N 27.86°E, give or take 1 km). High-precision radiocarbon dating of the burials shows the cemetery in use from 4560 to 4450 BCE, with hints that the richest graves are among the earliest. Sources: [T. Higham, J. Chapman et al., "New perspectives on the Varna cemetery (Bulgaria): AMS dates and social implications", Antiquity 81 (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003598x00095636); [Wikipedia, "Varna Necropolis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_Necropolis).

### Relics

#### Ark of the Covenant (legend)

The Hebrew Bible describes the Ark as a gilded wooden chest that held the tablets of the law and stood in the inner room of Solomon's Temple in Jerusalem. It is not mentioned among the things the Babylonians carried off when they destroyed the Temple in 587 BCE, and nothing certain is heard of it again. Later traditions hide it in a cave or carry it to Ethiopia; no claim has ever been open to inspection. *Last placed in the Temple before 587 BCE.*

What to look for: A wooden chest would not survive 2,600 years in most soils. The claims that exist rest on tradition and on access being refused, so there is nothing for a survey to test.

- **Temple Mount, Jerusalem** (where it began, leading view; 31.78°N 35.24°E, give or take 1 km). The Ark stood in the Holy of Holies of Solomon's Temple on this hill. Jewish tradition is divided on its fate: that it was taken to Babylon, or that King Josiah hid it in the Temple or in chambers under it. Against: No excavation is possible on the Temple Mount, and no trace of the First Temple's furnishings has ever been found. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "Keepers of the Lost Ark?" (P. Raffaele, 2007)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/keepers-of-the-lost-ark-179998820/); [Wikipedia, "Ark of the Covenant"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark_of_the_Covenant).
- **Mount Nebo** (proposed, fringe; 31.77°N 35.73°E, give or take 10 km). The Second Book of Maccabees says the prophet Jeremiah took the Ark to the mountain that Moses climbed to see the promised land, hid it in a cave and sealed the door, and that the place will stay unknown until God gathers his people again. Against: The book was written centuries after the event and is a religious text, not a record; searches on Mount Nebo have found nothing. Sources: [2 Maccabees 2:4-8 (King James Version), Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/2_Maccabees); [Wikipedia, "Ark of the Covenant"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ark_of_the_Covenant).
- **Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion, Aksum** (claimed find, fringe; 14.13°N 38.72°E, give or take 1 km). Ethiopian Christians have held for centuries that the Ark rests in a chapel at Aksum, brought from Jerusalem by Menelik, son of Solomon and the Queen of Sheba, as the national epic the Kebra Nagast tells. A single guardian monk tends it and no one else may see it. Against: No outside scholar has been allowed to examine the object, and the story of Menelik is a medieval legend. Sources: [Smithsonian Magazine, "Keepers of the Lost Ark?" (P. Raffaele, 2007)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/keepers-of-the-lost-ark-179998820/); [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Aksum"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/15).

#### Holy Grail (legend)

The Grail comes from the Arthurian romances of the Middle Ages, where it became the cup of the Last Supper. Scholars treat it as a literary creation. Several real cups are now shown as the Grail, of which the agate cup in Valencia Cathedral has the longest paper trail. *Medieval romances; Valencia cup documented from 1399 CE.*

What to look for: A relic is tested by its material and its documents. A stone cup can be dated by its style of carving and its mounts by their craft, but nothing can tie any cup to one supper in Jerusalem.

- **Valencia Cathedral (Santo Cáliz)** (claimed find, fringe; 39.48°N 0.38°W, give or take 1 km). The upper part of the Valencia chalice is a cup cut from agate, which an archaeologist who studied it in 1960 judged to be Middle Eastern work of between the 2nd century BCE and the 1st century CE. Church tradition says it was sent from Rome to Huesca in Spain in the 3rd century for safety. Against: A cup of the right age and region is not proof of use at the Last Supper; the handles and base are medieval, and the early history is tradition. Sources: [BBC Travel, "Is this the home of the Holy Grail?" (2018)](https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20180528-is-this-the-home-of-the-holy-grail); [Wikipedia, "Holy Chalice"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Chalice).
- **Basilica of San Isidoro, León (2014 claim)** (claimed find, rejected; 42.60°N 5.57°W, give or take 1 km). Two Spanish historians claimed in a 2014 book, Kings of the Grail, that the jewelled Chalice of Doña Urraca in León is the true cup, on the strength of two Egyptian parchments they found in Cairo. Crowds came and curators had to take the cup off display. Against: The researchers themselves admit that Europe has some 200 supposed grails; the claim has not been accepted by other scholars. Sources: [The Guardian, "Crowds flock to Spanish church after holy grail claim" (2014)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2014/mar/31/crowds-flock-spanish-chuch-leon-holy-grail-claim).

#### Lost Peking Man fossils (not found yet)

Skulls and bones of Homo erectus were dug from a cave at Zhoukoudian near Beijing in the 1920s and 1930s. In 1941, with war coming, they were packed in two crates to be shipped to the United States, and they vanished on the way to the port of Qinhuangdao, near the US Marine base of Camp Holcomb. Only casts remain. *Excavated 1920s to 1937; lost December 1941.*

What to look for: Two wooden footlockers buried in haste in a camp that is now under a city. Old maps and photographs fix where the camp stood; ground radar could look under the tarmac before anyone digs.

- **Zhoukoudian (where the fossils were found)** (where it began, confirmed; 39.68°N 115.95°E, give or take 2 km). Scientific work at the cave site began in 1921 and uncovered the remains known as Peking Man. The site still yields finds and is a World Heritage Site. Sources: [UNESCO World Heritage Centre, "Peking Man Site at Zhoukoudian"](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/449); [Wikipedia, "Peking Man"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_Man).
- **Qinhuangdao (former US Marine camp)** (search area, plausible; 39.94°N 119.59°E, give or take 10 km). A former US Marine, Richard Bowen, reported that in 1947 he dug up a box of bones while digging a foxhole at Camp Holcomb in Qinhuangdao and reburied it. Lee Berger and Chinese colleagues investigated in 2012 and judged the report credible. Against: The place has not been excavated, and it is one lead among many that have come to nothing since 1941. Sources: [L. Berger, W. Liu and X. Wu, "Investigation of a credible report by a US Marine on the location of the missing Peking Man fossils", South African Journal of Science 108 (2012)](https://doi.org/10.4102/sajs.v108i3/4.1122); [Wikipedia, "Peking Man"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking_Man).

#### Noah's Ark (legend)

Genesis says that Noah's ark came to rest "upon the mountains of Ararat" after the flood. Geology finds no trace of a worldwide flood, and scholars read the story as one of a family of Mesopotamian flood myths. Expeditions have still searched Mount Ararat in Turkey since the 19th century, and a boat-shaped formation nearby has been promoted as the ark since 1959. *Written down in the first millennium BCE; searches since the 1800s.*

What to look for: Boat-shaped outlines are common where rock layers fold into a trough and erode. "Petrified timbers" and "iron brackets" can be tested under a microscope, and at the best known site they are natural minerals.

- **Mount Ararat** (proposed, fringe; 39.70°N 44.30°E, give or take 20 km). Tradition fixed the "mountains of Ararat" of Genesis, the old kingdom of Urartu, on this single volcano, and ark hunters have reported wood and shapes in its ice since the 1800s. Against: No expedition has produced wood of the right age or any structure, and Genesis names a region, not a peak. Sources: [Genesis 8:4 (King James Version), Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bible_(King_James)/Genesis); [Wikipedia, "Mount Ararat"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat).
- **Durupınar site** (claimed find, rejected; 39.44°N 44.23°E, give or take 1 km). A Turkish army captain, İlhan Durupınar, picked out the boat-shaped outline in an air force photograph in October 1959. Ron Wyatt and David Fasold later promoted it as the remains of the ark. Against: Fasold himself later co-wrote a geological study: the outline is a natural rock structure, and the "iron brackets" and "walls" are weathered volcanic minerals and rock. Sources: [L. Collins and D. Fasold, "Bogus 'Noah's Ark' from Turkey Exposed as a Common Geologic Structure", Journal of Geoscience Education 44 (1996)](https://doi.org/10.5408/1089-9995-44.4.439); [Wikipedia, "Durupınar site"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durup%C4%B1nar_site).

#### Vinland Map (hoax)

In 1965 Yale University announced a 15th century world map that showed "Vinland", part of the North American coast, drawn before Columbus sailed. In 2021 Yale's own analysis showed that the ink throughout the map contains a titanium compound first made in the 1920s. The map is a modern forgery. *Announced 1965 CE; shown to be a forgery 2021.*

What to look for: Chemistry of ink and parchment. X-ray fluorescence scans of the whole sheet show which elements are in every line, and a pigment invented in the 20th century cannot be in a 15th century drawing.

- **Beinecke Library, Yale University** (claimed find, hoax; 41.31°N 72.93°W, give or take 1 km). Yale caused a sensation in 1965 when it announced the map and published a scholarly book on it. The library still holds it. Against: Analysis found a titanium compound used in inks first made in the 1920s throughout the lines and text; the library's curator of early books says there is "no reasonable doubt" that the map is a fake. Sources: [Yale News, "Analysis unlocks secret of the Vinland Map: it's a fake" (2021)](https://news.yale.edu/2021/09/01/analysis-unlocks-secret-vinland-map-its-fake).

### Terrain

- **AWS Terrain Tiles (Mapzen terrarium)** (on the globe): Global land and seafloor heights as PNG tiles; the base terrain for the globe and enough for a browser-made hillshade anywhere on Earth. Covers: Global, land and ocean; resolution depends on the source used in each area. Licence: Mixed open sources; attribution list required.
- **Copernicus DEM GLO-30 and GLO-90 (COGs on AWS)**: The best open global surface model at 30 m, one cloud-optimised GeoTIFF per degree; agents use it for terrain analysis outside lidar countries. Covers: Global land.
- **Microsoft Planetary Computer tile API (hillshade from any DEM, on request)**: Renders Web Mercator tiles on demand from open archives, including a hillshade or terrarium encoding of Copernicus DEM 30 m anywhere on Earth. Covers: Global (depends on the collection).
- **OpenTopography global DEM API (free key)**: Cuts a GeoTIFF of a global DEM (the tested request asked for demtype=COP30, Copernicus 30 m) for a bounding box in one call. Covers: Global.
- **ArcticDEM and REMA (Polar Geospatial Center STAC)**: High-resolution stereo-satellite elevation models of the Arctic and Antarctica, for high-latitude sites far from any lidar survey. Covers: ArcticDEM: land north of about 50 N. REMA: Antarctica (not in this box). Licence: CC-BY-4.0.
- **SRTM-style 1-degree HGT files (AWS Terrain Tiles 'skadi')**: Plain 1-degree elevation files for scripts that want SRTM-like data without a login. Covers: Global (same sources as AWS Terrain Tiles). Licence: Mixed open sources; attribution list required.
- **NASA GIBS ASTER GDEM shaded relief (global)** (on the globe): Ready-made global shaded relief to z12; shows tells, craters and large earthworks anywhere without computing a hillshade. Covers: Global land. Licence: CC0 unless marked otherwise (NASA data use guidance).

### Sea floor

- **EMODnet Bathymetry: mean depth, multi-colour** (on the globe): Shaded colour bathymetry of European seas; shows drowned river valleys and banks such as Doggerland. Covers: European seas (approximate box). Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- **EMODnet Bathymetry WMS: source references, high-resolution areas, quality** (on the globe): Shows which survey each patch of European seafloor comes from and where high-resolution grids exist, so you can tell real seabed features from interpolation. Covers: European seas (approximate box). Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- **GMRT: Global Multi-Resolution Topography (WMS)** (on the globe): Global seafloor relief that switches to ship multibeam detail wherever a research cruise has mapped; good for seamounts, canyons and ridge crests. Covers: Global; high detail only along multibeam swaths. Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- **GMRT PointServer and GridServer (depth at a point, grid cut-outs)**: Returns the elevation at one coordinate, or a GeoTIFF of the GMRT grid for a box, so an agent can measure a seafloor feature it has spotted. Covers: Global. Licence: CC BY 4.0.
- **GEBCO global bathymetry grid (WMS, broken today)**: The standard global ocean depth grid as shaded relief; the default seafloor picture outside Europe and multibeam tracks. Covers: Global.
- **NOAA NCEI DEM global mosaic, colour hillshade**: Seamless land-and-sea colour hillshade from NOAA's coastal DEMs; around US coasts it resolves channels, shoals and drowned landforms to a few metres. Covers: Global at coarse scale; fine coastal DEMs mainly for the United States.
- **NOAA NCEI multibeam bathymetry mosaic, colour hillshade**: Only real ship multibeam soundings, hillshaded: where it has colour the seabed was actually surveyed. Covers: Global, only along multibeam survey swaths (transparent elsewhere at low zoom).
- **NOAA NCEI BAG hydrographic surveys, colour hillshade**: High-resolution NOAA harbour and coastal surveys (BAG grids) hillshaded; fine enough to show individual wrecks and dredged channels. Covers: US coastal waters where NOAA surveys have BAG grids (box from the service); transparent elsewhere.
- **NOAA NCEI marine survey coverage (tracklines, multibeam, hydrographic surveys)**: Lines and outlines of every archived marine survey: bathymetry, gravity, magnetics, side-scan sonar and sub-bottom profiles. Tells you what data exists over a spot before you claim it is unexplored. Covers: Global.
- **SRTM15+ global bathymetry and topography grid (Scripps)**: The 15 arc-second global grid behind much of the open seafloor picture: ship soundings where they exist, gravity-predicted depth elsewhere. Covers: Global.
- **Sea floor: measured or predicted (GEBCO 2024 TID)** (on the globe): Picture of what kind of source each GEBCO cell comes from: green multibeam, yellow combination of direct methods, grey predicted from satellite gravity, dark grey computer interpolation. Tested at Spartel Bank: five colours, nearly all green or yellow. Covers: Whole ocean. Licence: Underlying data public domain (GEBCO terms); service carries no licence text.
- **EMODnet: where high-resolution sea-floor surveys exist** (on the globe): Outlines of every contributed high-resolution grid here, with a GetFeatureInfo that returns the download link. Covers: European seas. Licence: CC BY 4.0 (from catalogue entry emodnet-bathymetry-multicolour).

### Radar

- **DLR TanDEM-X 90 m: radar amplitude mosaic and elevation (WMS)**: A global radar brightness mosaic and the matching 90 m elevation model; the amplitude image shows settlement mounds, field walls and dunes as texture. ### Gravity, magnetics and geology

- **EMAG2v3 Earth magnetic anomaly grid**: Global map of crustal magnetic anomalies at 2 arc-minutes; shows seafloor spreading stripes, buried volcanic bodies and impact structures, not objects the size of a ship. Covers: Global, with gaps where no survey exists.
- **USGS gravity anomaly maps of the United States** (on the globe): Isostatic and Bouguer gravity anomaly maps of the conterminous US; show buried basins, faults and dense intrusions at a regional scale. Covers: Conterminous United States. Licence: Public domain (US Government work).
- **Sandwell and Smith marine gravity and vertical gravity gradient (Scripps)**: Satellite-altimetry gravity over all oceans; the vertical gradient grid outlines seamounts, fracture zones and buried ridges that ships never surveyed. Covers: Global oceans.
- **USGS earthquakes (live GeoJSON feeds and catalog search)**: Live and historical earthquakes as GeoJSON; context for coasts that sank or rose and for sites destroyed by quakes. Covers: Global. Licence: Public domain (US Government work).
- **Plate boundaries (PB2002 model) as GeoJSON**: Lines of the world's plate boundaries; a quick check on whether a 'sunken continent' sits on a spreading ridge or a trench. Covers: Global.
- **Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program: Holocene volcanoes (WFS)**: Every volcano active in the last 12,000 years with type and last eruption year; context for buried towns and ash-covered landscapes. Covers: Global.
- **Macrostrat geologic map tiles and point lookup** (on the globe): A stitched geological map of the world from hundreds of sources, plus an API that names the rock unit and age under any point; tells limestone karst from basalt before you read shapes into terrain. Covers: Global at small scale; detailed maps for North America and parts of other continents. Licence: CC-BY 4.0.
- **GPlates Web Service (reconstructed coastlines and plates through time)**: Returns coastlines, plate polygons or your own points moved back to their position millions of years ago; useful for debunking, since plate motion is far too slow for legends of sunken continents. Covers: Global.

- **UKHO global wrecks and obstructions (ADMIRALTY Marine Data Portal)**: The UK Hydrographic Office's worldwide database of over 94,000 charted and uncharted wrecks and obstructions, the fullest open wreck list. Covers: Global. Licence: Open Government Licence.
- **NOAA Wrecks and Obstructions (AWOIS and ENC wrecks), service down today**: US coastal wrecks and obstructions from NOAA's AWOIS database and electronic charts, with position, depth and history notes. Covers: United States coastal waters.
- **GeoNames gazetteer (downloads)**: Millions of named places worldwide, including ruins, wrecks, mounds and abandoned settlements as feature codes; for finding the nearest named thing and old place names. Covers: Global. Licence: CC BY (Creative Commons Attribution).

### Catalogues and downloads

- **USGS 3DEP elevation and lidar coverage index** (on the globe): Shows where 1 m DEMs and lidar point clouds exist in the United States, so you know whether a blank-looking hillshade is real or just coarse data. Covers: United States and territories. Licence: Public domain (US Government work).
- **USGS WESM (lidar project metadata index)**: One row per 3DEP lidar project with dates, quality level and links, for agents that need to know when and how an area was flown. Covers: United States and territories. Licence: Public domain (US Government work).
- **USGS TNM Access API (product search)**: Finds downloadable USGS products (lidar LAZ tiles, 1 m DEM GeoTIFFs, historical topo maps) for a bounding box. Covers: United States and territories. Licence: Public domain (US Government work).
- **OpenTopography catalog API (who has lidar here?)**: Answers 'is there an open lidar point cloud or DEM covering this box?' across OpenTopography, USGS 3DEP and other federated archives. Covers: Global index; most datasets are in the United States.
- **NASA CMR search for GEDI and ICESat-2 lidar tracks**: Finds spaceborne lidar passes (GEDI, ICESat-2) over a box; sparse tracks, but they reach jungle with no airborne lidar. Covers: GEDI: low and middle latitudes only (flown on the space station). ICESat-2: global. Licence: CC0 unless marked otherwise (NASA data use guidance).
