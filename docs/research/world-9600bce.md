# world-9600bce: what the Earth and its people were like at Plato's date

Checked 2026-11-11. Data file: `world-9600bce.json` (same folder). Every number below has a source in that file, with a short quote.

Plato's "9,000 years before Solon" is about 9600 BCE, or about 11,600 years ago. The Greenland ice-core end of the Younger Dryas is 11,700 years before AD 2000 (about 9700 BCE, with a dating error of 99 years at 2 sigma). So Plato's date falls within about a century of the end of the last cold phase of the ice age.

## Summary

- **Sea level at 11.6 ka is not known to better than about 15 m.** The sources I could read give: about -45 m (Spratt and Lisiecki 2016 stack, interpolated, 1 sigma 12 m), about -43 m (Lambeck 1996 model, ages in radiocarbon years), -58 m (Barbados coral at 11.65 ka, a local value; Liu and Milliman also -58 m at 11.5 ka, second source). The globe's -60 m at 11.4 ka is at the low end of this spread. I could not read Lambeck and others 2014 Table S3 (the file is blocked to scripts), so I could not check the globe's number against the paper.
- **Meltwater Pulse 1B is disputed.** Barbados (Abdul and others 2016) has a rise of 14 +/- 2 m between 11.45 and 11.1 ka. Tahiti (Bard and others 2010) shows no step. Lambeck and others 2014 write "no evidence for a global MWP-1B event". After the Younger Dryas the rise was steady at about 15 m per thousand years, which is 1.5 m per century.
- **The end of the Younger Dryas was very fast.** In the NGRIP ice core Greenland warmed by more than 10 K, mostly in 50 to 60 years, and a moisture marker flipped in 1 to 3 years (Steffensen and others 2008).
- **The Younger Dryas impact hypothesis does not match Plato's date, and most of the literature I read rejects it.** The claimed impact is at the START of the cold phase, about 12.9 ka (about 10,950 BCE), 1,300 years before Plato's date. Proponents (Firestone 2007, Kennett 2015, Moore 2020, Sweatman 2024) and critics (Surovell 2009, Pinter 2011, van Hoesel 2014, Holliday 2023) are in the JSON with their claims. The tie to Atlantis is made by Graham Hancock's books, not by the impact papers.
- **People at 9600 BCE were hunter-gatherers and the first villagers, and they built in stone.** Goebekli Tepe (9600-8200 BCE per UNESCO) has circular compounds of carved T-shaped pillars built by hunter-gatherers. Jericho has a stone wall and tower by about 8000 BCE. Villagers crossed to Cyprus by sea by 11,100 years ago. These are real and impressive. They are not a bronze-working, ship-building empire.
- **Most of what Plato describes did not exist for thousands of years more**: copper smelting (5000 BCE), bronze (about 3000 BCE), writing (about 3350 BCE), cities and states (4th millennium BCE), plank ships (about 2500 BCE), horse and chariot (3500 and 1950 BCE), irrigation canals (about 6000 BCE).
- **36 sites** (coordinates from Wikipedia/Wikidata, dates with the dating method), **16 "firsts"**, **9 datasets** (5 with an open licence we can share; 4 whose terms I could not confirm), **9 local sea-level entries** (3 with numbers).

## Disagreements

1. **Global sea level at 11.6 ka.** Spratt and Lisiecki (2016): -45 m (interpolated between -35.85 m at 11 ka and -51.05 m at 12 ka; 1 sigma 12 m). Lambeck (1996): about -43 m at 10,000 radiocarbon years. Barbados (Abdul 2016): -58 m "at the end of the Younger Dryas". The Spratt stack is not pinned to zero today (its 0 ka row is +8.49 m), so read against today it could be about -53 m. We must say "about -50 m, give or take 10".
2. **MWP-1B.** Abdul and others (2016): real, 14 +/- 2 m, lagged 400 years behind the end of the Younger Dryas. Bard and others (2010): "None of these reconstructions shows an abrupt 15-m step around 11,300 cal yr B.P." and the Barbados step is an uplift artefact. Lambeck and others (2014): none globally. Other estimates cut it to 7.5 m or under 6 m (Wikipedia; originals not read).
3. **Black Sea.** Ryan and others (2003): lake at -105 m, rose to about -30 m in the Younger Dryas, then dropped, and reconnected catastrophically at 8.4 ka (radiocarbon). Aksu, Hiscott and others: slow, staged reconnection with outflow to the Marmara during the Younger Dryas. Soulet and others (2011): first marine inflow at about 9,000 cal BP. This is 2,500 years after Plato's date.
4. **When Melos obsidian first reached Franchthi.** Wikipedia: from 13,000 BCE. Lambeck (1996): "11,000 b.p." in radiocarbon years (about 10,900 BCE).
5. **Tell Qaramel's towers.** Wikipedia: 10,650-9,650 BCE, but "the very early dates ... appear too old as compared to dating of similar cultural phases at other sites".
6. **Calendars.** Dalton and others (2020) ice margins are in radiocarbon years (their 10 ka line is about 11.5 cal ka). NADI-1 and DATED-1 are in calendar years.
7. **The "Comprehensive refutation" paper is in Earth-Science Reviews 247 (2023), not the Journal of Geology** as the lane brief said. The 2024 reply was printed in Airbursts and Cratering Impacts, a journal that Wikipedia says the proponents' own group runs.

## What the globe should change

1. **Sea level at the Plato date: use about -50 m with a stated range of -40 to -60 m** (our reading across the sources, not a published uncertainty). Say what the sources are: Spratt -45, Lambeck 1996 -43, Barbados -58, Liu and Milliman -58. Keep the current -60 m only after someone opens Lambeck 2014 Table S3 and confirms it; it sits at the low end. At 15 m per thousand years, the date uncertainty is under 2 m and does not matter next to this.
2. **Show a band, not a line.** Draw the sea at -50 m with a lighter band from -40 to -60 m. Near Spartel Bank that band flips the bank from shoal to island.
3. **Treat the Black Sea as a lake at this date.** Its level is its own (-30 m to -105 m depending on the author), not the ocean's. At a global -50 m the Bosphorus sill is dry (Ryan puts the spillway near -30 m; Soulet and others say it was about 10 m shallower than commonly assumed). Do not show it as an inlet of the ocean.
4. **Local effects.** Near the old ice (North Sea, Baltic, Hudson Bay) the land was rising or sinking by tens of metres, so the plain global level is wrong there. Use the difference between the ICE-6G_C 11.5 ka surface and today's as a correction. Elsewhere (Gibraltar, Atlantic islands) the correction is expected to be small, but I found no published number.
5. **Pick datasets in calendar years.**

## What I could not verify

- Lambeck and others 2014 Table S3 (blocked), so the globe's -60 m is unchecked.
- Local sea level at 11.6 ka for Gibraltar, the Gulf of Cadiz, the Azores, the Bahamas, the Sunda Shelf and the North Sea: no number found. The Aegean (Lambeck 1996, -43 to -55 m) and the Black Sea (Ryan 2003) are the only local numbers besides Barbados.
- Yokoyama and others, Deschamps 2012, Stanford 2011, the original Liu and Milliman 2004: not fetched.
- The shapefile address for Dalton 2020 (in the journal supplement); NADI-1 file names; ICE-6G_C file sizes and licence.
- Excavation reports for most sites. Dates are from Wikipedia with UNESCO, or paper abstracts for 11 sites. See the JSON `not_verified` list for 16 items.
- Iberia and north-west Africa: no securely dated monumental or metal-working site for 10,500-8,500 BCE turned up in my searches. Taforalt (foragers, Morocco) ends about 12,600 cal BP. This is not proof there was nothing.
- Whether Near Eastern pottery appears before about 7000 BCE; the first tin bronze; open-sea crossings older than the Mediterranean cases.
